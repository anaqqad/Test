# Long-form video ideas (8–12 min, Blueprint 04 style)

Written 2026-11-20 for LingoDude, using `long-form-thelanguagemap.md`.

**How the list was built:**
- Every idea is a **language paradox told on a map**: two facts that seem to contradict each other.
- None repeats a topic TheLanguageMap has already made: Philippines, Indonesia, Brazil, India/English, German in America, Louisiana French, Irish, Basque, Paraguay, Madagascar.
- These are history-of-a-language questions, not "the languages of country X" surveys, which the owner rejected for the short format.

**Scores, each out of 5:**
- **Fit:** does it suit language fans, and can we use real recordings?
- **Curiosity:** how sharp is the paradox?
- **8+ min:** are there enough causes, evidence and payoffs to fill eight minutes or more?

## 1. Arab Rule in Malta Ended in 1091. So Why Is Maltese Still Arabic at Its Core?

**Thumbnail:**
- A Mediterranean map; Malta and Gozo raised in red 3D, with an inset zoom circle because Malta is tiny.
- Tag pill "ARAB RULE ENDED 1091".
- Red hook with a white outline: "So why does it sound Arabic?"
- Speech bubble "Il-lejl it-tajjeb" ("good night").
- LingoDude strip; optionally a small calm Wojak.

**The hook:**
- Every other Mediterranean island lost Arabic after the Arabs left. Malta, Catholic, in Europe, in the EU, kept an Arabic-descended language.
- Viewers hear real Maltese words next to their Arabic cousins.

**What the viewer learns:**
- Malta's language survived because the people who stayed kept it, even after the religion changed.
- Italian sat on top as the prestige language for centuries, and Maltese still took half its dictionary from Italian.
- A nationalist myth claimed Maltese came from Phoenician.
- It took a political fight under the British for Maltese to become official.

**Outline (10:00):**

| Time | Section | Material that earns the time |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:50 | Cold open | Sicily, Spain and Crete lost Arabic; then Malta. 1091 → 935 years. Hear "gżira" next to "jazīra"; a row of everyday words. Assumption "just leftovers" → no, the grammar too. Plant: "the people who saved it weren't Arabs". Title card. (Fully written in Blueprint 04 §8.) |
| 0:50–2:00 | AN ISLAND BETWEEN TWO WORLDS | Distances (about 90 km to Sicily, about 290 km to Tunisia). Phoenicians, Romans, Byzantines. The 870 Aghlabid conquest. A medieval source suggests the island was nearly empty and resettled around 1048 (debated: show it as a claim). |
| 2:00–3:20 | THE NORMANS ARRIVE | 1091 raid, 1127 full rule. The Arab administration stays. Governor Abbate's report of about 1241: 836 Muslim and 250 Christian families (Document Card). |
| 3:20–4:40 | THE RELIGION LEFT, THE LANGUAGE STAYED | Muslims expelled in the 1240s. The Christians who stayed already spoke the same Siculo-Arabic. **Mid-video payoff of the plant.** Contrast: Sicily was resettled by Latin speakers; Malta was too small and poor to attract many. |
| 4:40–5:50 | ITALIAN ON TOP | The Knights of St John (1530–1798). Italian for law, church and books; Maltese for home and street. The Kantilena (Pietru Caxaro, before 1485), the oldest known Maltese text (Document Card with a Commons scan if available). Loanword chips. |
| 5:50–6:50 | THE PHOENICIAN MYTH | 18th–19th-century scholars (Agius de Soldanis) claimed Maltese came from Phoenician; why the idea appealed; what linguists found instead (Siculo-Arabic). A "MYTH" stamp. |
| 6:50–8:00 | THE LANGUAGE QUESTION | British rule (1800/1814–1964). English against Italian in the late 1800s. The writers' association alphabet of the 1920s. Maltese made official with English in 1934 (verify). Recolour by era. |
| 8:00–9:00 | TODAY | About 500,000 speakers. An official EU language since 2004: the only Semitic one, and written in Latin letters. Dictionary blend (Brincat: Semitic core 32–41 %, a large Italian/Sicilian share, rising English). Hear-It: aħna, belt, ħobża. |
| 9:00–9:40 | IT'S STILL THERE (twist) | Catholic Maltese call God "Alla" in their prayers. Tunisian Arabic speakers can catch parts of Maltese. Hear-It: llejla. |
| 9:40–10:00 | Recap + end card | 1091 card returns; chip row; a personal last line; NEXT. |

**Key visuals:**
- A Mediterranean camera with a Malta inset; Recolour by Era (Arab gold → Norman red → Knights' cross → British blue).
- Document Cards (Abbate, the Kantilena); Stamp "MYTH"; Number Roll (1091, 836/250, 2004).

**Sources to gather:**
- Wettinger and Luttrell on medieval Malta.
- Brincat, *Malta. Una storia linguistica* and his Lexicographica article.
- Abbate's report.
- The Kantilena scan (Commons).
- The constitution and the 1934 Letters Patent.
- Lingua Libre Maltese (GħawdxiVeru, CC BY-SA 4.0, already used in `build_arabic.py`) plus more Maltese words.
- An Arabic word recording only if a real one is found; otherwise text.

## 2. Rome Ruled Romania for Only 165 Years. So Why Is Romanian Still a Latin Language?

**Thumbnail:**
- Southeast Europe; Romania raised in gold 3D inside a sea of Slavic-green and Hungarian-purple neighbours.
- Tag "ROMAN RULE: 165 YEARS".
- Red hook: "So why is it Latin?"
- Bubble "Bună ziua".

**The hook:**
- Rome ruled Britain for nearly 400 years and left almost no Latin. It ruled Dacia for about 165 and left a Romance language surrounded by Slavic ones.

**What the viewer learns:**
- What Roman Dacia was, and the withdrawal in 271–275.
- The two competing origin theories, presented fairly as a debate, not a verdict.
- The Balkan Romance cousins: Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian, Istro-Romanian.
- The heavy Slavic layer; the Cyrillic script until the 1860s; the 19th-century "re-Latinisation" through French loanwords.

**Outline (10:00):**

| Time | Section | Material |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Cold open | Britain about 400 years, no Latin → Dacia about 165, still Latin. Hear Romanian (Raluca clip in the repo). Plant: "the answer may not even be in Romania". Title. |
| 0:45–2:00 | TRAJAN'S GOLD | The conquest of 106, the gold mines, colonists "from the whole Roman world" (Eutropius). Trajan's Column scan (Commons). |
| 2:00–3:00 | ROME WALKS AWAY | Aurelian withdraws (271–275) and moves "Dacia" south of the Danube. Route Line south. |
| 3:00–4:30 | A THOUSAND QUIET YEARS | Goths, Huns, Slavs, Avars, Bulgars; almost no written sources. Two theories on a split-screen map: continuity north of the Danube against migration from the south. **Mid payoff of the plant**: Romance speakers also lived south of the Danube. |
| 4:30–5:40 | THE COUSINS | Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian, Istro-Romanian (a few hundred speakers in Croatia). Pin Swarm across the Balkans. Hear-It if recordings exist. |
| 5:40–6:40 | THE SLAVIC LAYER | Slavic loanwords (da, iubi…), Church Slavonic, Cyrillic. Neacșu's letter of 1521, the oldest dated Romanian text (Document Card). |
| 6:40–7:50 | BACK TO ROME | The Transylvanian School, Latin script made official in the 1860s, a flood of French loanwords. Chip arrows Slavic → French. |
| 7:50–8:50 | TODAY | About 24 million speakers (Romania and Moldova); the closest relatives tested on Swadesh words (reuse `similar_score.py`). |
| 8:50–9:35 | IT'S STILL THERE (twist) | Romanian keeps Latin features the others lost: the case system, the neuter, "cer" from caelum. |
| 9:35–10:00 | Recap + end card | |

**Key visuals:**
- A time-lapse of the Danube frontier; a split-screen theory map; Document Card (Neacșu); a Swadesh score chart (our own data).

**Sources to gather:**
- *Origin of the Romanians* literature from both schools (cite each).
- Eutropius; Trajan's Column photographs (Commons).
- Recordings: Romanian (in repo); Aromanian and Istro-Romanian on Commons or Wikitongues (to find).

**Care:** the origin question is politically sensitive between Romanian and Hungarian historians. Present both theories with sources and no verdict.

## 3. Why Does Hungary Speak a Language From Beyond the Urals?

**Thumbnail:**
- Europe; Hungary raised in purple 3D, surrounded by grey Indo-European neighbours.
- A dashed Route Line back to Western Siberia.
- Tag "RELATIVES: 3,000 KM AWAY".
- Red hook: "Why is it here?"
- Bubble "Jó napot!"

**The hook:**
- Hungary is surrounded by Germanic, Slavic and Romance languages. Its closest relatives, Khanty and Mansi, are spoken by small communities in Western Siberia, thousands of kilometres east.

**What the viewer learns:**
- The Uralic family; the migration and the conquest of the Carpathian Basin (about 895).
- Why a small group's language replaced or absorbed the locals' instead of disappearing.
- The Turkic, Slavic, German and Latin layers.
- Latin stayed the official language of Hungary until 1844.
- The language reform that coined thousands of words.

**Outline (10:00):**

| Time | Section | Material |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | Cold open | Pattern: Europe's neighbours share roots (mother/mutter/mat'), Hungarian "anya". Hear-It. The Siberian relatives. Plant: "a myth most Hungarians grew up with is wrong". Title. |
| 0:45–2:00 | THE FAMILY | The Uralic map: Finnish, Estonian, Sami, Khanty, Mansi; how far apart Finnish and Hungarian really are (Swadesh check). |
| 2:00–3:20 | THE LONG ROAD WEST | The steppe migration; Turkic contacts and loanwords (a Route Line through the steppe). |
| 3:20–4:40 | THE CONQUEST | About 895 into the Carpathian Basin; raids across Europe until 955 (Lechfeld). **Mid payoff**: the "Huns" myth, which linguistics doesn't support (Stamp "MYTH"). |
| 4:40–5:50 | WHY IT DIDN'T VANISH | An elite language that held on, unlike the Bulgars (Turkic rulers who switched to Slavic). Contrast map. |
| 5:50–7:00 | A KINGDOM THAT WROTE IN LATIN | Stephen I (1000). Latin official until 1844. The "Funeral Sermon" (about 1192–95), the oldest Hungarian text (Document Card). |
| 7:00–8:10 | INVENTING WORDS | The 18th–19th-century language reform (Kazinczy): about 10,000 coined words. Chip pairs (old loan → new Hungarian). |
| 8:10–9:10 | TODAY | About 13 million speakers. Hungarian-speaking communities in neighbouring countries since 1920 (factual, brief). Hear-It. |
| 9:10–9:40 | IT'S STILL THERE (twist) | Words Hungarian shares with Khanty/Mansi (for example "hal" for fish, "kéz" for hand): 3,000 years apart and still recognisable. |
| 9:40–10:00 | Recap + end card | |

**Key visuals:**
- A Uralic family map; a steppe Route Line; a contrast map with the Bulgars; Document Card (the Funeral Sermon scan, public domain).

**Sources to gather:**
- Uralic handbooks (for example Abondolo, *The Uralic Languages*).
- Glottolog for the map.
- The *Halotti beszéd* scan (Commons).
- Lingua Libre Hungarian (plentiful); Khanty/Mansi recordings (to find; otherwise text only).
