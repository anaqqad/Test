# LANGUAGE VIDEO BLUEPRINT: the "Map Tour" format for language learning

Adapted from `MASTER_VIDEO_BLUEPRINT.md`. Read that file first: it holds the frame table, the sound kit and the QC checklist, and this file only adds the language layer.

**Input to generate an episode:** `target language`, `CEFR level`, `topic`. Section 6 turns those three values into a full episode.

---

## 0. What carries over from the reference, and what can't

The reference is a **wordless, data-driven map tour**: zoom into a country, a face reveals with a hit, collectibles stack up, the characters stay on the map, and the newest item comes last.
- It has **no dialogue and no narration**.
- Its humour is micro-acting only (blinks, winks, eyes opening late).

So for language learning:

| Reference element | Language version |
|---|---|
| Country face reveal + impact hit | The country character reveals **the word**. The hit is replaced by the **real pronunciation**. |
| Trophies stacking (count) | **Word cards** stacking: one card per meaning, form or example |
| Result chips (year, flags, score) | **Word chips**: flag + word + IPA + English meaning, and a ✅ or ❌ for "means the same?" |
| Persistent characters on the map | Characters keep their word bubble on the map, so the map ends up as a **visual glossary** |
| 2026 result saved for last | **The twist saved for last**: the false friend, the rude meaning, the country that does it differently |
| Fireworks used once | One finale gag used once: a donkey appears, a character's eyes go wide, a sweat drop |

**Dialogue categories** (misunderstandings, conversations) need speech. To keep the reference's visual language, the dialogue is shown as **short speech bubbles from the map characters**, plus a voice:
- **Words and phrases that teach pronunciation must be real recordings by native speakers.** Use Wikimedia Commons or Lingua Libre, credited in the description. This is the channel rule.
- **Comedy narration and character voices** may use the labelled AI voice set-up already used for "Living in the USA with A2 English" (Piper voices). Label it in the description.
- If no real recording of a word can be found, **replace the word**. Never fake its pronunciation.

## 1. Series identity

**Series name:** "Word Trip" (working title).

**Cast:** recurring map characters, the language-specific characters in our own style.

| Character | Shape | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Brit** | outline of Great Britain, Union Jack fill | English "home" character, a bit smug |
| **Sol** | outline of Spain | loud, confident, always hungry |
| **Lune** | outline of France | dramatic, corrects everyone's pronunciation |
| **Kurt** | outline of Germany | literal-minded, takes every word at face value |
| **Nina** | outline of Italy | expressive, everything is about food or family |
| **Sami** | a small round traveller with a backpack (from our A2 video) | the learner who travels the map and makes the mistakes |

**Rules**
- **Faces:** Wojak-style faces from Codex/ChatGPT, as in `lingo/wojaks/`, kept the same in every episode.
- **The map:** flat, with **warm sand-coloured land** to stay distinct from the reference's green. Ocean in the series blue.
- **Persistence:** the cast always stays on the map. Sami travels between them along a dotted line.

### 4.4 Animation instructions
- Follow MASTER §8 frame table for blocks 2–6.
- **Reveal:** the character scale pops 0 → 110% → 100% over 7 frames. The pronunciation audio starts **exactly on the first fully revealed frame**.
- **Card stack:** each new block adds one butter card to a row at the bottom-left of the world map. These are the persistent "trophies".
- **Finale card flip:** a 3D Y-axis flip over 8 frames, swapping the butter for the donkey at 90°. Add a 4% camera push-in and the bray.
- **Micro-acting:** Brit's smug half-blink, Kurt's slow nod (2 frames down, 2 up), Lune's eyebrow raise, Nina's double blink, Sol's grin, Sami's sweat drop (falls 20 px over 10 frames).

### 4.5 Sound
- **Levels:** pronunciation clips at −16 LUFS short-term, and always at least 6 dB above everything else. Hit and whoosh as in MASTER §10.
- **No music bed under the words**, so the pronunciation stays clean.
- **The donkey bray** is the loudest moment (−8 dBFS peak).

### 4.6 Learning check
- **A1 vocabulary:** butter / Butter / beurre / burro / mantequilla.
- **One false friend:** *burro*.
- **No grammar.**
- **Reading:** every chip has a word, IPA and an emoji meaning, so it works muted on phones.

## 5. Episode rules for every language and level

- **Entertainment first:** every episode has one visual twist saved for the last block.
- **CEFR control:**
  - **A1:** single words or 2–3 word phrases; concrete nouns, numbers, greetings; no grammar labels.
  - **A2:** short phrases (≤6 words), everyday situations, one simple structure (e.g. *Je voudrais…*).
  - **B1:** false friends, idioms, 1 contrast per episode; sentences ≤10 words.
  - **B2–C2:** idioms, register (formal/informal), nuance; still ≤12 words per chip.
- **Teach through consequence:** a character uses the word, something happens, then the chip explains. Never use a teacher voice.
- **Recurring cast and the same map:** characters stay where they live. Sami travels.
- **Every target word gets a real native recording.** Drop or swap a word if none is found.

## 6. Auto-generation template

**Input:** `LANG`, `CEFR`, `TOPIC`.

1. **Pick the episode type by topic:**
   - **Word Trip** (default): one concept, 5 countries, a twist last. Good for vocabulary, false friends, onomatopoeia.
   - **Sami's Day:** one country, 4 mini-scenes of 25 s each. Good for situations (café, hotel, doctor).
   - **Challenge:** quiz rounds with a score stack. Good for recognition and numbers.
2. **Build the item list:**
   - 4–6 items with target words in `LANG` (+ comparison languages for Word Trip).
   - Check each word against a dictionary.
   - Choose **the twist item** (false friend, unexpected meaning, odd one out) and place it **last**.
3. **CEFR filter:** keep only words and phrases in the level band from §5. Replace anything harder.
4. **Audio check:**
   - Search Lingua Libre / Commons for each word (`lingo/fetch_audio.py` with `ll` or `seq`).
   - If a word is missing, replace it and repeat.
   - Record the credits.
5. **Assets:**
   - Reuse the cast from `lingo/wojaks/`.
   - Generate only new props with the §4.1 prompt pattern.
6. **Timeline:**
   - Cold open 3.0 s (the twist teaser), title 4.7 s, N × 11.67 s blocks, a 7 s "correct way" beat, a 5 s world outro.
   - Pronunciation on the reveal frame and at the hold midpoint.
7. **Text:**
   - Chips use `flag + word + IPA + meaning + ✅/❌`.
   - Title: `[WORD/SITUATION] in 5 Countries` or `[A] or [B]?`.
   - Thumbnail: the twist character plus the two meanings.
8. **QC:** MASTER §11, plus:
   - [ ] every word is spoken by a real native recording
   - [ ] the CEFR band is respected
   - [ ] the twist is last
