HOW WE SIMULATED THEM
Nobody alive has heard these languages, so every voice in this video is an AI simulation, not a recording. The method:
1. A real surviving text: every line is an actual ancient text, not invented sentences.
2. Reconstructed pronunciation: linguists rebuild how a language sounded from its descendants (Coptic for Egyptian, Icelandic for Old Norse), from how foreigners spelled it (Babylonian scribes wrote Egyptian names with vowels), from ancient grammarians (Romans described their own v and c), and from poetry, rhyme and spelling mistakes. We wrote each line in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) following these standard reconstructions.
3. AI voice reading the sounds directly: open-source Piper text-to-speech voices were fed the IPA sounds themselves, so they say exactly what the reconstruction says instead of reading the words in their own language. Each language got a voice from a modern language with similar sounds: Latvian for Old Prussian, Catalan for Old Occitan, French for Old French, Spanish for Mozarabic, Italian for Early Japanese, Russian for Old Church Slavonic, German for Old High German, Welsh for Old Irish, Mandarin for Middle Chinese, Arabic for Coptic.
Limits: the modern voice's accent comes through, sounds a voice was never trained on are replaced with the closest one, and some details are educated guesses even among experts: the exact vowels of Middle Chinese, the stress of Old Prussian, and how the Arabic-script Mozarabic verses were really pronounced. Think of it as a best guess built on real scholarship.

Texts: the Lord's Prayer from the 1561 Old Prussian Enchiridion, Bernart de Ventadorn's Can vei la lauzeta mover, the Song of Roland, a Mozarabic kharja, the Iroha poem, the Lord's Prayer in Old Church Slavonic, the Hildebrandslied, Pangur Bán, Li Bai's Quiet Night Thought (Middle Chinese after Baxter), and the Lord's Prayer in Bohairic Coptic.
