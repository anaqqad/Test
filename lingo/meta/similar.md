## Description
Some languages are so close that their speakers chat without a translator. We ranked 7 pairs on one fair basis: the same 41 core words for every pair (the Swadesh list: water, fire, sun, moon, eye, hand, one, two, three...), scored three ways. Vocabulary: how many of the 41 words are the same word. Sound: how close the spellings of those words are. Grammar: a 6-point checklist (noun cases, genders, the definite article, verb endings, word order, script). The similarity score is the average of the three.

In the video you hear 10 of the core words in both languages, every one a real recording by a native speaker (Lingua Libre, via Wikimedia Commons).

HOW THE SCORE WORKS
Vocabulary = share of the 41 core words that come from the same root in both languages. Sound = average edit distance between the two spellings of each word (Hindi and Urdu romanised), so 100% means written identically. Grammar = how many of 6 features match: number of noun cases, number of genders, where the definite article goes, whether verbs change by person, basic word order, writing system. It is a fair, repeatable test, not a measure of full mutual intelligibility. Czech-Slovak and Danish-Norwegian are missing because there are no recordings of the core words in Czech and Danish on Lingua Libre yet, and we only use real recordings.
