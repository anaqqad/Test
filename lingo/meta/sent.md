Languages That Are Basically the Same be like

## Description
Some languages are so close that their speakers chat without a translator. We gave 6 language pairs one and the same sentence (article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, official translations) and compared them three ways. Words: how many words have a twin in the other sentence. Sound: how close the spellings of those twins are. Grammar: a 6-point checklist (noun cases, genders, the definite article, verb endings, word order, script). The similarity score is the average of the three.

The voices are AI voices (Piper text-to-speech, one voice per language), labelled on screen. No real recording of this sentence exists in both languages of a pair under a free licence, so we used AI rather than fake it with a non-native reader.

HOW THE SCORE WORKS
Words = share of all words in the two sentences that have a cognate (same root) in the other sentence; the words without one are underlined in red. Sound = for every word, the edit distance to its closest word in the other sentence, averaged (lower-cased, accents folded), so 100% means written identically. Grammar = how many of 6 features match: number of noun cases, number of genders, where "the" goes, whether verbs change by person, basic word order, writing system. A fair, repeatable test on one sentence, not a measure of full mutual intelligibility.

Sentence texts: the official translations of article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (United Nations).
