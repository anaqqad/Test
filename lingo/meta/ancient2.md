HOW WE SIMULATED THEM
Nobody alive has heard these languages, so every voice in this video is an AI simulation, not a recording. The method:
1. A real surviving text: every line is an actual ancient text, not invented sentences.
2. Reconstructed pronunciation: linguists rebuild how a language sounded from its descendants (Coptic for Egyptian, Icelandic for Old Norse), from how foreigners spelled it (Babylonian scribes wrote Egyptian names with vowels), from ancient grammarians (Romans described their own v and c), and from poetry, rhyme and spelling mistakes. We wrote each line in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) following these standard reconstructions.
3. AI voice reading the sounds directly: open-source Piper text-to-speech voices were fed the IPA sounds themselves, so they say exactly what the reconstruction says instead of reading the words in their own language. Each language got a voice from a modern language with similar sounds: Mexican Spanish for Maya, Russian for Tocharian, Swahili for Meroitic, Welsh for Gaulish, Persian for Old Persian, Hebrew for Aramaic, Romanian for Etruscan, Mandarin for Old Chinese, Arabic for Phoenician, Greek for Mycenaean Greek.
Limits: the modern voice's accent comes through, sounds a voice was never trained on are replaced with the closest one, and some details are educated guesses even among experts: the vowels of Phoenician and Old Chinese, and the sounds of Etruscan and Meroitic, two languages we can read aloud but still barely understand. Think of it as a best guess built on real scholarship.

Texts: the royal title of Pakal of Palenque, Tocharian B kinship words, the Meroitic words for king and queen, the Gaulish dedication from Vaison-la-Romaine, Darius's Behistun inscription, Daniel 5:25, the numbers on Etruscan dice, the first poem of the Shijing (Old Chinese after Baxter and Sagart), the Ahiram sarcophagus, and Linear B words from Pylos.
