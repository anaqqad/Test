## Title
Ranking the World's Most Polite Languages

## Description
Which language has the most ways to be polite? We built a database of every polite, honorific, humble and royal word we could verify in open dictionaries, for about 100 languages, removed duplicates and set phrases, and ranked the top 10. From Japanese humble verbs to Javanese words you only use with your elders, every polite word you hear is a real recording by a native speaker.

How we ranked: one rule set for every language. A word counts if a dictionary says it is polite, honorific, humble or royal, or if its definition says it is used to say please, thank you or sorry, or as a respectful title or a polite "you". Sources: the English Wiktionary (about 100 languages), 16 other Wiktionary editions (Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Malay, Italian, Portuguese, Turkish, Polish, Spanish, Dutch, Russian, German, French), the JMdict Japanese dictionary, the CC-CEDICT Chinese dictionary, and S. Poedjosoedarmo's list of Javanese polite and honorific words (Cornell University), counted in October 2026. Each word counts once (different spellings are merged); phrases, idioms, endings, merely "formal", archaic and slang words are left out. Chinese means Standard Mandarin only.

Keep in mind: this counts words we could check, not politeness itself. Korean politeness lives mostly in verb endings, so a word count underrates it, and the main Thai, Korean, Tibetan and Khmer reference dictionaries are not openly available. Tibetan (52 words) would rank #8, but we found no free recording of a single Tibetan polite word, so it was skipped and Polish takes #10.

Which language is the most polite? Tell us in the comments 👇
