## Post text

In 1942, one of the most beautiful voices in America signed a seven-year contract with MGM. It was almost unheard of for a Black performer at a major Hollywood studio. And the contract said one thing clearly: she would not be cast as a maid.

So the studio found another way to handle her.

Lena Horne was born in Brooklyn in 1917. Her father left when she was three, and she was raised mostly by her grandparents, in a middle-class family active in the NAACP.

At sixteen she was dancing in the chorus at Harlem's Cotton Club. The performers were Black. The audience was white only.

She sang with Noble Sissle's orchestra, toured with Charlie Barnet's white band, and in 1941 sang at Cafe Society, New York's first integrated nightclub, where she worked with Paul Robeson.

Then MGM signed her. The NAACP's leader, Walter White, saw her as a test case for how Hollywood would treat Black women.

But in film after film, she simply sang, alone, in a scene of her own, with no white star beside her. That was on purpose. Those scenes could be lifted out, so theaters in the South that would not show Black performers could cut her from the movie. In some cities, they did.

In 1943 she finally got real roles, in Cabin in the Sky and Stormy Weather. The song Stormy Weather became hers for the rest of her life.

During the war, she sang for the troops. At one Army camp, she saw German prisoners of war seated in the front rows, and the Black American soldiers behind them. She walked off the stage, past the prisoners, and sang to the Black soldiers at the back.

She fell in love with Lennie Hayton, MGM's white musical director. In California, their marriage was against the law. So in 1947 they married in Paris.

When MGM remade Show Boat, the role of Julie, a woman of mixed race, went to Ava Gardner, a white actress. In the early 1950s, Lena was blacklisted for her political ties.

So she went back to the nightclub stage, and became one of the biggest singers in America. In 1957 she starred on Broadway in Jamaica.

In 1963 she sang at a civil rights rally in Jackson, Mississippi, where Medgar Evers spoke, days before he was murdered. That August, she marched on Washington.

Then, in 1970 and 1971, she lost her son, her father and her husband.

In 1981, at sixty-three, she opened her own one-woman show on Broadway, Lena Horne: The Lady and Her Music. It ran for 333 performances and won her a special Tony and two Grammys.

She died on May 9, 2010. She was 92. In 2022, a Broadway theater was renamed the Lena Horne Theatre.

Hollywood filmed her so she could be cut out. She made sure she never was.

Had you heard her full story?
