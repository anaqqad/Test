## Post text

In 1930, a young man from Baltimore wanted to study law in his home state. The University of Maryland's law school would not take him, because he was Black.

Five years later, he walked into court against that same university, and won.

Thurgood Marshall was born in Baltimore on July 2, 1908. He graduated with honors from Lincoln University in 1930 and set his mind on becoming a lawyer.

Shut out in Maryland, he went to Howard University's law school in Washington, D.C. There he met the man who would shape his life: Charles Hamilton Houston, a teacher who was training Black lawyers to take on segregation in the courts.

Marshall graduated first in his class in 1933.

Two years later, he and Houston went after the school that had refused him. A young Black man named Donald Gaines Murray had applied to the University of Maryland law school and been turned away because of his race. Marshall and Houston sued. The court ordered the university to admit him, and the state's highest court upheld it.

Donald Murray became the first Black student at the University of Maryland School of Law.

It was only the beginning. Marshall joined the NAACP in 1936, and he went on to lead the NAACP Legal Defense Fund, taking case after case against segregation in voting, in housing, in transportation and in schools.

The biggest came in the early 1950s. Marshall argued that segregated public schools violated the Constitution.

On May 17, 1954, the Supreme Court ruled in Brown v. Board of Education. The vote was unanimous. Chief Justice Earl Warren wrote that separate educational facilities are inherently unequal.

Segregated schools had lost the protection of the law.

In 1961, President Kennedy named Marshall to a federal appeals court. In 1965, President Johnson made him Solicitor General, the lawyer who argues for the United States before the Supreme Court.

On August 30, 1967, the Senate confirmed him to the Supreme Court itself. Thurgood Marshall became the first Black justice in its history.

He served for 24 years, until 1991. He died on January 24, 1993, at the age of 84.

In 2019, Maryland renamed its state law library in his honor: the Thurgood Marshall State Law Library.

The state that once kept him out of its law school now keeps its law books under his name.
