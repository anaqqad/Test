## YouTube Short

Title:
The Silent Marx Brother's Secret Moscow Mission #shorts

Description:
Harpo Marx never said a word on stage or screen. But in late 1933, after America recognized the Soviet Union, he went to Moscow as a goodwill performer, and he later wrote that he carried the American ambassador's papers out of Russia taped to his leg. In 1941 he tried, in writing, to save a Jewish relative trapped in Italy. And at his last concert in 1964, the silent Marx Brother finally spoke.

## Facebook Reel caption

He was the Marx Brother who never said a word. And he later wrote that he smuggled secret papers out of Stalin's Russia, taped to his leg.

Harpo Marx was born Adolph Marx in New York in 1888, to Jewish immigrants. He left school as a little boy, taught himself the harp, and went silent on stage after a critic said his clowning was spoiled whenever he spoke. In late 1933 he went to Moscow as a goodwill performer, and by his own account carried Ambassador Bullitt's papers out of the country. In 1941 he wrote to the American envoy in Portugal to try to save a Jewish relative trapped in Italy. The answer was no. And at his last concert, in 1964, he walked up to the microphone and said: "Believe it or not, I'm gonna talk."
