## Post text

In 1915, American audiences watched a pale woman in a dark hat step up to a vase of roses, pull one out, and smile at the camera. The film was A Fool There Was. Her character had no name. The credits called her only "The Vampire."

Within months she was one of the most famous women in America. Her name was Theda Bara. At least, that was the name Fox gave her.

She was born Theodosia Goodman in Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1885. Her father, Bernard Goodman, was a Jewish tailor from Poland. Her mother, Pauline, had been born in Switzerland. The 1900 census lists the family in Cincinnati, with young Theodosia at school.

She graduated from Walnut Hills High School in 1903 and spent two years at the University of Cincinnati. Then she turned to the stage.

In 1908 she reached Broadway, in a play called The Devil. It was no great success. For years her career went nowhere.

Then, in 1914, the director Frank Powell cast her as the lead in a film for William Fox. She was 29. The film was based on a play inspired by Rudyard Kipling's poem "The Vampire." The film opens with its lines: "We called her the woman who did not care."

In A Fool There Was, she plays a woman who seduces a married Wall Street lawyer and destroys him: his family, his career, his name. One title card read, "Kiss me, my fool!"

The film made her a star. It also gave America a new word: the vamp.

But Fox wanted more than a star. It wanted a legend.

Its press agents told the world that she had been born in the Sahara, in the shadow of the Sphinx. They said her name, Theda Bara, was an anagram of "Arab Death." Fan magazines and publicity cards printed one exotic life story after another. A 1916 publicity card made her the daughter of an Italian sculptor and a French actress, born in Cairo.

None of it was true. She was a Jewish girl from Ohio. And in 1917, her whole family legally changed its name to Bara.

In five years she made almost forty films for Fox, nearly all of them as the woman who ruins men: The Serpent, The She-Devil, Salome. At her peak, Fox paid her 4,000 dollars a week.

In 1917 she played Cleopatra, one of her biggest hits. Today only fragments of that film survive.

But the vamp became a trap. She wanted serious roles. The public wanted the vampire. In 1919, tired of being typecast, she let her five-year Fox contract run out.

In 1920 she went back to Broadway in The Blue Flame. Crowds came to see her. The critics savaged her.

In 1921 she married the director Charles Brabin. They stayed together for 34 years, until her death.

She tried a comeback in 1925 with The Unchastened Woman, then made two short comedies for Hal Roach. In 1926 she left the screen for good.

In 1937, a fire at a Fox film vault destroyed most of the studio's silent films. Most of Theda Bara's films are lost forever. A Fool There Was is one of the few that survive.

Theda Bara died of cancer in Los Angeles on April 7, 1955. She was 69.

The studio sold her as Arab Death, born beside the Sphinx. She was Theodosia Goodman, a Jewish tailor's daughter from Cincinnati.
