## Facebook Reel

Caption:
She was one of Hollywood's biggest stars. She also patented a secret system to guide torpedoes, and the Navy shelved it.

Hedy Lamarr was born Hedwig Kiesler in Vienna, the only child of a Jewish family. At 18 she married Fritz Mandl, an arms manufacturer with ties to Mussolini and the Nazis. At his table she listened to talk of weapons. In 1937 she fled.

In Hollywood she became a star. In wartime, with the composer George Antheil, she patented a way to make a torpedo's radio signal hop between 88 frequencies so it could not be jammed (U.S. Patent 2,292,387, August 11, 1942). The Navy did not use it. She was told to sell war bonds.

Frequency hopping later became part of military communications, and, in newer forms, of Bluetooth and early Wi-Fi. She never earned a cent from it. The award came in 1997. Her reported reaction: "It's about time."

Had you heard that she was an inventor?
