Title:

Post text:
Her hair fell out. She built an empire.

Madam C. J. Walker was born Sarah Breedlove on December 23, 1867, on a cotton plantation in Delta, Louisiana. Her parents had been enslaved; she was the first child in the family born after slavery ended.

She was orphaned at seven, married at fourteen and widowed at twenty, with a small daughter to raise. In St. Louis she worked as a washerwoman for as little as $1.50 a day.

In the 1890s a scalp disease made most of her hair fall out. Looking for a cure, she tried the hair products of the time, worked as a sales agent for another Black businesswoman, Annie Malone, and then in 1905 moved to Denver and started selling her own hair grower, door to door. After marrying Charles Joseph Walker, she became "Madam C. J. Walker".

By 1910 she had settled in Indianapolis, with a factory, a laboratory and a beauty school that trained women to sell the "Walker System". Thousands of Black women became Walker agents. At the company's first national convention in 1917, one agent said she had earned $5 a week as a servant; now she made $250 a week.

Walker gave generously: to the YMCA, to Black schools, and in May 1919 a pledge of $5,000 to the NAACP's anti-lynching fund, which the NAACP's secretary called the largest gift it had ever received.

She built Villa Lewaro, a mansion on the Hudson at Irvington, New York, designed by Vertner Tandy, New York's first licensed Black architect. She died there on May 25, 1919, at 51. Her total assets were put at more than a million dollars, which made her one of the wealthiest Black women of her time.

The washerwoman had built an empire.

## Notes before posting

- The reel does NOT call her "the first self-made female millionaire" (Guinness). She denied being a millionaire in her lifetime (Indiana Historical Society), her estate was valued at $600,000-700,000, and other women may have come before her. It says "worth over a million dollars" (total assets, per her biographer and the NWHM) and "one of America's richest Black women".
- The "$5 a week to $250 a week" line is agent Margaret Thompson's own claim at the August 1917 convention (A'Lelia Bundles).
- The NAACP pledge was May 1919, shortly before her death; some sites give 1918.
- Not used: her savings on arriving in Denver ($1.25 or $1.50, sources vary); total agent numbers (several thousand / 23,000 / 40,000, sources vary); the 1908 Pittsburgh school.
