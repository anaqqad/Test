Private William Cathay had a secret.

William Cathay was a woman: Cathay Williams. Born enslaved in Missouri, she cooked and washed for the Union army in the Civil War.

In November 1866, women could not enlist. So she enlisted as a man, in the 38th U.S. Infantry, one of the new Black regiments later called the Buffalo Soldiers. "I wanted to make my own living," she said, "and not be dependent on relations or friends."

She marched hundreds of miles to forts in New Mexico. She was in the hospital five times, and no one found out. In 1868 an Army surgeon did. She was discharged; the paper still called her "he."

Years later, old and sick, she applied for a veteran's pension. Her claim was rejected. No one knows when she died, or where she is buried.

She is the only woman known to have served as a Buffalo Soldier.

Had you heard of Cathay Williams?
