She went to space, then onto Star Trek.

Dr. Mae Jemison grew up in Chicago watching Star Trek, and Lieutenant Uhura, played by Nichelle Nichols. She left for Stanford at 16, then earned her medical degree at Cornell in 1981.

She served as a Peace Corps doctor in Sierra Leone and Liberia. In 1987, NASA picked her as one of 15 new astronauts, from about 2,000 applicants.

On September 12, 1992, the space shuttle Endeavour lifted off, and Mae Jemison became the first African American woman in space. She opened her shifts with Uhura's own line: "Hailing frequencies open." She carried a photo of the aviator Bessie Coleman, and for eight days she ran experiments on bone cells, frog eggs and motion sickness.

In 1993 she left NASA. That same year she appeared on Star Trek: The Next Generation, in the episode "Second Chances," the first real astronaut to appear on Star Trek.

Star Trek showed her space. She went, and came back a real astronaut.
