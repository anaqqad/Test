## Facebook

Title:
She Escaped Slavery Disguised as a White Slave Owner

Post text:
In 1848, an enslaved woman escaped disguised as a white slave owner, and her husband played her slave.

Ellen Craft cut her hair, put on a top hat and green glasses, wrapped her face in a bandage and her right
arm in a sling, because she could not write. With William at her side as her "servant," she rode north by
train and steamboat. A friend of her enslaver sat right next to her, so she pretended to be deaf. In
Baltimore an officer demanded proof that William was hers, and still let them through. On Christmas Day
1848, they reached Philadelphia, free.

Would you have kept your nerve in that seat?

In 1848, Ellen Craft escaped slavery in Georgia disguised as a white slave owner, while her husband
William posed as her slave. They travelled a thousand miles by train and steamboat, past a friend of her
enslaver and a suspicious officer in Baltimore, and reached Philadelphia on Christmas Day.

## Notes before posting

- Start date differs between sources (18-24 December 1848); the reel says "December 1848".
- "A thousand miles" comes from the title of their book; it is an approximation.
- "Slave owner": sources describe the disguise as a white planter / slaveholder / master travelling with
  his slave. Accurate.
- No quotes are used.
- Images: 1860 frontispiece and title page (Internet Archive scan of the Boston Public Library copy,
  published 1860, public domain); portraits of Ellen and William Craft; 1898 engraving (Siebert);
  Baltimore bird's-eye view, 1869; Boston warning poster, 1851. All public domain.
