Post text:
Banned from a hall, she sang to 75,000.

Marian Anderson was born in Philadelphia in 1897, and by 1939 she was a celebrated singer, famous in Europe and America.

That year, Howard University tried to book her at Constitution Hall in Washington. The owners, the Daughters of the American Revolution, said no: the hall was for white performers only.

First Lady Eleanor Roosevelt resigned from the DAR in protest. And the government offered a bigger stage: the steps of the Lincoln Memorial.

On Easter Sunday, April 9, 1939, about 75,000 people came, Black and white together, and millions more listened on the radio. Her first song was "My Country, 'Tis of Thee."

In 1955 she became the first African American to sing with the Metropolitan Opera. In 1963 she sang at the March on Washington.

They closed a hall to her. She sang to the whole country instead.
