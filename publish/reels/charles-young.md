Post text:
The Army said he was too sick to serve. So he rode 497 miles.

Charles Young was born enslaved in Kentucky in 1864. In 1889 he became only the third Black graduate of West Point. He led Buffalo Soldiers, became the first Black superintendent of a national park, and in 1916 led a cavalry charge in Mexico without losing a single man.

When America entered the First World War, he was the highest-ranking Black officer in the Army. Some white officers refused to take orders from him. In 1917, the Army retired him for high blood pressure.

So on June 6, 1918, he got on his horse in Wilberforce, Ohio, and rode to Washington, D.C. 497 miles. The Army still said no.

He died in 1922 in Lagos, Nigeria. A century later, the Army promoted him to brigadier general.
