## Facebook

Post text:
He was the highest-paid Black entertainer in America. And every night, he had to paint his face black to go on stage.

Bert Williams was light-skinned. But in his day, Black performers had to wear blackface. So before every show, he blackened his face with burnt cork. He and his partner George Walker even billed themselves as "Two Real Coons", to set themselves apart from the white men in blackface.

In 1903 their show In Dahomey became the first all-Black musical at a major Broadway theater. In 1910 Florenz Ziegfeld made him the only Black star in his all-white Follies. When cast members demanded he be fired, Ziegfeld reportedly said: "I can replace every one of you, except him."

But at his hotel, he had to ride the freight elevator. In 1919, when Broadway's actors went on strike, nobody told him. He walked into an empty theater.

In 1922 he collapsed on stage in Detroit. The audience thought it was part of the act.

Had you heard his story?
