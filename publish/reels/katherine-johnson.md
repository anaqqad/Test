Title:
John Glenn Wouldn't Fly Without Her

Post text:
John Glenn would not fly without her.

Katherine Johnson was born in White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia, in 1918. She finished college at eighteen, graduating with top honors in mathematics and French from West Virginia State College.

In 1953, the Langley laboratory in Hampton, Virginia hired her as a "human computer", doing calculations by hand in a segregated unit of Black women. Within a few years she was working on the problem of space flight itself.

In 1961, she calculated the flight path of Freedom 7, the mission that made Alan Shepard the first American in space.

Then came 1962. John Glenn was about to become the first American to orbit the Earth. For the first time, the orbital numbers had been worked out by NASA's new electronic computers. Glenn did not fully trust the machines. He asked the engineers to "get the girl" to run the same calculations by hand. If she said the numbers were good, he said, he was ready to go.

She checked them. On February 20, 1962, Friendship 7 lifted off, and John Glenn came home.

Katherine Johnson went on to help calculate the path to the Moon for Apollo 11. She retired in 1986, after 33 years and 26 research reports.

In 2015, at 97, President Obama presented her with the Presidential Medal of Freedom. She died on February 24, 2020, at the age of 101.

The man who orbited the Earth trusted her more than the machine.

Did you know her story before the film Hidden Figures?

## Notes before posting

- Glenn's words are given as NASA reports them ("get the girl"; "If she says they're good, then I'm ready to go"). The film Hidden Figures turns the check into a launch-day emergency; in reality it took about two weeks before the flight.
- IEEE Spectrum gives 1939 for her graduation; NASA and the Computer History Museum give 1937, which is used.
- Not used: the 2019 Congressional Gold Medal and the Langley building named after her (2017), to keep to facts confirmed by two fetched sources; her Apollo 13 backup charts.
- Images: NASA photos (1983 portrait, Glenn boarding Friendship 7 and the launch on 20 Feb 1962, Langley 2014, Medal of Freedom 2015), all public domain; credits in `public/pd/reel-katherine-johnson/credits.json`.
