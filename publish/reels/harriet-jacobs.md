She hid in a tiny attic for seven years.

Harriet Jacobs was born enslaved in Edenton, North Carolina, in 1813. As a girl she was placed under the control of Dr. James Norcom, who harassed her for years.

In 1835 she ran. A notice went out for her capture. She did not get far, on purpose: she hid in a crawlspace at the top of her grandmother's house in Edenton. It was about nine feet long and seven feet wide, and only three feet high at its highest point. She could not stand up.

She bored small holes in the wall. Through them she got air and a little light, and she could see her children, who did not know she was there.

Summer heat, winter cold. She stayed almost seven years. In 1842 she finally escaped north by boat, to Philadelphia and then New York.

In 1861 she told her story in a book, Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl, under a false name, Linda Brent. For years many believed it was a novel written by its white editor. Historian Jean Fagan Yellin's research in the 1980s proved Harriet Jacobs wrote it herself.

Seven years in an attic, and she never gave up on freedom.
