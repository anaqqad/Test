Post text:
They made him a cook. Then Pearl Harbor.

Dorie Miller was a Texas farm boy who joined the Navy in 1939. Black sailors were kept to feeding and serving the officers.

On December 7, 1941, he was collecting laundry on the battleship USS West Virginia when the torpedoes hit. His battle station was destroyed, so he helped carry the wounded.

Then he took a .50-caliber machine gun he had never been trained to use, and he fired at the planes until the ammunition ran out. "I just pulled the trigger, and she worked fine," he said later.

The Navy praised him without giving his name. A Black newspaper, the Pittsburgh Courier, found it.

In May 1942, Admiral Nimitz pinned the Navy Cross on him. He was the first Black sailor to receive it. But his job did not change.

In 1943, a Japanese submarine sank his ship, the USS Liscome Bay. He was 24.

They gave him a tray. He picked up a gun.

Had you heard of Dorie Miller?
