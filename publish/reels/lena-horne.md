## Facebook

Post text:
Hollywood made her a star. Then it filmed her songs so theaters in the South could cut her out.

Lena Horne started at 16 in the chorus of Harlem's Cotton Club: Black performers, a whites-only audience. In 1942 MGM signed her to a seven-year contract, almost unheard of for a Black performer, and she refused to play maids.

So MGM gave her scenes of her own. She sang alone, apart from the white stars, so theaters in the South could cut her out of the film. Some did.

During the war, at one Army camp, German prisoners of war were seated in front of the Black soldiers. She walked off the stage, past the prisoners, and sang to the Black soldiers in the back.

She married Lennie Hayton in Paris, because their marriage was against the law in California. She was blacklisted in the 1950s, marched on Washington in 1963, and at 63 opened her own show on Broadway, which won her a special Tony and two Grammys.
