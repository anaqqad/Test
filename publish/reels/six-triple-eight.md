Seventeen million letters, and three months.

In 1945, in Birmingham, England, warehouses held about 17 million undelivered letters and packages for American soldiers. The Army said it would take six months. It sent 855 Black women of the Women's Army Corps: the 6888th Central Postal Directory Battalion, the Six Triple Eight.

They worked in cold, dark buildings, three shifts a day, seven days a week. Their motto: "No mail, low morale."

When a general threatened to send a white officer to show their commander, Major Charity Adams, how to run things, she said: "Over my dead body, sir."

They cleared the backlog in three months, half the time. Then they did it again in France.

In 1946 they came home. There was no parade. In April 2025, eighty years later, the battalion received the Congressional Gold Medal.
