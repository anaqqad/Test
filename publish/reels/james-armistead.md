Post text:
He spied on the British, and stayed enslaved.

In 1781, James, enslaved in Virginia, went to serve the Marquis de Lafayette as a spy, slipping into the British camp again and again. Lafayette learned that Cornwallis had moved his army to Yorktown, where he was trapped. On October 19, 1781, Cornwallis surrendered.

But Virginia freed enslaved men who had served as soldiers. James had been a spy. He stayed enslaved.

So Lafayette wrote for him: "His intelligences from the enemy's camp were industriously collected and most faithfully delivered." On January 9, 1787, Virginia set him free. He took the name Lafayette.

In 1824, the old general saw him in a crowd, called him by name, and embraced him.

He helped win the war, and then had to win his own freedom.
