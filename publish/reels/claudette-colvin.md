Nine months before Rosa Parks, she refused to move.

On March 2, 1955, Claudette Colvin was 15, riding the bus home from school in Montgomery, Alabama. The driver ordered her to give up her seat to a white woman. She stayed put and said it was her constitutional right to sit there.

Two police officers pulled her off the bus in handcuffs. She spent hours in jail before her minister paid her bail, and she was convicted in juvenile court.

Civil rights leaders considered building their fight around her case, then decided against it. She was a teenager, and soon pregnant. Nine months later, Rosa Parks was arrested, and the Montgomery bus boycott began.

But Claudette's story did not end there. In 1956 she was one of four plaintiffs in Browder v. Gayle, the federal lawsuit that struck down bus segregation in Montgomery. The Supreme Court upheld the ruling that November.

Her own arrest record stayed on the books for 66 years. In 2021, at 82, a judge finally ordered it expunged. Claudette Colvin died in January 2026, at 86.

History remembers Rosa Parks. Claudette Colvin went first.
