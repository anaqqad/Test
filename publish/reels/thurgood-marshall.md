Shut out of law school, he forced it open.

Thurgood Marshall was born in Baltimore in 1908. After college he wanted to study law at home, but the University of Maryland's law school did not admit Black students.

So he went to Howard University in Washington, and graduated first in his class in 1933. His teacher, Charles Hamilton Houston, trained lawyers to fight segregation in court.

Two years later, Marshall and Houston sued the school that had shut him out, for a young Black man named Donald Murray. They won, and Murray was admitted.

Then Marshall took on segregated schools across the country. In 1954, in Brown v. Board of Education, the Supreme Court ruled for him, 9 to 0.

In 1967 he became the first Black justice on the Supreme Court, and he served for 24 years. In 2019, Maryland renamed its state law library after him.

They kept him out. He opened the door for everyone.

Had you heard how his career began?
