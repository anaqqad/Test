Post text:
They said Black men could not fly.

In 1925, an Army War College study claimed Black men did not have what it takes to lead, or to fly.

In 1941, the Army started a test program at Tuskegee, Alabama. Thirteen men began the first class. Five finished. That spring, First Lady Eleanor Roosevelt went up in a plane with their chief instructor.

In 1943 they went to war. Their own commander reported they lacked discipline and aggressiveness. A War Department study found they had performed as well as the white squadrons.

They painted their tails red and escorted American bombers across Europe. In March 1945 they flew all the way to Berlin and shot down three German jets.

That same spring in Indiana, more than a hundred Black officers were arrested, in a fight over an officers club for white men only.

In 1948, President Truman ordered equal treatment in the armed forces. In 2007, the Airmen received the Congressional Gold Medal.

They said Black men could not fly. So they flew.
