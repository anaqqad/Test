Title:
The President Tried to Pull Cameras Off Her.

Post text:
The President of the United States tried to pull the cameras off her.

Fannie Lou Hamer was born in Mississippi on October 6, 1917, the youngest of twenty children of sharecroppers. She was picking cotton by the age of six. In 1961, during an operation, a white doctor sterilized her without her consent.

In 1962 she went to the courthouse to register to vote. When she got back, the plantation owner told her to withdraw her registration or leave. She left that same night. In 1963, in a jail in Winona, Mississippi, state patrolmen ordered two Black prisoners to beat her with a blackjack. The injuries stayed with her for the rest of her life.

In 1964 her Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party went to the Democratic National Convention in Atlantic City to challenge the state's all-white delegation. On August 22 she testified before the Credentials Committee, on live television, and told the country what had been done to her.

While she was speaking, President Lyndon Johnson called a sudden press conference at the White House to draw the cameras away. It didn't work: that evening, the networks broadcast her testimony anyway.

"Is this America," she asked, "the land of the free and the home of the brave, where we have to sleep with our telephones off the hooks because our lives be threatened daily, because we want to live as decent human beings, in America?"

The party offered her delegation two token seats. They refused. Four years later, at the 1968 convention, she was seated as a delegate.

She kept organizing, founded the Freedom Farm Cooperative to feed poor families in the Delta, and died on March 14, 1977. Her gravestone reads: "I am sick and tired of being sick and tired."

## Notes before posting

- Sources disagree on whether Johnson's press conference actually cut her off mid-testimony, so the reel says he "tried to pull the cameras away" and that the networks aired it that evening (AP; Bill of Rights Institute).
- Her eviction: in her testimony she says she left "that same night"; one introduction says the day after. The reel follows her own words.
- The "Credentials Committee" image shows the committee hearing on the same day (MFDP chair Aaron Henry speaking).
- The 1961 sterilization and the 1968 seating are in the post only (National Women's History Museum).
- Not used: the 2025 Presidential Medal of Freedom (one fetched source).
