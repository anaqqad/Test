## Post text

In 1862, an enslaved man stole a Confederate warship, and sailed his family to freedom.

Before dawn on 13 May 1862, Robert Smalls, wearing the captain's straw hat, steered the armed steamer
Planter out of Charleston harbor, past Fort Sumter, with sixteen people aboard. Under a white bedsheet,
he handed the ship to the Union blockade. He later bought the house where he had been enslaved and
served five terms in the US Congress.

Had you heard of Robert Smalls before today?

## Notes before posting

- "16 people": US House, US Navy (DANFS) and NPS give 16; Henry Louis Gates Jr. gives 17. We use 16.
- "Warship": the Planter was an armed Confederate transport and dispatch steamer (Harper's Weekly in 1862
  called it a "gun-boat"). The post text says "armed steamer" in the body.
- No quotes are used. The often-repeated "I've brought you some of the old United States guns" line has
  a single source, so it is left out.
