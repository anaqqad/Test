Shot twice. He never dropped the flag.

William Harvey Carney was born enslaved in Norfolk, Virginia, on February 29, 1840. He found freedom in New Bedford, Massachusetts, where his family settled.

In 1863, after the Emancipation Proclamation allowed Black men to fight, he joined the 54th Massachusetts Volunteer Infantry, the regiment later made famous by the film "Glory".

On July 18, 1863, the 54th led the assault on Fort Wagner, near Charleston, South Carolina. When the color sergeant was shot down, Carney grabbed the flag and carried it up to the parapet. The attack failed. As the regiment fell back, Carney, severely wounded twice, brought the flag back with him. His Medal of Honor citation puts it in one line: he "brought off the flag, under a fierce fire in which he was twice severely wounded."

When he reached his comrades, he is reported to have said: "Boys, I only did my duty; the old flag never touched the ground."

He was discharged in 1864, went home to New Bedford, and became one of the first Black letter carriers in the United States.

The army took 37 years to honor him. On May 23, 1900, he received the Medal of Honor. Twenty Black soldiers and sailors had received the medal before him, but his was the earliest action ever honored: July 18, 1863.

He died on December 9, 1908. At the Massachusetts State House, the flag flew at half-mast.

Shot twice. William Carney never dropped the flag.

## Notes before posting

- He was NOT the first Black Medal of Honor recipient (20 came before him, awarded for later actions); his was the earliest action honored. The reel and post say exactly that.
- "Shot twice": the citation's wording, "twice severely wounded". Wikipedia lists leg, chest and arm; not used.
- The quote is as reported in the sources (his own account); it is labelled "he is reported to have said" in the post.
- Not used: the elevator accident at the State House as the cause of his death (Wikipedia and a forum only); "32 years" as a mail carrier (Wikipedia only); how exactly he reached freedom (Underground Railroad, or his father buying the family out, sources differ).
- Images: 19th-century battle prints, no photographs of violence. Credits in `public/pd/reel-william-carney/credits.json`.
