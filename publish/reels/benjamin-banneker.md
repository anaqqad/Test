He called out Thomas Jefferson. In writing.

Benjamin Banneker was born free in Baltimore County, Maryland, in 1731, and grew up on his family's farm with very little schooling. At about twenty-two he built a striking clock almost entirely out of wood, from his own drawings and calculations. It reportedly kept time for decades.

Later, in 1788, with books and instruments lent to him by a neighbour, George Ellicott, he taught himself astronomy and calculated a coming solar eclipse. In 1791 he joined the surveyor Andrew Ellicott for the first months of the survey of the new federal district, the land that became Washington, D.C.

That same year, on August 19, 1791, he wrote to the Secretary of State, Thomas Jefferson: the man who had written that "all men are created equal", and who held people in slavery. Banneker quoted Jefferson's own words back to him and accused him of "detaining by fraud and violence" people in bondage. With the letter he enclosed his own almanac, full of his astronomical calculations, as proof of what a Black man could do.

Jefferson replied eleven days later. He wrote that "no body wishes more than I do to see such proofs as you exhibit", and sent the almanac to the Marquis de Condorcet, secretary of the Academy of Sciences in Paris. Both letters were printed and sold in Philadelphia in 1792. His almanacs sold in at least 28 editions.

Banneker died in 1806. On the day of his funeral, his house burned to the ground, and his wooden clock with it.

He called out Thomas Jefferson. In writing.

What would you have written to Jefferson?

## Notes before posting

- Myths NOT used: that Banneker laid out Washington, D.C., or redrew L'Enfant's plan from memory; that his clock was "the first clock in America". His survey role was real but short (about two months, early 1791).
- The clock: "reportedly" because its history comes from later accounts. PBS says it burned in the funeral fire.
- His death date is given as 9 or 19 October 1806 in different sources, so only the year is used.
