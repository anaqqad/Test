## Title

He Mailed Himself to Freedom: Henry "Box" Brown

## Post text

In 1849, an enslaved man mailed himself to freedom.

After his wife and children were sold away, Henry Brown had two helpers seal him inside a wooden crate three
feet long, marked "This side up with care," and ship it from Richmond, Virginia, to the Anti-Slavery office in
Philadelphia. For more than a day it went by wagon, train and steamboat, at times upside down. When the lid
came off, he climbed out alive and sang a hymn. From then on, the world called him Henry "Box" Brown.

Would you have had the courage to get in the box?

## Notes before posting

- Hours: 26 (William Still, Encyclopedia Virginia) or 27 (Britannica). The reel says "more than a day".
- Label: Still (1872) and Brown's 1851 narrative say "This side up with care"; the drawing in the 1849
  narrative reads "Right side up with care". The reel uses the first wording, with Still's drawing of the lid.
- "Mailed": he was shipped by Adams' Express, a private express company, not the US Post Office. "Mailed
  himself" is how he is commonly described (Britannica's own wording is "shipped"); keep "shipped" in any
  text that is read as a precise claim.
- No quotes are used. Still's "How do you do, gentlemen?" has only one source, so it is left out.
- Not claimed: the "350 miles" figure, and that he never saw his family again (not verified).
