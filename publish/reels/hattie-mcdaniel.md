Post text:
She won an Oscar. Hollywood still wouldn't let her be buried there.

Hattie McDaniel was the youngest of 13 children of formerly enslaved parents. After the 1929 crash she was stranded and took a job as a washroom attendant in a Milwaukee nightclub, until she talked her way onto its stage.

In Hollywood she was cast as a maid again and again. In 1939 she played Mammy in Gone with the Wind, but she couldn't attend the Atlanta premiere: the theater was for white people only.

On February 29, 1940, she became the first Black person to win an Oscar. At the ceremony she was seated at a separate table on the far side of the room.

When she died in 1952, her last wish was to be buried in Hollywood Cemetery. It refused: whites only. Her Oscar, left to Howard University, disappeared decades ago and has never been found.
