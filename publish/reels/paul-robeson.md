## Facebook

Post text:
He was one of the most famous men in the world. Then America took away his passport.

Paul Robeson's father escaped slavery. At Rutgers, his own teammates broke his nose at football tryouts. He made the team anyway, became an All-American twice and graduated as valedictorian.

He earned a law degree from Columbia, but quit the law after a white secretary refused to take his dictation. So he turned to the stage, and his voice made him a star: Ol' Man River, Othello on Broadway, films in America and Britain.

He used his fame to speak out against lynching and segregation. In 1950 the State Department took his passport. He was blacklisted, and his income fell from over $100,000 a year to almost nothing.

In 1956 Congress asked him why he didn't just stay in Russia. His answer: "Because my father was a slave, and my people died to build this country, and I am going to stay here, and have a part of it just like you."
