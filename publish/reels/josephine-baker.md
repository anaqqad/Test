## Facebook

Post text:
She spied on the Nazis for France, with secret notes in invisible ink on her sheet music. Then a New York nightclub refused to serve her.

Josephine Baker was born poor in St. Louis in 1906. As a child she cleaned houses for white families. At 19 she sailed for Paris, and within a year she was the star of the Folies Bergère.

When war came in 1939, French military intelligence recruited her as a spy. She carried secret notes on her sheet music and pinned inside her underwear. France later gave her the Resistance Medal, the Croix de Guerre and the Legion of Honour.

But in 1951, at New York's Stork Club, she said the staff refused to serve her. That same year she refused to perform in Miami Beach unless Black and white fans could sit together. The club gave in.

In 2021 she became the first Black woman honored in France's Panthéon.
