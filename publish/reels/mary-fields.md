Title:

Post text:
She was 60, armed, and carrying the U.S. mail.

Mary Fields was born enslaved in Tennessee around 1832 and freed after the Civil War. She was six feet tall, famously strong, and famously short-tempered. At the Ursuline convent in Toledo, Ohio, where she kept the grounds, one nun said: "God help anyone who walked on the lawn after Mary had cut it."

In 1885 she travelled to St. Peter's Mission, near Cascade, Montana, to nurse a sick nun, Mother Amadeus, back to health. She stayed, hauling freight, doing the heavy work, and running the mission's supply runs. But she drank, she cursed, and after an armed standoff with a man at the mission, the bishop ordered her out.

So in 1895, in her sixties, Mary Fields won a contract from the U.S. Post Office to carry the mail between Cascade and the mission. She was the first Black woman ever to hold a "star route" contract. She drove a wagon, carried a gun, and when the snow was too deep for the horses she strapped on snowshoes and carried the mail sacks on her own shoulders. For eight years she never missed a day. That is how she became "Stagecoach Mary".

In retirement she ran a laundry from her home, babysat half the children in town, and drank in the saloons of Cascade, a town that celebrated her. She died in 1914, at about 82, and is buried outside Cascade.

The armed woman who carried the mail.

## Notes before posting

- Much of the Mary Fields legend is unverifiable. The reel keeps to facts in the National Park Service, JSTOR Daily and the Postal Museum: born enslaved c. 1832; Toledo convent; Montana 1885; dismissed by the bishop; 1895 star route contract, first Black woman; eight years, never missed a day; snowshoes; carried a gun; laundry; died 1914.
- Not used: her mule Moses; Cascade closing the schools on her birthday (Wikipedia and Find a Grave only); "second woman" to hold a star route contract (NPS only); wolves and bandits; the 1959 Gary Cooper article in Ebony; "six feet tall" is in the post only (widely reported, Postal Museum) and not in the reel.
- Her age in 1895: NPS says "well into her 60s", Wikipedia "about sixty"; the reel says "in her sixties", the hook rounds to 60.
- "Stagecoach Mary": she mostly drove a wagon; the nickname is traditional.
