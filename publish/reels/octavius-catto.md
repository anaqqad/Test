He was shot for voting.

Octavius Valentine Catto was born free in Charleston, South Carolina, on February 22, 1839, and grew up in Philadelphia. He graduated at the top of his class from the Institute for Colored Youth in 1858 and came back to teach there.

During the Civil War he helped recruit Black regiments for the Union army. In May 1865 he refused to leave a whites-only streetcar in Philadelphia; the crew left him sitting in the car all night. His campaign, with his fiancée Caroline LeCount and the abolitionist William Still, led to the 1867 Pennsylvania law that outlawed segregation on the state's streetcars.

He also co-founded the Pythian Base Ball Club, one of the first Black baseball teams. In 1867 the National Association of Base Ball Players refused to admit them, drawing the colour line in American baseball.

In 1870 the Fifteenth Amendment gave Black men the vote, and Catto had fought for its ratification in Pennsylvania. On Election Day, October 10, 1871, white mobs attacked Black voters in Philadelphia, and the police did little. On his way to vote, near his home on South Street, Catto was shot dead by a white man named Frank Kelly. He was 32.

His funeral was one of the largest the city had ever seen. Kelly fled, was found in Chicago six years later and tried in 1877. A jury with no Black members acquitted him.

In 2017, 146 years after his murder, Philadelphia unveiled a twelve-foot bronze statue of Octavius Catto at City Hall, the first public statue of an individual Black American in the city.

He was shot for voting. Remember his name.

## Notes before posting

- Number of shots fired varies by source (three or four); the reel just says "shot him dead". No image of the shooting; the Election Day beat uses an 1863 street view.
- Funeral size varies (5,000 mourners in the procession vs "tens of thousands" watching); the reel says "one of the largest".
- The Pennsylvania streetcar law: March 1867; Catto's "bill of rights" for transit (SABR) is summarised as "his campaign forced it".
- Not used: "eleven regiments" (SABR only); the unhitched horses detail; his major's commission; Caroline LeCount's own court case.
