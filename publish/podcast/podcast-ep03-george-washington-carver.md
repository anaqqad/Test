As a baby, he was traded for a horse. He grew up to become one of the most famous scientists in America.

George Washington Carver was born into slavery in Missouri, kidnapped as an infant, and turned away from college because he was Black. He became Iowa State's first Black student and faculty member, spent nearly fifty years at Tuskegee helping poor farmers escape cotton, held Congress's attention far beyond his ten minutes, and was honored with the first national monument to an African American. And no, he did not invent peanut butter.
