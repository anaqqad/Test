## Description

In 1921, Greenwood in Tulsa, Oklahoma, was one of the most successful Black communities in America, with its own newspapers, churches, hotel, theater, doctors and lawyers. People called its main street the Negro Wall Street. On May 31 and June 1, 1921, after a newspaper story about an incident in an elevator, a white mob crossed the railroad tracks and burned 35 blocks of it to the ground. About 10,000 people lost their homes. For most of a century, the story was left out of the history books.

Every fact in this video comes from at least two reliable sources. No graphic images are shown.
