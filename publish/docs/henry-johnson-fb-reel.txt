He fought off a German raid almost alone. America took 97 years to thank him.

In May 1918, Private Henry Johnson, a railroad porter from Albany, New York, serving with the 369th Infantry, the Harlem Hellfighters, was on sentry duty in France when a German raiding party attacked his post. Wounded 21 times, he fought them off and saved his fellow soldier Needham Roberts from capture. France awarded him the Croix de Guerre. His own army sent him home with discharge papers that did not mention a single wound. It took 97 years for the United States to award him the Medal of Honor.
