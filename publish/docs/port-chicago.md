## Title

320 Dead. Then the Navy Put Survivors on Trial for Mutiny.

## Description

On July 17, 1944, two explosions destroyed the Navy ammunition pier at Port Chicago, California, killing 320 people, 202 of them Black sailors. Three weeks later, 258 survivors refused to go back to loading ammunition. Fifty were tried for mutiny in the largest mutiny trial in U.S. Navy history, with Thurgood Marshall watching, and all 50 were found guilty. It took the Navy until July 17, 2024 to exonerate them.
