## Title

Born Enslaved, He Rode 500 Miles to Prove the Army Wrong

## Description

Charles Young was born enslaved in Kentucky in 1864. He became the third Black graduate of West Point, led Buffalo Soldiers, served as the first Black superintendent of a national park, mapped Haiti, and led a cavalry charge in Mexico without losing a man. In 1917, as the highest-ranking Black officer in the U.S. Army, he was retired as medically unfit, just as America entered the First World War. So in June 1918 he rode 497 miles on horseback, from Wilberforce, Ohio, to Washington, D.C., to prove he could still serve.
