She escaped slavery disguised as a white man. Her husband played her slave.

In December 1848, Ellen Craft, an enslaved woman in Macon, Georgia, cut her hair, put on a top hat, green glasses and a sling, and travelled as a sick white planter named William Johnson. Her husband William travelled as her slave. For four days they rode trains and steamboats through the slave South, past a friend of her enslaver, a slave dealer and an officer in Baltimore, and reached Philadelphia on Christmas Day. Then the Fugitive Slave Act sent slave catchers after them, and they had to run again, all the way to England.
