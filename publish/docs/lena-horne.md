## Title

Filmed So the South Could Cut Her Out: The Lena Horne Story

## Description

Lena Horne signed a seven-year contract with MGM in 1942, a first for a Black woman in decades, on the condition that she would not play maids. So the studio put her in scenes of her own, singing alone, apart from the white stars, so that theaters in the South could cut her out of the film. She walked off a stage when German prisoners of war were seated in front of Black soldiers, married across the color line in Paris because California forbade it, was blacklisted, marched on Washington, and at 63 opened her own one-woman show on Broadway.
