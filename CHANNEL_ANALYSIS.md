## 2. What the winners have in common

All five hits use exactly the same skeleton:

> **German POWs [were told / thought / swore] [a false belief about America]. Then [they SAW an ordinary American thing].**

1. **The proof is something you can see, and it's mundane.** Cars in a parking lot, a train that won't stop,
   a harbor skyline, schoolchildren, Main Street. The viewer can picture the moment from the title alone.
2. **The theme is America's scale and abundance as seen by the enemy.** Big, rich, untouched, endless. The two
   biggest videos are both about **the cross-country train ride** (616k, 269k), and the third is the arrival in
   New York harbor. The audience (older, mostly American viewers) enjoys seeing their country through astonished
   outsider eyes.
3. **Belief vs. reality inside the title**, with a "Then..." twist that resolves it in one image.
4. **It takes place in the USA.**

## 3. What underperforms

| Pattern | Example | Why it likely fails |
|---|---|---|
| No propaganda-vs-reality contrast | Wedding at Aliceville, 80 cents a day, U-boat in Chicago | No tension or "aha" in the title, just a fact |
| Not about America | Canada | The audience comes for America through enemy eyes |
| The proof is reading or politics, not seeing | Newspapers / FDR, Congress menus | Abstract and hard to picture; the Congress one shifts the viewpoint away from the POWs |
| Named-person title | Hans Goebeler | Unknown name, no hook |
| Grievance angle | "Never saw a cent" | Off-brand for a channel built on wonder at American plenty |

The tractor-farmer video (OK, not a hit) follows the formula, but the "proof" is a kindness, not a spectacle
of scale. Kindness works less well than scale.

## 4. Title rules for the next videos
- Keep the exact skeleton. Open with "German POWs". Keep the false belief to 3-6 words. Make the "Then" clause
  an **image** (a place, an object or a crowd), not an argument.
- Prefer **scale / abundance / normal life** proof: distance, lights, ships, food in shops, factories, highways,
  farm country.
- Put the setting inside the USA, use no named individuals in the title, keep it under ~90 characters.
- Lean on the train/journey/size theme, because sequels to the two breakouts are the safest bets.

## 5. Suggested next videos (ranked)

The facts below are the angles to research. Per the blueprint ("no source, no video"), confirm each one in a
memoir, oral history or camp report before scripting.

1. **German POWs Were Told American Cities Were Blacked Out. Then the Train Passed Through Chicago at Night.**
   Germany was under a strict blackout, while interior US cities stayed lit (only the coasts had dim-outs). It's
   the train theme again, plus a striking night image.
2. **German POWs Were Told U-Boats Had Closed America's Ports. Then They Counted the Ships.**
   The U-boat war was a core German propaganda claim. A harbor full of Liberty ships and convoys directly echoes
   the New York harbor hit.
3. **German POWs Thought They'd Reach the Camp by Nightfall. The Train Ran for Four Days.**
   A direct sequel to "going in circles": what the scale of America meant to men from a country you could cross
   in a day. Check the typical East Coast → Texas/Kansas/Arizona journey times.
4. **German POWs Were Told Texas Was an Empty Desert. Then They Saw the Cotton Fields.**
   Scale and abundance; many camps were in Texas; big-sky farm footage is easy to find.
5. **German POWs Were Told Americans Were Going Hungry. Then the Guard Bought Them a Coca-Cola.**
   A small, iconic American object as the proof. Research PX/canteen access and the documented scrip system.
6. **German POWs Were Told America Couldn't Build Ships Fast Enough. Then They Saw a Liberty Ship Launched.**
   Production scale, the same theme as the staged-cars breakout. Check whether any POW memoir records seeing a
   shipyard or launch.

**Make next:** #1 and #3, because they extend the two breakout videos. Hold off on non-US settings, politics and
person-named titles until the channel is bigger.

## 6. Also worth testing
- **Thumbnails**: the breakouts' thumbnails weren't analysed here. Check in Studio whether they share a look
  (e.g. a train window or crowd) and reuse it.
- **Upload cadence** is about every 1-2 days. If a slower pace frees research time, fewer but stronger "scale"
  topics should beat many weak ones.
- In YouTube Studio, compare CTR and 30-second retention for the flops vs. the hits to see whether the title or
  the opening hook is what failed.
