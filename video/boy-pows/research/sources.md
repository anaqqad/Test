# Boy POWs in America - research sources (27 Nov 2026)

Rule: only pages actually read (WebFetch / curl). Quotes copied from the fetch result. "NOT in page" = searched, not there.
Honest summary: no first-person account of an under-16 German POW held IN the US was found. Best documented teenagers: Welzel (drafted at 16, captured 10 Oct 1944 "just shy of his 18th birthday"), Kleindt and the Houlton group (Maine). Older young men (Golz 19, Erichsen 19, Gräwe 18, Ernst ~18) are backup.

## A. Named people

### 1. NPR, "A Veteran's Journey From Hitler Youth To U.S. Army" (Whit Richardson, All Things Considered, 11 Nov 2010) - HANK WELZEL
URL: https://www.npr.org/transcripts/131186773 (full transcript read via curl)
- Lede: "At 16, he was drafted into the German army during World War II. Captured by the Americans, he spent the rest of the war at a POW camp in the U.S. -- the country of his birth."
- Welzel: "This is the Hitler Youth when I was about 12. Okay, and this is my first week in the German army. I'm probably 16 and a half or so."
- Narrator: "He was 13 when the war began; 16 when the German army drafted him. He was trained as a medic and sent to the Italian front to face the U.S. and British armies."
- Welzel: "You had to consider yourself a German if you wanted to stay alive. You know what I mean? You know, you had to play the game, you know?"
- Narrator: "On October 10, 1944, just shy of his 18th birthday, Hank was captured on a hill north of Florence."
- Welzel: "Should I tell him I was born in the United States or shouldn't I? ... I never told anybody that I was an American citizen by birth. That was my secret. You know, it was my highest secret."
- Narrator: "Hank spent the rest of the war at a prisoner of war camp in Alabama, keeping that secret." After the war, in France (Marshall Plan), he told the US embassy in Paris; "He was 23 in 1949 when he arrived in the United States, with nothing but his German accent and a suitcase of dirty laundry." Marines refused him (last address East Germany); a year later the Army drafted him; medic in Korea, Purple Heart and Bronze Star. Nightmares "for 45 years"; wife Gloria: they kept his German service secret "until the kids were in high school".
- NOT in transcript: fear of being shot, the ship, food, guards.

### 2. Bangor Daily News, Eric Russell, 28 May 2010, "WWII POW to share unrivaled story" - Welzel (called "Henry")
URL: https://www.bangordailynews.com/2010/05/28/bangor/wwii-pow-to-share-unrivaled-story/
(Fetch-tool summary, not raw text.) "first deployed at 17, in 1943" (conflicts with NPR). Entire unit captured in Italy by US soldiers; about two weeks in Italy, then Spain, then an ocean liner; "a few weeks after leaving Spain ... back on American soil in Norfolk, Va."; then "Fort Rucker" (sic) Alabama about a year.
- On Hitler Youth: as a teenage male "you didn't really have a choice."
- "There were still some serious Nazis there" and they treated him as an enemy.
- 1948 France; left post to visit parents; Russians held him ~6 months in East Germany: "But I was an American." Reached US embassy; drafted <2 years later; Korea 1951; home for good early 1953.
- Search snippet only (page 403): Sun Journal puts the camp at "Camp Rucker, Ala." (https://www.sunjournal.com/?p=230954).

### 3. America magazine, Bea Broder-Oldach, "Blessed Are the Peacemakers", 11 Dec 2006 - GERHARD KLEINDT, Camp Houlton, Maine
URL: https://www.americamagazine.org/faithinfocus/2006/12/11/blessed-are-peacemakers/
(Fetch-tool summary.) Kleindt "An 18-year-old draftee"; vehicle overturned in a gunfight on a Normandy beach, Americans pulled him to safety; "He was certain he would be shot immediately."
- Jobs: first cooked for the guards, then a potato farm; camp education programs "that prepared him for life after the war".
- Kleindt: "The best years of my life were spent in a prison camp behind barbed wire." Called the war "a senseless war that Germany had started."
- Unnamed young POW broke a colander of apples in a farm kitchen, braced for punishment; kindness followed, "he left with a box of apples". Another young POW in a Maine logging camp "thought he was digging his own grave and wept. His guards comforted him."
- Rudi Richter, back in Germany, could not recognise his bombed hometown, asked an old man for directions to his family home and the stranger was his own father. John (Vohn) Micka: Maine farmer friends sent food and baby formula. Farmer Fenton Shaw arranged a two-day Christmas holiday; POWs sang "Silent Night" outside barracks.
- Verify exact wording against the raw page before using any as a quote.

### 4. Lauren Wolfe, Substack "Chills", 12 Feb 2021 (reprints her 2003 Houlton reunion piece; original outlet not named)
URL: https://chills.substack.com/p/i-interviewed-4-wwii-nazis-and-then
(Fetch-tool summary.) Hans Krueger "17 when captured" ("the best time of my life"). Gerhardt Kleindt "17 when conscripted"; captured Normandy after his convoy was attacked at night; before capture "he had been told that American forces would not take prisoners"; GIs "fed him milk and cornflakes"; Americans "saved my life".
- Opening line of the post (read raw): "Actual, 80-something-year-old Nazis, who had been held in the town as young prisoners of war."

### 5. VOA, Adam Allington, "German POWs Return to Maine in Friendship" (page dated 29 Oct 2009 by fetch tool; URL slug says 2005-05-06 - unresolved)
URL: https://www.voanews.com/a/a-13-2005-05-06-voa46-67389482/275278.html
- Men: Hans Krueger (arrived Maine summer 1944), Gerhardt Kleindt, Rudi Richter, Hans-Georg Augustin.
- Richter: "As young men, we thought we were doing a good thing to become a soldier, and we feel as though we have been misused."
- Krueger: "This POW time has changed my life and the outlook of my life and how to see people and judge people."
- Augustin: "This was a part of my youth," and "I want to see it again." Kleindt: "Four years of our youth."
- Work: potatoes; lumber for a paper mill. Ages NOT stated.

### 6. Dialog International, David Vickrey, "POWs Then and Now", 5 Jan 2005
URL: https://dialoginternational.com/?p=6422
- Rudy Richter, enlisted as a teenager, Camp Houlton 1944-46: "When we got to the camp, we helped pick potatoes and got paid a few cents per barrel."
- Reinhold Pabel (Camp Ellis, adult; already our chicago-lights subject): "At Camp Ellis, Illinois, I decided to take full advantage of the educational facilities which were provided."
- Classes in English, chemistry, mathematics "often taught by fellow POWs who had been teachers, lawyers, and tradesmen".

### 7. University Times (UMPI), Sandra McDonald, 9 Mar 2018
URL: https://wp.umpi.edu/utimes/2018/03/09/world-war-ii-german-pows-in-maine/
- German POWs "15 years of age and older"; younger boys and older men drafted as the war turned.
- A guard asked a POW why he was crying digging a cesspool; he "thought they was digging their own graves."
- Local Kay Bell: prisoners "just boys, just like our boys."
- Houlton up to 4,000 POWs; 80 cents a day; farmers paid government 45 cents/hour per man; Red Cross rated it highly in 1944.

### 8. Stars and Stripes (reprint of Bangor Daily News, Troy R. Bennett), 27 Jul 2021
URL: https://stripes.com/history/2021-07-27/houlton-army-air-base-german-pows-world-war-II-2332577.html
- "One unnamed soldier, 15, cried when asked to dig a latrine pit because he thought he was digging his own grave."
- About 3,500 men at Camp Houlton 1944-46; paid "a dollar a day in credit"; University of Maine offered classes; curator Laskey: "A lot of them were reluctant soldiers." 2003: former prisoners returned, honorary citizens.

### 9. HistoryNet, Liesl Bradner (25 Oct 2017, upd. 2022) - PAUL GOLZ (19 at capture), backup / expectation quote
URL: https://historynet.com/paul-golz-german-view-d-day/
- Born 4 Apr 1925; joined at 17 (fall 1943); captured 9 June 1944 at La Fière, age 19.
- "The Americans were nice-I didn't think they would shoot me."
- On a British transport after three days without food: "frankfurter sausage, mashed potatoes, white bread and a cup of coffee." Queen Mary to New York; Camp Patrick Henry, Newport News VA. Released from US in May 1946: "We were told we were going home," but sent to Scotland; home Oct 1947.

### 10. Army.mil, Ruth Kingsland, 13 Oct 2017 - GÜNTER GRÄWE (18) - backup
URL: https://www.army.mil/article/195355/former_german_pow_says_thank_you_america
- Captured Normandy Aug 1944, age 18, foot wound; Queen Mary to "Dover, Maine"; camp at Camp Lewis (JBLM). 80 cents a day picking apples/potatoes in Washington, cotton in Arizona. "All we cared about was the food." "I suddenly realized how extremely lucky I had been to be captured by the American Army and not the Russian one." Married 1950.

### 11. WOOD TV / Yahoo, Matt Jaworowski, 30 Jun 2024 - "ERNST" (b. Aug 1925, ~18 at Normandy) - backup
URL: https://www.yahoo.com/news/ernst-story-german-pows-became-090000030.html (as fetched: https://www.yahoo.com/news/ernst-story-german-pow-became-090000030.html)
- "I wanted to become a prisoner. I didn't want to fight for that Germany." Convoy to New York 12 days; Ellis Island, first shower in three months; Camp Custer, then Camp Ellis, "Our work was night work in the corn cannery." Custer meals "a happy time". Emigrated 1957, citizen 1967.

### 12. Heino Erichsen, "The Reluctant Warrior" (Eakin 2001) - 19 in 1943, NOT a boy
- TexasEscapes: http://texasescapes.com/WorldWarII/ReluctantWarriorGermanPOWinTexas/RelectantWarriorByHRErichsen.htm : Hearne -> Mexia (escaping "die-hard Nazis who intimidated the regular soldiers who just wanted to sit the war out and correspond with their families") -> "Fort Knox, Kentucky for the duration of the war"; talks to guards in English. Age/voyage/expectation NOT in page.
- Army.mil https://www.army.mil/article/201077/german : died at 93; baked at Fort Knox under Maj. John Warrick.
- Search snippet only: "19-year-old German soldier Heino Erichsen", Ellis Island train to Hearne, ~3,200 POWs there.

### 13. Peter Lang "Youth at War" - GERHARD G. (b.1927) letters
URL: https://www.peterlang.com/document/1129644
- Luftwaffenhelfer at 15 (Sept 1943); "at the age of seventeen" became "American prisoner of war" May 1945; "Because of his young age, he was released and returned home in August 1945." Held in Europe, not US.

## B. General facts

### 14. Wikipedia "German prisoners of war in the United States" (first 100,000 chars read; pointer only)
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoners_of_war_in_the_United_States
- "A total of 425,000 German prisoners lived in 700 camps across the United States during World War II."
- 1929 Convention: "40 square feet for enlisted men and 120 for officers"; same rations as US soldiers; enlisted min "$0.80 a day"; scrip; "$22 million in 1944" from POW wages.
- Special Projects Division from fall 1943; "The 25,000 graduates of these classes returned directly to Germany"; little change found in most prisoners' views.
- Most worked in US until 1946, then "up to three more years as laborers in France and the United Kingdom".
- Schooling: POW-taught courses (German, English, languages, business, maths); May 1944 procedure via Red Cross for credit at German schools/universities; some correspondence classes through local universities.
- Ages of prisoners: NOT stated.

### 15. Geneva Convention 1929 (Yale Avalon) https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/geneva02.asp
- Art. 11: "The food ration of prisoners of war shall be equal in quantity and quality to that of troops at base camps."
- Art. 17: "So far as possible belligerents shall encourage intellectual diversions and sports organized by prisoners of war."
- Art. 31: "Labor furnished by prisoners of war shall have no direct relation with war operations."
- Art. 27: "Noncommissioned officers who are prisoners of war shall only be required to do supervisory work" (tool truncated; check full text).

### 16. HistoryNet, Ronald H. Bailey (11 Sep 2008, upd. 2022) https://historynet.com/the-secret-and-controversial-attempt-to-teach-german-pows-about-freedom/
- "nearly three hundred eighty thousand Germans imprisoned in the United States"; "nearly five hundred camps" (caption). Launched summer 1944 under cover name "Intellectual Diversion Program". Stimson: "the feasible one of imbuing them with respect for the quality and potency of American institutions." (Page 1 only.)

### 17. New England Historical Society https://newenglandhistoricalsociety.com/top-secret-reeducation-camp-narragansett-r/
- Special Projects Division ran three secret camps in Rhode Island: Forts Kearny, Getty, Wetherill; "Idea Factory". 27 Feb 1945: 85 German writers, artists, professors moved to Fort Kearny. Der Ruf Feb 1945 - Apr 1946; "a nickel per copy"; 26 editions; fort closed Apr 1946. Hardcore Nazis went to a max-security prison in Oklahoma. (Fetch summary; verify.)

### 18. Wikipedia "Der Ruf (newspaper)" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Ruf_(newspaper)
- First issue 1 Mar 1945, 11,000 copies to 134 camps; later 75,000; five cents; 26 issues; last 1 Apr 1946. Capt Robert Kunzig: "indoctrination by correct facts." Staff incl. Hocke, Andersch, Hans Werner Richter.
- The program targeted screened anti-Nazi adults; no source found that boys took part.

### 19. Baylor Line https://baylorline.com/german-pows-in-texas/
- Wilhelm Nellessen (bank employee) took an art history course at Camp Hearne; notebook shown at Camp Hearne museum. "Baylor and 102 other American universities offered higher education to German soldiers."

### 20. Bangor Daily News, 17 Sep 2019 https://bangordailynews.com/2019/09/17/news/nazi-pows-may-have-lived-better-at-this-down-east-camp-than-their-passamaquoddy-neighbors/
- Indian Township camp: "More than 200 young German soldiers", 1944-46; "Saturday dances in Princeton or take UMaine correspondence courses".

### 21. Pechmann oral history, Wisconsin Veterans Museum OH345 (1996) https://wisvetsmuseum.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Pechmann-Kurt_OH345.pdf (read via pdftotext, abstract)
- Adult (b. 1 Mar 1922), captured 6 Nov 1943; Naples to Norfolk; Camp Ellis, Hoopeston; "clean barracks, the 'German-friendly' guards, and the high-quality food"; farmer in Lodi, Wisconsin served a large feast; Roosevelt apology over delayed rations after a Mississippi flood; handed to French after the war, forced farm labour; emigrated 1952. Not a boy; camp texture only.

## Disagreements
- Welzel age: NPR "16 when the German army drafted him"; captured "just shy of his 18th birthday" (10 Oct 1944) vs BDN "first deployed at 17, in 1943". Use NPR.
- Welzel route: NPR "camp in Alabama"; BDN "Fort Rucker" (sic), mentions Spain and Norfolk; BDN calls him "Henry".
- Kleindt age: Wolfe "17 when conscripted"; America magazine "18-year-old draftee"; a search snippet said the Houlton four were drafted at "15 and 16" (source not found). Say "17 or 18".
- Houlton size: Stripes "about 3,500"; UMPI "up to 4,000"; Dialog "More than 6,000"; a snippet "more than 1,100". Unresolved.
- POW pay: 80 cents/day (Wikipedia, UMPI, Gräwe) vs Stripes "a dollar a day in credit". Gräwe article: Nazi prisoners "10 cents a day" per a JBLM historian (fetch summary) - unclear.
- Total German POWs: 425,000 (Wikipedia, over whole war); "nearly 380,000" (HistoryNet); 371,683 end May 1945 (snippet only); 307,931 (snippet).
- Henke photo: Commons "15-year-old"; other sites say 16; Commons does not confirm the boy is Henke. Photo is in Germany (Giessen, Apr 1945).
- Golz: 19 at capture (HistoryNet); a snippet said he joined at 17 (consistent). 
- VOA date: 2009 in text vs 2005 in URL slug.
