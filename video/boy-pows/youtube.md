## Description
```
In the woods of northern Maine, an American guard handed a 15-year-old German prisoner of war a shovel and told him to dig. The boy began to cry: he believed he was digging his own grave. It was a latrine pit.

This is the documented story of the teenage German soldiers sent to America as prisoners of war: Gerhard Kleindt, told the Americans took no prisoners, who was fed milk and cornflakes instead; Hank Welzel, drafted at 16, who hid a secret in a POW camp in Alabama — he had been born in the United States; and the boys of Camp Houlton, Maine, who picked potatoes, cut pulpwood, sang Silent Night for a farm family, and came back sixty years later as honorary citizens.
```
