# Sources: Wainwright, MacArthur and the pen

## Why this topic
The reference channel WWII Box (@WWIIBox; our format blueprint came from its "German POWs Thought America Staged the Cars")
has run a "Japan Expected MacArthur to X. Then He Y." series since early November 2026: Let Them Starve 135K views in 12 days,
Hang Hirohito 80K, Women's Vote 65K, Jail Its Soldiers 49K in 3 days. The owner asked for an episode in that title and
thumbnail format. Our documented, prisoner-of-war version: Wainwright, the general MacArthur left on Corregidor.

Full research notes, page references and photo list: `research/facts_raw.md` (sources [1]–[16] there).

## How the script uses the sources
- The Missouri call "Will General Percival and General Wainwright step forward…": Citino, National WWII Museum [8].
- Five pens; the first, mid-signature, to Wainwright; the next to Percival; three for institutions; Mrs. MacArthur's red pen:
  Wainwright's own 1945 serial [2]; Wolf [9] and Citino [8] differ in detail (see Disagreements in facts_raw.md).
- The March 1942 farewell (repeated protests, cigars and shaving cream, third star, "if he is alive"): Wainwright's 1945
  serial [1]; Morton [4]. "I came through and I shall return": Morton [4], citing the NYT of 21 Mar 1942.
- 6 May 1942: "to sacrifice one day of freedom…", the message to Roosevelt, Homma's demand and "my equal", the 11,000 men,
  the KZRH broadcast "in a voice husky with suppressed emotion": Morton [4].
- "Orders … have no validity" and "temporarily become unbalanced": Morton [4] (radiograms of 9 May 1942).
- July 1942 radiogram against the Medal of Honor: Sterner [7] (government radiogram as quoted); Marshall withdrew it and
  revived it on 5 Sep 1945 with no objections [7].
- Tarlac, Karenko, weight 132 lb (Oct 1942), the Domei reporter's question and the diary reasons: [2], [3].
- Camps and the move to Sian with 15 other senior prisoners (Dec 1944): [2], [3].
- The OSS team (16 Aug), Lamar and Leith at Sian (19 Aug), "what people at home thought of him", the trip out, the flag on
  28 Aug after 39 months: [2], [12], [13], [14].
- The reunion and its versions (Wainwright 1945; MacArthur 1964; Casey's 1964 letter): [2], [10], [11].
- Baguio, 3 Sep 1945, and the second pen from Gen. Leavey: [2].
- 10 Sep 1945: more than 100,000 at the Monument, "Remember Bataan! Remember Corregidor!", Truman's walk in the garden, the
  citation: [2], [3], [5]. New York parade 13 Sep: [16].
- Truman's diary, 17 Jun 1945: [6].
- Fourth Army, retired 1947, died 2 Sep 1953: [16].

## Disagreements
See `research/facts_raw.md`, section "Disagreements". The script follows Wainwright's 1945 account for the pens and gives
both versions of the reunion. MacArthur's departure date (11 or 12 March) is not stated in the script.
