# Sources: "A German POW Escaped to Detroit. The Man Who Fed Him Was Sentenced to Hang."

No source, no line. Every fact in `script.md` maps to one of the entries below.

## Why this topic (competitor research, 3 Nov 2026)
YouTube search sorted by upload date (3 Nov 2026): the "German POWs met German-speaking Americans" format is the newest hit
("German POWs Were Told Americans Hated Germans. Then a Texas Farmer Answered in German.", Captive Witness, 220K in 4 days;
"German POWs Couldn't Believe Texas Farmers Spoke Fluent German", 62K). The arrival/food/generals formats keep pulling views
(248K, 266K, 299K in 2-3 weeks). Almost all of them are dramatized. Our documented version of the German-American format:
a German-born Detroit restaurant owner who spent one weekend helping an escaped Luftwaffe prisoner, and was sentenced to hang
for treason. No YouTube video covers the Max Stephan / Hans Peter Krug case (searches "max stephan treason", "hans peter krug
escape", "german pow escaped detroit": nothing relevant). Hook = the "birthday pleasure trip" from the court record.

## Main sources
1. **Stephan v. United States, 133 F.2d 87 (6th Cir., 6 Feb. 1943), opinion by Judge Hicks** (via the Caselaw Access
   Project, static.case.law/f2d/133/cases/0087-01.json). Public-domain court record; the main source:
   - Krug: German citizen, Oberleutnant in the German Air Force; on 28 Aug. 1940 piloting a bombing plane over England, shot
     down and captured, injured, hospital; January 1941 to a war prisoners' camp near Neys, Ontario; November 1941 to the
     officers' camp at Bowmanville; escaped (court: 17 Apr. 1942). Forged papers; a priest "influenced by false stories";
     reached Windsor; a stolen boat across the river to Detroit.
   - About 9:00-9:30 a.m. on 18 Apr., in coveralls, to 259 Phillip Ave., home of Mrs. Bertelmann (name and address from
     fellow prisoners); showed the epaulets of a German officer. She was German-born, came to Canada in 1928, never a U.S.
     citizen; knitted for the German prisoners in Canada and through Stephan bought tobacco for them; packages had to carry the
     sender's name and address. She phoned Stephan; he arrived 10-15 minutes later.
   - Bertelmann: she was scared, "it was not right to have him in her house"; Stephan: "what are you shaking for? You're
     crazy." Krug: Stephan sat down, "held his hands on the upper part of his legs and smiled." Bertelmann heard Stephan ask
     "Why don't you give up, you haven't a chance?"; Krug: "I have to try". Stephan told Krug it would be impossible to get to
     Germany by way of South America and that he should go back where he came from.
   - Shoes (broken sole), socks she had darned for her husband, underwear she had planned to send to Canada; her $20 on the
     table near Stephan, gone when the men left. Stephan escorted Krug to his car (overt act 3, proved by two witnesses).
   - Restaurant at 7209 Jefferson Avenue: Stephan went in the back, told Krug to use the front; Krug ordered food, did not pay;
     walked downtown; afternoon: "Now, I have nothing to do and it is your birthday today and we will take a pleasure trip."
     Gave a necktie and billfold; bought a small traveling bag.
   - Stops: a glassware and crockery rental business (the owner phoned about trains to Chicago and the fare); Haller's Cafe,
     1407 Randolph (beer and whiskey; "one of the Meyers boys"; the forgotten bag); the Progressive Club/Hall (beer; "a friend
     from Milwaukee"); Theodore Donay's shop: Krug introduced himself as "Oberlieutnant Krug", told of his escape, the camp and
     his plan to get back to Germany; candy; $20 from Donay's cash drawer; clerk Rintelen saw the three in a secret
     conversation and next day found a slip for $20.68. Krug told Donay the story of a German lieutenant (Von Werra) captured
     in New York who got back to Germany.
   - Testimony about a visit to an "assignation house" that day was struck by the judge.
   - Back at the restaurant about 6:00-6:30 p.m.; meal; sat until about 9:00 p.m.; introduced to Karl and Eva Erhardt as a
     friend from Milwaukee; waitress Erna Schwartz offered a vacant room; Stephan: "Oh, I think he can go in Field Hotel to
     sleep."; let out the back door. 19 Apr., about 8 a.m. at the restaurant door by arrangement; Stephan had looked up the bus
     schedule; breakfast downtown; bus ticket to Chicago.
   - 20 Apr. 1942: written statement to the FBI (checked bus schedules, bought the bag, went to the station, bought the ticket).
   - Stephan naturalized 24 June 1935, oath 28 June 1935 (renounce "particularly to Germany").
   - Indictment: twelve overt acts on 18-19 Apr. 1942; constitutional rule of two witnesses to the same overt act; intent to
     aid the enemy is essential. Court of Appeals: acts 3, 7, 11 and 12 proved by two witnesses; 1, 2, 8 and 9 not. Stephan
     introduced no testimony. Affirmed.
   - Krug refused to answer (military secrets): money on arrival and departure, where he landed, his squadron, how long he
     stayed in Chicago, why he went to New York, a handwritten card found on him in San Antonio ("I won't read it"). In the
     afternoon: "I don't intend to testify against Stephan." The court: "I have nothing to do with whom the testimony helps or
     who it harms." Krug: "I never can testify against a man who helped me". Brought from Canada in the custody of Canadian
     military authorities.
   - Exhibits: epaulets, a Greyhound bus map, "Matthews Atlas of the World at War", a revolver and cartridges taken from
     Krug at his recapture.
   - Prosecutor's closing: it was Stephan who tried "to get Lieutenant Krug back to Germany so he could get up in a bomber and
     come over here and bomb the United States"; "a blackhearted traitor".
2. **United States v. Stephan, 50 F. Supp. 738 (E.D. Mich., 5 June 1943), Judge Tuttle**: grand jury of 23 indicted 17 June
   1942; jury impaneled 29 June; verdict 2 July 1942; death sentence 6 Aug. 1942 (execution first set for 13 Nov. 1942, then
   27 Apr. 1943); jury told he was not guilty if he helped Krug "through friendship or through sympathy"; Supreme Court refused
   certiorari; "some sympathetic people ... state to the public press and to the President" that he acted from sympathy; "the
   statement is not supported by the record"; "There should be no trifling with traitors."
3. **Wikipedia, "Max Stephan"** (citing the Detroit Free Press, Detroit Times, Windsor Star 1942-43 and the trial record):
   born 10 July 1892, Cologne; Imperial German Army, wounded 1915; Cologne police officer; to Windsor 1928; Detroit 1933;
   citizens 1935; restaurant a meeting place of the German American Bund, membership denied. Krug escaped 16 Apr. 1942 (court:
   17th); shot down "over the English Channel in 1941" (court: over England, 28 Aug. 1940). In Toronto stopped by military
   police, a forged document as a carpenter of the SS Normandie's crew; Father McGrath bought him a bus ticket to Windsor; rowed
   across in a stolen rowboat, reached Belle Isle about 9 p.m.; slept in a doorway and a vegetable cart. Clerk's tip to the FBI;
   Stephan arrested Monday morning, 20 Apr. Krug arrested 1 May 1942 in San Antonio during a manhunt to stop him reaching
   Mexico. U.S. Attorney John C. Lehr sought treason; defence counsel Verne Amberson compared Stephan to the Good Samaritan,
   Lehr rejected it. Jury out 1 hour 23 minutes, verdict 5:39 p.m. 2 July. First person convicted of federal treason in a
   civilian court since the 1790s Whiskey Rebellion. Tuttle at sentencing: "The life of the traitor Max Stephan is less
   valuable than the lives of our loyal sons"; "the penalty for treason is death." Execution set (5 June 1943) for 2 July 1943
   at the Milan prison; scaffold built. Commutation received 4:55 p.m., 1 July 1943, nine hours before; Roosevelt believed a
   death sentence too harsh. White House statement: "The man was properly convicted of treason. He was guilty."; "pro-German or
   pro-Nazi"; "the President hopes that no one of his successors will commute that sentence." Atlanta penitentiary eight
   years, then the Medical Center at Springfield, Missouri; died of intestinal cancer, 13 Jan. 1952. Agnes Stephan interned,
   deported; Bertelmann interned, deported; Donay disappeared 1950 near Catalina Island. Krug returned to Canada, escaped again
   in August 1943, recaptured, held in Britain until 1946; later a steel salesman in West Germany. Headline: "Citizen Who Helped
   Nazi Escape Sorry for Self", Detroit Free Press, 22 Apr. 1942.
4. **John Sase with Gerard J. Senick, "Treason in Detroit - The curious case of Max Stephan" (part two), *Detroit Legal News*,
   17 Mar. 2021** (authors of *Peter Krug & Max Stephan: Nazis, Espionage, and Treason in Detroit*): in Chicago people outfitted
   Krug with a suit and $100 and sent him to an address in Philadelphia, then New York (hoped to stow away on a neutral Swedish
   ship), then Pittsburgh, Cincinnati, Nashville, Dallas, aiming to cross into still-neutral Mexico at Laredo; in San Antonio
   the woman who ran his hotel heard his accent and called the FBI; Krug "had the duty to attempt an escape"; the Geneva rule
   that a POW could not be forced to testify; Donay later convicted and jailed; Krug a businessman near Dortmund. Their
   espionage-ring speculation is not used. Used as "one later account".
5. **Wikipedia, "Bowmanville POW camp"**: Camp 30; built as the Bowmanville Boys Training School; converted in 1941: barbed-wire
   fences 15 ft apart, nine guard towers; German officers from late 1941; National Historic Site of Canada, September 2013.
6. **Wikipedia, "Franz von Werra"**: 21 Jan. 1941 jumped from a prison train in Canada, crossed the frozen St. Lawrence to
   Ogdensburg, New York (U.S. neutral), helped over the border to Mexico, reached Germany 18 Apr. 1941.
7. **U.S. Constitution, Art. III, Sec. 3**: "Treason against the United States, shall consist only in levying War against
   them, or in adhering to their Enemies, giving them Aid and Comfort. No Person shall be convicted of Treason unless on the
   Testimony of two Witnesses to the same overt Act, or on Confession in open Court."
8. Mexico declared war on Germany on 22 May 1942 (general knowledge; Wikipedia "Mexico in World War II"), so it was neutral
   when Krug headed for it.

## Disagreements
- **Escape date:** 16 Apr. (Wikipedia) vs 17 Apr. (Court of Appeals). Script: "In the middle of April 1942".
- **Shot down:** over England, 28 Aug. 1940 (Krug's sworn testimony, Court of Appeals) vs over the Channel in 1941 (Wikipedia).
  Script follows the court.
- **Birthday:** Stephan said 18 Apr. "is your birthday today" (court); Wikipedia: the eve of his 22nd birthday. Script quotes
  the court.
- **The $20s:** whether Stephan took Bertelmann's money was not proved by two witnesses; Krug said Donay handed him the second
  $20. Script: "When the men left, it was gone" / "twenty dollars came out of Donay's cash drawer".
- **Commutation time:** nine hours (Wikipedia, citing the Free Press) vs eight hours (Sase). Script: "about nine hours".
- **"First since the Whiskey Rebellion":** Wikipedia (citing the press); one contemporary account says first in U.S. history.
  Script: "By most accounts ... the first American convicted of treason in a civilian federal court since the Whiskey
  Rebellion".
- **Route after Chicago, the hotel tip:** only Sase. Script: "According to one later account".
