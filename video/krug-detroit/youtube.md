## Description
On April 18, 1942, a Detroit restaurant owner told a young man at one of his tables that it was his birthday, and took him on a "pleasure trip": a necktie, a wallet, a traveling bag, drinks in two bars, candy, dinner, a hotel room, and the next morning a bus ticket to Chicago. At every stop he introduced his guest as "one of the Meyers boys" or "a friend from Milwaukee".

The guest was Lieutenant Hans Peter Krug, a Luftwaffe bomber pilot who had just escaped from a prisoner of war camp in Canada and rowed across the Detroit River. The restaurant owner was Max Stephan, a German-born American citizen. Four months later a federal judge sentenced Stephan to hang for treason. This is the documented story of that weekend, told from the court record: the escape, the kitchen on Phillip Avenue, the clerk who called the FBI, Krug's run toward Mexico, the trial where the escaped pilot was the main witness, and the phone call that reached the prison nine hours before the hanging.
