## Description
Manila, 9 January 1946. A master sergeant with 28 years in the U.S. Army stands with a pointer at a map of Bataan and tells five American generals what he saw on the road four years earlier: no food, no water, and bodies, "hundreds of them. God knows how many of them." Then he turns and points at the man who commanded the army that marched him: Lieutenant General Masaharu Homma.

This is the documented story of the trial of General Homma for the Bataan Death March, told from the trial record itself: the survivors who testified, Captain Pedro Felix showing the court his bayonet scars, Homma's own words on the stand ("I am morally responsible…"), his wife Fujiko's testimony, the five-minute verdict, Justice Frank Murphy's dissent, Fujiko's 40 minutes with General MacArthur, MacArthur's review ("The leader is the essence"), and the firing squad at Los Baños. With the original 1945 voice of Bataan survivor Chaplain Leslie Zimmerman. Where the sources disagree, we tell you so.
