## Description
On September 16, 1944, a group of Japanese prisoners of war sat down to lunch on Angel Island in San Francisco Bay. Every one of them expected to be tortured and killed. Lunch was sukiyaki, cabbage salad, rice and caramel pudding.

The food was not an accident. The War Department had learned that Japanese prisoners passed through three phases: terror, then gratitude, then silence. Within days, many of these men were driven to Byron Hot Springs, a former luxury resort hotel turned into one of only two secret interrogation centers in the United States. Its plans were labeled "Officers' School." Its light fixtures hid microphones. Its forty-five interrogators, many of them Japanese Americans trained in Minnesota, were each assigned to a single prisoner.

This is the documented story of Camp Tracy: the meal, the clock it started, the threat that worked better than force, and why some Japanese prisoners ended up guiding American bombers to their targets.
