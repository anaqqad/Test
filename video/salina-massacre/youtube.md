## Description
On a Saturday night in July 1945, a 23-year-old American soldier told a waitress in Salina, Utah, that something exciting was going to happen tonight. At midnight he reported for guard duty, climbed a tower, and loaded a machine gun. Below him, about 250 German prisoners of war were asleep in their tents. The war in Europe had been over for exactly two months.

In fifteen seconds he fired 250 rounds into the camp. Nine men died, and nineteen were wounded. This is the documented story of the Salina shooting, told from the 1945 newspapers of Utah: the town that woke to the screams, the doctor who worked through the night, the prisoners sent back to the beet fields on Monday, the five Germans hanged at Fort Leavenworth the same week, and the American guard who was never tried.
