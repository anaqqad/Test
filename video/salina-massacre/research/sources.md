# Sources: the Salina, Utah, prisoner of war shooting (July 8, 1945)

## Why this topic
Competitor research, 6 Nov 2026 (YouTube search by date and by views, POW niche):
- The niche is flooded with "German POWs were shocked by American kindness": 695K (Wisconsin donuts, 3 weeks), 489K (Kansas
  lumberyard, 2 weeks), 370K (Texas farmer, 7 days), 227K (Thanksgiving feast, 4 weeks). All say the same thing: America was kind.
- The documented opposite: two months after V-E Day, an American guard machine-gunned the tents of sleeping German prisoners.
  An old InRangeTV video on it has 380K views (7 years ago), so the demand is proven; the recent versions have 29 to 2,158
  views. None uses the 1945 newspapers.
- Hook: he tells a waitress something exciting is going to happen tonight, then climbs the tower.
- Not covered by our past episodes.

## Sources
1. **Utah Digital Newspapers (J. Willard Marriott Library), 71 items 1945-46, "No Copyright - United States"** (main source):
   - **Salt Lake Telegram, 9 July 1945, p. 1, "Soldier Kills 8 Nazis in Utah Prison Camp" (Clarence Williams)**: 15 seconds;
     native of New Orleans; eight months in England, never in combat; tower No. 1; camp about four blocks east of Main Street;
     three towers, one guard each; high barbed wire fence; guns kept unloaded, "after going on duty at midnight put a full
     belt load of bullets in the gun"; prisoners worked in beet fields in Sevier and Sanpete counties; a few beers, nothing to
     drink after 11 p.m. when he went to a restaurant for coffee and food; "showed no signs of remorse"; seven living brothers
     and six sisters, one brother in service in England, another in Germany; of 43 tents the three bursts pierced 30, slugs
     went through beds and wooden floors; Lt. Albert I. Cornell, on a cot at the foot of the tower: "I heard the shots and ran
     out"; "I saw no attempt at a prison camp break"; ordered him to cease firing and come down; he "shouted for more
     ammunition because he allegedly said he wasn't through yet"; Corp. Delmire O. Butts climbed the tower; men killed or
     wounded in 11 tents (Tribune; Ogden says 13), four killed in tent 14; Dr. Rae E. Noyes, credited by Col. Ericsson with
     saving lives: "a horrible mess"; six died outright, two in the Salina hospital; "One man whose body was virtually cut in
     half by the bullets lived until 6 a.m."; wounded taken on their cots; four nurses and some civilians helped; Sgt. Hans
     Fertig, the Germans' spokesman, asked that prisoners have the next two days off; "the nazis were on the job Monday because
     Col. Ericsson told the sergeant it was highly desirable that the men do their work as usual"; Fertig testified no prisoner
     had been threatened by guards and no shots fired before; the presence of machine guns had made them fear something serious
     might happen; Fertig "said he realized it was one of those things"; camp first opened 8 June 1945; born 14 Sept. 1921;
     enlisted 1940; hospitalized 12 times; England Sept. 4, 1943 to May 1944; punished three times (AWOL at Augusta; refused
     guard duty at McLean, Texas; missed a train at Memphis); sixth grade education "but that he is intelligent". Names of the
     dead and wounded.
   - **Salt Lake Telegram, 9 July, p. 1, photo "Guard Who Shot Into Camp"; "This Weapon Dealt Death to Eight"; p. 5 "Germans
     Silent on Guard's Act"; "Wounded Nazis Expected to Live"** (Klebe probably to lose his right foot; Max Brendamour left
     eye; Lampe right arm).
   - **Salt Lake Tribune, 9 July, p. 1**: population of the camp 250; the town three blocks away aroused; "prisoners and soldiers
     at the camp ... began treatment of the injured and dying".
   - **Ogden Standard-Examiner, 9 July, p. 1 (AP)**: mother Mrs. Mary Bertucci, widowed: "Something must have happened to him".
   - **Ogden Standard-Examiner, 10 July, p. 1 (UP), "Hatred for Germans Given As Reason for Slaying"**: "He just didn't like
     Germans," the colonel said. On several occasions tempted to turn the tower gun on the prisoners and "not at all sorry" for
     what he had done. Same front page: **"Five German prisoners of war who murdered a fellow prisoner in an Oklahoma internment
     camp were hanged this morning"** at Fort Leavenworth; "It was the first time that an enemy war prisoner had been executed
     in the United States"; seven reporters; gallows in an elevator shaft.
   - **Salt Lake Telegram, 10 July, p. 9**: "because he told military authorities he didn't like Germans"; solitary confinement,
     medical ward at Fort Douglas; smoking cigarettes and sleeping; "he had often been tempted to do what I did"; funeral under
     the Geneva convention; a recent war department order prohibits the swastika flag; no German flag available.
   - **Salt Lake Tribune editorial, 10 July, p. 4**: "not only deplorable but damnable"; "embarrassed his countrymen and disgraced
     the army in which he is enrolled".
   - **Salina Sun, 13 July, p. 1**: "awoke nearly every citizen"; "Fear was expressed by many that the Nazis were making a break";
     about six men to a tent; hospital rooms, hall and waiting room filled, several treated outside; "The dead were placed on the
     west lawn of the hospital grounds"; citizens kept from the hospital grounds.
   - **Salt Lake Telegram, 12 July, p. 13**: buried Thursday afternoon at Fort Douglas, Capt. Frank B. Edwards, chorus of 20 from
     Ogden (Deseret News 1995: 17), about 15 prisoners from Salina under guard, the public not allowed.
   - **Salt Lake Telegram, 16 July, p. 7**: Pfc. Friedrich Ritter, 48, died Friday afternoon at Kearns of peritonitis; 17 July:
     rites Tuesday.
   - **Salt Lake Telegram, 17 Aug., p. 13, "Killer of Nazi Prisoners Found Insane by Army"**: "had told of planning the shootings
     in advance". **Salt Lake Tribune / Ogden, 23 Aug.**: "mentally unbalanced", to Mason General Hospital, Brentwood, N.Y.
     **Salt Lake Tribune, 22 July**: "had it on his mind" for a long time.
   - **Salt Lake Tribune, 24 May 1946, p. 9**: prisoners again in the fields around Salina in 1946.
2. **Time, 23 July 1945, "Midnight Massacre"**: "he had hated Germans, so he had killed Germans"; two courts-martial.
3. **Wikipedia, "1945 Utah prisoner of war massacre"** (citing Powell, *Splinters of a Nation*, 1989; Carlson, *We Were Each
   Other's Prisoners*, 1998; Harris thesis, 2009): names and ages of the dead; "Someday I will get my Germans"; Capt. Wayne Owens
   of the Ogden camp concluded he was sane, recommended a court-martial, was told he had no authority to judge sanity; telegrams
   of sympathy for Bertucci; the wounded barred from American compensation; 1988 German Air Force refurbished the memorial, two
   men wounded in 1945 attended; died 2 Dec 1969.
4. **HistoryNet, "The Deadliest Nazi POW Massacre On US Soil Took Place After WWII Ended"**: prisoners arrived June 7, 1945, from
   Arizona; Rodney Rasmussen, then six, 300 feet away: "We came out on the front porch and we could hear the cries of the
   prisoners."; coffee at Mom's Cafe; southwestern tower; mattresses on neighboring lawns; the town's lone doctor and a pair of
   nurses; museum: restored guard barracks, motor pool garage, tent pegs, a prisoner-made matchstick jewelry box; the mess hall
   survives under later siding.
5. **Eileen Hallet Stone, Salt Lake Tribune, 7 Nov 2016**: a full day in the beet fields on July 7, waiting to be shipped home;
   violated the 1929 Geneva Convention; "the worst massacre at a POW camp in U.S. history".
6. **Christian Flanders, "The P.O.W. Camp at Salina, Utah," Intermountain Histories**: families not informed until 1948; a camp
   unusual for being inside a town; guards were men judged unfit for the front.
7. **Nicholas Demas, "The Salina POW Camp Shooting," Utah Humanities (2008)**: 12:25 a.m.; on duty less than 30 minutes; three
   bursts; prisoners from Ogden bought wreaths; "Good Comrade".
8. **Twila Van Leer, Deseret News, 4 July 1995**: American khaki, caskets like those of U.S. casualties; Taps; Dieter Lampe, wounded,
   returned to Salina in September 1977, "received excellent care", left strongly opposed to war.
9. **Fox 13, 23 Oct 2016**: Dee Olsen, Tami Olsen Clark: "History is not pretty ..."; the Salina mayor did not learn of it until
   after he was elected.
10. **Lowell A. Bangerter, "German Prisoners of War in Wyoming," Journal of German-American Studies 14:2 (1979)**: summary of the
    shooting (how this story was found).

## Disagreements
- **The death toll**: first reports 8 dead, 20 wounded; Ritter died later: 9 dead, 19 wounded (Telegram 17 Aug). Tribune 1946 says 8 and 24.
- **When Ritter died**: Telegram: Friday (July 13); Wikipedia July 14 / "five days later". Script: "days later".
- **The gun**: Wikipedia M1917; Flanders M1919; newspapers ".30 caliber air-cooled". Script: "a thirty caliber machine gun".
- **The time**: "shortly after midnight"; Demas 12:25; Lt. Cornell "a few minutes after midnight and 12:30". Script: "about half an hour after midnight"? No: "shortly after midnight".
- **Beer or coffee**: Telegram: a few beers, nothing after 11, then coffee and food; Time "drank in town". Army: not intoxicated. Script: both.
- **"Someday I will get my Germans"**: reported later, unverified. Script: "people in town said later".
- **The name of the café**: Mom's Cafe (HistoryNet, Fox 13); newspapers "a restaurant".
- **Flags at the funeral**: Telegram: none available; Deseret News 12 July: caskets draped with the German national flag;
  Wikipedia: no flags. Script leaves flags out.
- **Afrika Korps or not**: Wikipedia "mostly Afrika Korps"; Tribune 2016 "rumored". Script: "many of them".
- **His later life**: Flanders: in an institution until his death; KSUB: later released; HistoryNet: "much of the rest of his life".
  Script: "He spent much of the rest of his life in mental hospitals".
- **Rank**: Private (most); Pfc (AP, Salina Sun).
- **The doctor**: "lone doctor" (HistoryNet); "doctors from surrounding communities" came (Salina Sun). Script: "the town's doctor".
- **Tower**: No. 1, above the main entrance (Telegram); "southwestern" (HistoryNet). Script: "the tower".
- **Choir**: 20 (Telegram), 17 (Deseret 1995, Demas). Script: "a choir".
