## Description
Luzon, 30 January 1945, twenty minutes to seven in the evening. A P-61 Black Widow night fighter drops to a few hundred feet over the Japanese prison camp at Cabanatuan, and every guard looks up. In the field in front of the gate, more than a hundred Rangers of the 6th Ranger Battalion are crawling the last of a mile of open ground. At 7:45 p.m. Lt. John Murphy fires the first shot.

This is the documented story of the raid at Cabanatuan, "The Great Raid": Lt. Col. Henry Mucci and Capt. Robert Prince's 6th Rangers, the Alamo Scouts of Lts. William Nellist and Tom Rounsaville, and the Filipino guerrillas of Capts. Juan Pajota and Eduardo Joson, who went 25 miles behind Japanese lines to bring out more than 500 survivors of Bataan and Corregidor. Told from the Army's own study of the raid, the War Department's 1945 release, the Distinguished Service Cross citations, the Ranger log and the newspapers of February 1945, with the original voices of the freed men, filmed weeks later. Where the sources disagree, we tell you so.
