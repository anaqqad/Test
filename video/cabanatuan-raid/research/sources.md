# Sources: The Raid at Cabanatuan ("The Great Raid"), Pangatian, Nueva Ecija, Luzon, 30 January 1945

Research date: 21 Nov 2026. Free sources only. "No source, no line."

Reliability key used below: **[P]** primary/official record or eyewitness, **[S]** solid secondary (government history,
archival study), **[W]** weak or derivative (wiki, pop-history, local press repeating others), **[1x]** found in only one
source, **[M]** comes from one memoir/oral history/later interview (say so in the script: "he later recalled…").

How the sources were read:
- **Michael J. King, Leavenworth Paper No. 11 [1]** was read in full (chapter 6 and its notes) as the HyperWar HTML text.
  King wrote from the 6th Ranger Battalion's own files at Suitland (its report "Ranger Mission at the Pangatian Prison
  Camp", the battalion narrative, Sixth Army G-2 Weekly Report No. 74) plus Johnson's *Hour of Redemption*. It is the
  backbone of this file: follow King for clock times unless noted.
- **Krivdo, *Veritas* 14:2 (2018) [8]** was read in full as the USASOC PDF (text layer). It quotes Mucci's two 1945
  articles (*Saturday Evening Post*, 7 Apr 1945; *Infantry Journal*, Apr 1945), which are not online; those quotes are
  marked "Mucci, 1945, via [8]".
- **McRaven's 1993 NPS thesis [9]** (U.S. government work) was read in its Cabanatuan chapter; it quotes the 6th Ranger
  Battalion Operation Report and log and 1992-93 interviews with Prince, Nellist, Harris and Ott.
- **1945 newspapers [13]-[15]** were read as archive.org OCR full texts. OCR is rough; quotes were checked in context.
- **War Department release [4]** and the two **DSC citations [2]-[3]** were read in full.
- Robert Ross Smith, *Triumph in the Philippines* (CMH), chapters X-XIV were checked on HyperWar: **the raid is not
  narrated there** (no "Ranger", "Mucci" or "Pangatian" in those chapters). Not used.
- Hampton Sides, *Ghost Soldiers*, Breuer, Rottman, Alexander, Zedric: used **only through Wikipedia's footnotes [17]**
  and marked [W]. Do not quote them on screen.

## Editor's notes (21 Nov 2026)

- **POW count: say "more than 500" in narration**, and if one number is needed on a card, use **511** (King [1], the
  War Department release [4], the 1945 AP story by Hampson [14]) with a note that later roster work gives 516 [8]. The
  522 in Wikipedia [17] counts civilians separately (Rottman). Avoid 513 except when showing the 1945 AP front page,
  where it is the printed figure. See Disagreements 1.
- **Opening shot 19:45**, fired by 1st Lt. John F. Murphy, 2d Platoon, F Company, into a barracks window [1] [8].
  Wikipedia's 19:40 [17] is from Breuer; the 1945 War Department release has a sergeant saying Prince's red flare was
  the signal [4] (contradicts everyone else; do not use).
- **Sweezy**: do not say "friendly fire" as fact. King says he was hit by an automatic weapon from the compound [1];
  Sides (via [17]) says friendly fire; *Veritas* says mortars and misspells him "Sweeny" [8]. Use: "Corporal Roy Sweezy
  was shot as the last Rangers pulled back; he died within minutes" and attribute any cause.
- **Edwin Rose** (the man left behind): King and McRaven call him a dysentery-weakened British *civilian* hidden in the
  latrine, found after midnight by guerrillas [1] [9]; *Veritas* calls him a deaf British *soldier* recovered next
  morning by Team Nellist [8]; Wikipedia (Sides) has the shave-and-best-clothes story [17] [W]. Tell it as "a British
  prisoner, sick and nearly deaf" and attribute the shaving detail to "one account".
- **Guerrilla losses**: sources range from "no fatalities or serious wounds" [1] to "26 guerrillas killed" [8]. Say
  "Pajota's guerrillas held the bridge" and do not put a guerrilla death toll on screen.
- **Best primary on-screen material**: the two DSC citations (GO No. 39, 21 Feb 1945) [2] [3]; the War Department
  release with Prince's own words [4]; the AP and INS front pages of 1-2 Feb 1945 [13]-[15]; King's Maps 5 and 6 [1];
  NARA photo 531251 [11]; the 1946 Navy "Prisoner of War Photographs: Cabanatuan" album [12].
- **Copyright caution**: *Veritas* [8] and King [1] are U.S. government works; 1945 newspapers on archive.org: show
  headline crops and one short quote each; Life (26 Feb 1945) is not public domain: skip. The National WWII Museum
  Prince oral history [30] is licensable, not free.

## Sources

### PART A: OFFICIAL HISTORIES, ORDERS, GOVERNMENT RECORDS

[1] **Michael J. King, *Rangers: Selected Combat Operations in World War II*, Leavenworth Papers No. 11 (Fort
Leavenworth: Combat Studies Institute, U.S. Army Command and General Staff College, June 1985), chapter 6
"Cabanatuan", pp. ~55-71, with Maps 5 and 6.** U.S. government work. **[S/P-based]**
URL (read): https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/CSI/CSI-Rangers/Lvn_Rangers-WWII.html (section "#CA")
Also: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA531336 (DTIC scan with page numbers and maps).
King's sources for this chapter: 6th Ranger Bn, "History of King II Operation"; letter "Ranger Mission at the Pangatian
Prison Camp," HQ 6th Ranger Bn to Sixth Army G-3, SF-INBN 72-37, roll 6, frames 236-40; "Narrative of the Sixth Ranger
Infantry Battalion from January 2, 1945, to July 1, 1945"; Sixth Army G-2 Weekly Report No. 74 (31 Jan-7 Feb 1945);
message Col. White to Col. Hutchinson, 31 Jan 1945; Johnson, *Hour of Redemption* (1978).
- Opening: "The rescue of 511 American and Allied prisoners from a Japanese POW compound near Cabanatuan … was the most
  complex operation that Rangers conducted during World War II. It was also one of the most successful."
- Unit: 6th Ranger Bn grew from the 98th Field Artillery Bn (activated Ft. Lewis, Jan 1941); Mucci, "an aggressive 1936
  West Point graduate", took command at Port Moresby in April 1944; redesignated 6th Ranger Infantry Battalion on
  25 September 1944; landed on Dinagat, Guiuan and Homonhan on 17 October 1944, three days before the Leyte landing.
- Camp location: "a compound at Pangatian, five miles east of Cabanatuan." Lapham's runners kept him informed.
  "The POW compound at Pangatian did double duty as a transit camp."
- Mission given on the recommendation of Sixth Army G-2 Col. Horton White. Force: Mucci; Company C (Capt. Robert W.
  Prince); 2d Platoon, Company F (1st Lt. John F. Murphy); two Alamo Scout teams; four combat photographers of the
  832d Signal Service Battalion. "The total strength of Mucci's force was 8 officers and 120 enlisted men."
- Alamo Scout teams had together freed "thirty-two Javanese civilians" at Moari, New Guinea, Oct 1944 (Nellist later said
  66; see [9]).
- Rangers carried an SCR 694 radio; wore "soft caps and fatigue uniforms with no insignias or badges of rank"; Mucci
  "was armed with only a .45-caliber pistol"; each man carried a trench knife, at least two bandoliers, two grenades.
- 28 Jan: left base camp (Calasiao) by truck at 0500, halted at Guimba, left on foot at 1400 for Lobong; Capt. Eduardo
  Joson joined with 80 men (left 20 guarding Lobong); crossed into enemy territory south of Baloc after dark, forded the
  Talavera River at midnight, crossed the Rizal highway at 0400, arrived Balincarin 0600 on 29 Jan: "the
  fourteen-mile march from Lobong without incident."
- Balincarin: met Lts. Thomas Rounsaville and William Nellist and Capt. Juan Pajota ("approximately 90 armed and 160
  unarmed men"). 200-300 Japanese bivouacked on Cabu Creek a mile north; "at least one Japanese division was at
  Cabanatuan City less than four miles to the south." Mucci "decided to postpone the raid for twenty-four hours."
- Guerrillas to assemble "a carabao-cart train large enough to carry 200 liberated POWs, and prepare food for 650 men";
  civilians told to "pen all chickens and tie and muzzle all dogs."
- Camp: south side of the Cabanatuan City-Cabu highway, "measured 600 by 800 yards", three barbed-wire fences "six to
  eight feet high"; main gate eight feet high "secured with a heavy lock"; three twelve-foot guard towers and one pillbox
  with four men. "Although only seventy-three Japanese were on guard at the stockade, about one hundred and fifty
  additional troops had entered the compound at 1100"; "about eight hundred Japanese with tanks and trucks at Cabu";
  prisoners in the northwest corner.
- Plan: Joson's roadblock "800 yards southwest of the compound", with a six-man bazooka team under S/Sgt. James O.
  White; Pajota's roadblock "at the highway bridge over Cabu Creek 300 yards northeast of the compound".
- **The plane was Pajota's idea**: "On Pajota's recommendation, an American airplane would buzz the compound just
  prior to the attack."
- Signals: Prince to fire "a red flare" to withdraw the Rangers; a second flare a mile out for the guerrillas.
- 30 Jan: left Platero 1700; "The entire force under Mucci's command numbered nearly 375." Pajota had secretly sent "an
  additional 400 guerrillas ahead" with "four water-cooled .30-caliber machine guns" (Squadrons 200, 202, 201A, 204).
- Time bomb under the far end of the Cabu bridge, "delivered by an American submarine, was set to detonate between
  1940 and 1950 hours."
- 1800 twilight: open plain "for more than a mile"; Prince's platoons crawled: "It was a mile away, and the Rangers
  would have to crawl for seventy-five minutes to reach it."
- **P-61: "At 1840 … a single P61 Black Widow from the 547th Night Fighter Squadron … flew over the bridge and prison
  compound twice at an altitude of 200 feet."**
- Murphy's men crept "under the highway through a large culvert"; a tower sentry raised his rifle toward the ditch then
  lowered it; last men in position "at 1925"; Murphy's checks "delayed the attack fifteen minutes."
- "The moonlight was bright"; Rangers aimed "at the red glow of cigarettes" and men "in their underwear"; guards "a
  polyglot assortment of Japanese, Koreans, and Formosans."
- **"At 1945, Murphy aimed his M-1 rifle at an open window in the nearest barrack and fired. His shot was the signal."**
- "All guard towers, guard shacks, and pillboxes were neutralized within thirty seconds"; S/Sgt. Theodore R. Richardson
  "shattered the lock with a shot from his .45-caliber pistol"; S/Sgt. Manton P. Stewart's section destroyed "two trucks
  and a corrugated metal tank shed with bazooka fire."
- Bridge: guerrillas fired on "a Japanese battalion in bivouac less than 300 yards beyond Cabu Creek"; the bomb "blew a
  gap in the bridge"; bazookamen with Pajota knocked out "two Japanese tanks and one truck."
- PFC Leland A. Provencher "liberated the first POW", a generator operator.
- After about twelve minutes resistance dwindled; "a Japanese light mortar fired three rounds toward the front gate and
  wounded six men. Alamo Scout Rounsaville and battalion surgeon Captain James C. Fisher were among the casualties."
- Shortly after 2000 a P-61 "strafed and destroyed a Japanese convoy" heading toward Joson's roadblock.
- **2015: Prince "fired one red flare into the sky to begin the withdrawal."** A "dysentery-weakened British civilian
  prisoner had hidden in the latrine … He would be discovered near the camp after midnight by Filipino guerrillas";
  "one POW had died of an apparent heart attack while being helped out of the compound."
- **Sweezy**: "When Corporal Roy Sweezy turned to fire his M-1 at the Japanese, he was shot through the chest with an
  automatic weapon and died several minutes later. He and the fatally wounded Captain Fisher were the only Rangers to
  die in the operation."
- 2030 most at the Pampanga River; 2045 all across, second flare; Joson withdrew at once; Pajota's fight "continued until
  shortly after 2200"; "His guerrillas had virtually destroyed a Japanese battalion without suffering any fatalities or
  serious wounds."
- Platero: guerrilla doctor Carlos Layug; first walkers left at 2100. Balincarin: 15 carts added to 25. "Captain Fisher
  was left in Balincarin with thirteen Alamo Scouts and Rangers … a light aircraft was requested … The aircraft would
  never arrive." Left at midnight for Matoas Na Kahey (arr. 0200, 31 Jan, 11 more carts); "When the column left at 0230,
  it had fifty-one carts and was a mile and a half long."
- Rizal highway: had to march "one mile south" on it; roadblocks 400 yards NE and 3,000 yards S; cleared "by 0430";
  halted 0530; reached Sibul 0800 (20 more carts); radio contact with Guimba; resumed after 0900; ~1100 T/5 Patrick
  Marquis met a Sixth Army patrol; "an hour later, the former POWs were at the 92d Evacuation Hospital in Guimba."
- Commentary: "At a cost of two Rangers killed … liberated 511 American and Allied POWs and killed or wounded an
  estimated 523 Japanese." MacArthur said the raid was "magnificent and reflect[ed] extraordinary credit to all
  concerned"; DSC to Mucci, Silver Star to all American officers, Bronze Star to all American enlisted men; "All Filipino
  officers and enlisted men were awarded the Bronze Star."
- Sixth Army weekly G-2 report: "an almost perfect example of prior reconnaissance and planning..." and "what patrols can
  accomplish in enemy territory by following the basic principles of scouting and patrolling, 'sneaking and peeping'…"
- King's own verdict: success "would have been unlikely without Filipino friendship and support, and impossible had the
  Filipinos sympathized with the Japanese."
- After Cabanatuan the battalion's records show "only one Ranger killed in action, one dead of wounds, and three wounded."

## Timeline

**Background**
- 9 Apr 1942: Bataan surrenders; 6 May 1942: Corregidor [8] [17].
- 1942: survivors moved from Camp O'Donnell to Cabanatuan (Pangatian); deaths "40 to 50 daily" [6]; "16 January 1943
  … the first day in the history of the camp without a death" [6].
- 26 Oct 1942: 1,000 POWs entrained from Cabanatuan Camp 1 for Davao [6].
- By Dec 1943: "2650 Americans were buried in the camp's cemetery" [9].
- Oct 1944: about 1,600 prisoners moved out of Cabanatuan [13] [17]; 13 Dec 1944: 1,619 POWs loaded on the Oryoku Maru
  in Manila [6].
- 17 Oct 1944: 6th Rangers land on Dinagat/Guiuan/Homonhan [1]; 20 Oct: Leyte landing [1] [17].
- 14 Dec 1944: Palawan massacre, 139 killed, 11 escaped [10]; 7 Jan 1945: survivor PFC Eugene Nielsen debriefed by Army
  intelligence [17] [W].
- ~5-6 Jan 1945: original Japanese garrison leaves camp; Col. James Duckworth left in charge [13] [17].
- 9 Jan 1945: Sixth Army lands at Lingayen Gulf [9] [17].
- 26 Jan 1945: 6th Infantry Division takes Guimba [1]; Lapham proposes the rescue; Krueger assigns Col. White and Maj.
  Rowale to plan [8] [17].

- Mucci and Prince briefed at Sixth Army HQ (Dagupan) by Col. White, with Lapham and Lts. Dove, Nellist, Rounsaville

## Disagreements

1. **How many prisoners were freed.** Ranger log 31 Jan: 510 [5]; King, WD release, AP Hampson, Rounsaville HOF, 2025
   Army.mil: 511 [1] [4] [14] [23] [26]; McRaven: 512 (incl. Rose) [9]; AP McDaniel and Dixon front page 1945: 513 [13]
   [15]; *Veritas* (rosters): 516 [8]; Wikipedia (Rottman, incl. civilians): 522 [17]; wwiirangers.org: 571 (error)
   [20]. **Follow 511 for official/period consistency, or "more than 500" in narration;** mention 516 as the later roster
   count if a card needs precision. The 1945 headline number 513 can appear on the newspaper itself.
2. **Size of the raid force.** King: Mucci's force 128 Americans, whole force "nearly 375" [1]; INS 407 [14]; WD
   121 + ~280 [4]; AP "some 400" [13]. **Follow King** ("about 120 Rangers, a dozen Alamo Scouts and some 250 Filipino
   guerrillas at the camp, with hundreds more unseen in reserve").
3. **What signalled the attack.** Murphy's rifle shot at 1945 [1] [8]; WD release (S/Sgt. Brown): Prince's red flare
   [4]. **Follow King/Veritas**: the red flare was the withdrawal signal at 2015.
4. **Time and manner of the P-61 pass.** King/McRaven: 1840, twice at 200 ft [1] [9]; *Veritas* timeline: "1935 P-61
   aerobatics begin", ~500 ft per Prince [8]; Wikipedia: took off 1800, "about 45 minutes before the attack", 1,500 ft
   down to 200 ft, twenty minutes [17]. **Follow King** for the time; say "low" not a single altitude.
5. **Opening time.** 1945 [1] [8] [14] vs 19:40 [17]. **Follow 1945** (King, Veritas, 1945 INS).
6. **Radio contact.** Wikipedia: Sixth Army got a success message at 23:00 on 30 Jan [17]; King: no contact with Guimba
   "since before the raid" until about dawn, contact made at Sibul ~0800 [1]. **Follow King.**
7. **How Sweezy died.** Automatic-weapon fire from the compound, "through the chest" [1]; "struck in the back by two
   rounds" [9]; friendly fire (Sides via [17]); the mortar rounds, as "Sweeny" [8]. **Follow King's wording; attribute
   the friendly-fire claim** if used.
8. **P-61 crew spelling.** Schrieber/Rucks (Prince in 2006 [29]; Wikipedia [17]) vs Schreiber/Bucks [8]. **Use
   "Capt. Kenneth Schrieber and Lt. Bonnie Rucks"** (Prince's own spelling), and note the variance in the research log.
9. **Who left behind / found the last prisoner (Edwin Rose).** British civilian, found after midnight by guerrillas [1]
   [9]; deaf British soldier, recovered next morning by Team Nellist [8]; found by passing guerrillas after shaving and
   dressing (Sides via [17]). **Say "a British prisoner"**, found by Filipinos; keep the shaving detail attributed.
10. **Carts.** 71 [8], derived from King's counts [1]; "106" [17]; "over one hundred … by some estimates" [7].
    **Follow 71** (Mucci's own counts via [8]).
11. **Japanese killed.** 523 killed or wounded (estimate) [1]; 523 killed (AP 1945) [13]; 250 + 300 (Ranger log) [5];
    ~500 [4]; at least 530, possibly over 1,000 [8] [17]. **Say "hundreds of Japanese soldiers, by Army estimates more
    than 500, killed or wounded."**
12. **Pajota's death.** 1976 [21] [22] vs Chicago Tribune story dated 29 Dec 1977 cited for it [22]. **Say "in the
    1970s, in Chicago, days before he was to become an American citizen."**
13. **MacArthur's words.** 1945 AP text: "No incident of the campaign has given me such personal satisfaction." [13];
    Wikipedia (O'Donnell 2003): "No incident of the campaign in the Pacific has given me such satisfaction as the release
    of the POWs at Cabanatuan. The mission was brilliantly successful." [17]; King: "magnificent and reflect[ed]
    extraordinary credit to all concerned" [1]. **Use the 1945 AP version.**
14. **Guerrilla losses.** See Numbers. **Do not state a number.**
15. **Who freed the camp, in 1945 headlines.** AP 1945 gave Maj. Lapham's guerrilla unit a central role and "27 Rangers
    and Filipinos" dead [13]; wrong per [1] [5]. Useful as a "first reports" beat; correct it on screen.
16. **Did Palawan trigger the raid?** Wikipedia (Sides) links it [17]; Krivdo's Palawan article does not say so [10];
    the DSC citation gives the motive as "It was believed that the enemy would kill or remove the prisoners" [2].
    **Use the DSC wording as the motive; Palawan as context ("Sixth Army knew what had happened on Palawan" is NOT
    sourced; avoid).**
17. **Murphy's rank.** 1st Lt. [1] vs 2nd Lt. [8]. Follow King.
18. **Distance from Cabanatuan City.** "five miles east" [1] [9] vs 4.5 miles NE (ABMC) [26]. Say "about five miles".
19. **How many met FDR.** Ten Rangers and two Scouts [8 text] vs twelve Rangers and two Scouts [8 endnote 77]; WD tour
    had twelve men (ten Rangers, two Scouts named) [4]. Say "a dozen of the raiders".
