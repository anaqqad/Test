## Description
On September 7, 1945, the Catholic bishop of Amarillo sat down to lunch in a Texas prisoner of war camp and ate what the prisoners ate: watery soup with no salt, a dry herring, bread and water. The Italian prisoners at Camp Hereford had been hungry for months. They were catching rattlesnakes, frying grasshoppers in hair oil, and eating the camp's dogs and cats.

This is the documented story of "la fame", the hunger: how prisoners who had once been fed like American soldiers saw their rations cut after V-E Day, how one camp commander went far beyond his orders, how a bishop's letter forced an investigation, and how a sergeant smuggled jackrabbits past his own guards. And it is the story of St. Mary's Church in Umbarger, Texas, where a few of those prisoners painted the walls in exchange for the parish women's dinners. The paintings are still there.
