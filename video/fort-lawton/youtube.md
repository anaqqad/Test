## Description
At six in the morning on August 15, 1944, two military policemen at Fort Lawton, an Army post in Seattle, found an Italian prisoner of war hanging from a cable on the obstacle course. His name was Guglielmo Olivotto. Hours earlier, a riot had broken out between Black American soldiers about to ship out to the Pacific and the Italian prisoners who lived next door.

The Army put 43 Black soldiers on trial, the largest and longest Army court-martial of the Second World War. Two defense lawyers had about ten days to prepare. The prosecutor, Leon Jaworski, later the Watergate special prosecutor, kept the Inspector General's report from the defense. Twenty-eight men were convicted. In 2007 the Army ruled that the trial had been "fundamentally unfair". By then, only two of them were still alive. This is the documented story, told from the Army board's decision, the bill that reopened the case and the Congressional Record.
