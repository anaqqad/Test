## Description
371,683. That is how many German prisoners of war the United States held in May 1945, and most of them crossed America the same way: by train, looking out of a window. They had been told American cities were in ruins. In 1944, an Iowa newspaper headline said German prisoners believed New York was being bombed every day.

This is the documented story of what they saw: the Liberty ships and the Queen Mary, the upholstered coaches at Norfolk and the porter with the coffee, the automobiles everywhere, the paper plates thrown from the windows, the crowds on the platforms who came "to see if we had horns," the hundred-hour ride to Colorado, and the Black American soldiers who were sent to eat in the kitchen while German prisoners were served at the tables.
