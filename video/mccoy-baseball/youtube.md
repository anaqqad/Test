## Description
In December 1941, Ensign Kazuo Sakamaki woke up on a beach in Hawaii with an American rifle pointed at his face. He was the first Japanese prisoner of war taken by the United States, and the first thing he asked for was permission to kill himself.

Japan's military field code told every soldier that capture meant shame, and that he should die rather than be taken prisoner. So what happened to the few thousand who were taken anyway? Many of them ended up at Camp McCoy, Wisconsin, the largest camp for Japanese prisoners in the United States. There they found steamed rice, theatre, paper toys and a baseball league.

This is their documented story: the code, the shame, the fourteen escapes that all failed, the man who walked toward "Mexico" on the day Japan surrendered, and the fear of mass suicide that never came true.
