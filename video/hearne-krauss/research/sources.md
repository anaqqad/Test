# Sources: Hugo Krauss and Camp Hearne, Texas (1943–1949)

## Why this topic
Competitor research, 5 Nov 2026 (YouTube search sorted by views and by date, POW niche):
- The format rising this week is "German POWs met an American who spoke their language": "German POWs Were Told Americans
  Hated Germans. Then a Texas Farmer Answered in German." (338K views in 6 days); "German POWs Mocked Their American
  Guards—Then They Heard Perfect German!" (269K, 2 weeks); "German POWs in Wisconsin Got Donuts at Midnight. One Cried When
  America Sent Him Home." (686K, 3 weeks). Most are dramatized with invented dialogue.
- Documented version, turned around: the prisoner was the one who spoke perfect English. Hugo Krauss grew up in New York,
  came back to America as a German POW, said he wanted to stay, and was beaten to death by fellow prisoners at Camp Hearne,
  Texas. Not covered by our past episodes (Fort Hunt, generals, Hereford, Krug, Whitewater…).
- Hook: his parents came from New York to visit their son, a corporal of the German army, in a Texas prison camp.

## Sources
1. **Michael R. Waters et al., *Lone Star Stalag: German Prisoners of War at Camp Hearne* (Texas A&M University Press,
   2004), ch. "Problems at the Camp", pp. 121–134** (main source; Google Books preview read 5 Nov 2026). Built from Army
   records, the court-martial record (Dickson, Oliver and Boyle 1946), affidavits of Meisel, Hossann, Von der Heydt and
   Schiller (1945), Krauss's medical records (Hallaran 1943), and interviews.
   - Krauss went to school in New York City and returned to Germany in 1939, "ironically, as a Nazi supporter"; a friend in
     the German-American Bund arranged his transport; lived with his grandfather; drafted, released when he claimed to be an
     American citizen; later volunteered; served in Russia and North Africa; captured April 1943.
   - "Around 11:45 in the evening on December 17, after lights out, six to ten men entered Krauss's barrack and beat him
     with clubs, nail-studded boards, and a lead pipe." Skull fractured in three places, left forearm snapped, bones broken
     in both hands, right arm broken at the elbow. "Krauss yelled for help during the attack, but no one intervened."
   - Spoke English, worked as an interpreter, criticized the German government, praised the United States, told prisoners
     "he was going to get a Christmas furlough to visit his parents in New York and expected to arrange things so he could
     join the United States Army." Parents sent him a radio; he listened to American news. He left after meals when his
     company sang German songs.
   - The Nazis believed he had informed on NCOs behind work stoppages (they were transferred) and that he had revealed a
     hidden shortwave radio, which the Americans seized.
   - Heino Erichsen (same barrack, interpreter): "There was a lot of noise and the lights went out and there was a scuffle
     and screaming and beating, and it didn't take that long…" Erichsen: Krauss was a tailor; on a visit his parents gave
     him a sewing machine; he made custom tailor uniforms, shirts and hats; "I knew there was antagonism because he had
     American relatives who visited him in camp." Erichsen asked for a transfer the next day.
   - Guard Ken Johns: Krauss's mother and sister came to visit; "he ended up a man without the country." Guards later told
     of torture (sharpened fingers, a razor); Waters: "the medical report and autopsy photographs show that this did not
     actually happen—demonstrating the ability of rumors to distort the truth."
   - Investigation went nowhere "because of 'a complete lack of evidence until about mid-year 1945.'"
   - Father Henry Krauss wrote: "As a citizen of the United States, I would like a further investigation made and all
     details sent to me." (Krauss 1943). Army reply after the "exhaustive investigation" (forty-nine witnesses): "your
     son's attitude and conduct, praiseworthy from an American viewpoint, incurred for him the enmity of other German
     prisoners of war and was undoubtedly the underlying basis for the attack on him." (Rogers 1944). Assistant provost
     marshal general: Krauss "was very much disliked and was considered a traitor to Germany, especially by the younger
     prisoners of war who were devout Hitlerites." (Bryan 1944).
   - Meisel (photo, fig. 30, Camp Hearne Collection, Texas A&M): Hitler Youth member; worked in the truck workshops, drove a
     truck between camps; stopped for a young woman whose car hood was open, fixed it, asked to write; admitted he was a
     prisoner; she was a Christian and offered to send him a Bible; he had his work switched to cotton picking so he could
     visit her on the farm; confessed to her in the cotton field; she prayed with him and told him he must confess. His
     words: "A few days later I made a full statement to the officer in charge of the labor camp. He shook his head in
     disbelief. He asked, did I realize what I was saying and doing to myself? [And I responded that,] Yes, I knew."
     Crime confessed "eighteen months earlier".
   - Meisel's affidavit: the meeting in Barrack 4; "We all carried our sticks and went to Barracks 1"; lookouts; the light
     switch; "The attack on Krauss lasted less than a minute"; Krauss cried out "Meyer, cut it out, I am bleeding"; Meisel
     smashed the radio. Earlier, Krauss had said: "Let them come. I am not afraid." Schiller: Boehmer told the company "We
     have a man in our ranks who is disloyal to the cause".
   - Krauss transferred to McCloskey General Hospital, Temple, Texas, on 18 Dec 1943; died on the twenty-third, "thirty days
     short of his twenty-fifth birthday".
   - Court-martial at Camp Swift, Bastrop, Texas, 22–30 Jan 1946; seven accused (Boehmer, Braun, Hossann, Von der Heydt,
     Jaschko, Meyer, Meisel). Prosecution: "There are two of the accused who have been of assistance to me in the breaking of
     this case." After "more than two hours" all seven guilty, life imprisonment at hard labor. Leavenworth. Review board,
     Dec 1946: Jaschko and Meyer's findings disapproved; Meisel 15 years, the other four 10 years. 1948: President Truman
     reduced Meisel's sentence to 10 years. 1949: all five paroled and returned to Germany "after only three years in
     prison".
   - Krauss buried in the Camp Hearne cemetery in 1943, later moved to the National Cemetery at Fort Sam Houston, San
     Antonio, "a separate section of the cemetery with other German POWs."
2. **William Geroux, *The Fifteen: Murder, Retribution, and the Forgotten Story of Nazi POWs in America* (Random House,
   2026), pp. 69–70, 270–271** (Google Books preview). Camp Hearne 65 miles southeast of Waco, Brazos River bottomland;
   Hearne population 3,500, two cotton gins, two railroad lines. Krauss 24; beaten with fists, clubs and a lead pipe; some
   prisoners helped him walk to the camp hospital; coma; died six days later. Born in Germany, immigrated to New York at
   nine; family settled in Yorkville. 1939, at nineteen, the German American Bund paid his way to visit relatives; drafted
   after the invasion of Poland; first avoided service claiming US citizenship; then enlisted; "It's unclear what was going
   on in the young man's head at that point." Russia, then the Afrika Korps at Tunis; "Less than four years after leaving
   the United States, he was back as a German POW." Parents "visited him at Camp Hearne and bought him a radio." Talked of
   a Christmas furlough and joining the U.S. Army. A week before, he tipped off guards about sergeants orchestrating a work
   slowdown. Investigators questioned forty-five prisoners, no suspect. Dec 1945: Meisel, a trustee allowed to drive
   between bases, confessed; met a young woman by the road with her car's hood open; first said he was a long-haul trucker;
   she offered him a Bible; he contacted an American officer at Camp Fannin. Sources (p. 309): Camp Hearne Historic Site
   documents, court-martial testimony, *New York Times*, 16 Jan 1945, "Ex-Yorkville Man Slain as Prisoner".
3. **Wikipedia, "Camp Hearne"** (citing Krammer, *Nazi Prisoners of War in America*, 1979, and the *Lubbock
   Avalanche-Journal*, 31 Jan 1946): one of the few US camps to hold prisoners of all three Axis powers; central POW postal
   unit; waist-high castle replicas; "Fritz Ritz"; site sold off and abandoned about 70 years; replica barracks museum;
   Meisel's sentence cut to 15 years, the others to 10; released 1949 under an order from President Truman.
4. **Rachael Larkin, "Hearne Prisoner of War Camp", East Texas History (Stephen F. Austin State Univ.)**: first Afrika
   Korps prisoners 3 June 1943; about 4,800 prisoners; theaters for about 200; castle replica; cotton picking; Krauss made
   interpreter; "cracked Krauss's skull, broke both arms"; died six days later; Meisel and four others went to prison;
   paroled by Truman in 1949.
5. **Wikipedia, "German prisoners of war in the United States"**: 425,000 German prisoners, 700 camps; at least $0.80 a day;
   "The U.S. military executed 14 Germans after the war for murdering three fellow prisoners. Eight others served time in
   prison in two additional murders"; secret kangaroo courts, the "Holy Ghost".
6. **KBTX, 10 Dec 2024, Camp Hearne ghost walk**: Melissa Freeman, program director: "He was done with Nazi Germany."
   The walk is held each December near the anniversary.
7. **Tex Appeal, May 2023, "Camp Hearne's History"**: the camp covered 711 acres; site today 67 acres; three compounds and
   a hospital; officers built fountains and a theater.
8. **S. R. O'Konski, ww2history.org, "Fort Sam Houston National Cemetery (Part 2)"** (weaker, a personal history site):
   Krauss's tombstone at Fort Sam Houston; the author found "three pennies and a dime" left on it. Told as "one visitor".

## Disagreements
- **Birth year / age**: Waters "thirty days short of his twenty-fifth birthday" at death (born Jan. 1919); Geroux "24";
  ww2history 1920. Script: "a month short of his twenty-fifth birthday".
- **Why he went to Germany in 1939**: Waters "as a Nazi supporter", a Bund friend arranged the trip; Geroux the Bund paid his
  way to visit relatives; ww2history "visit relatives", trapped by the war. Script: the Bund paid / arranged; "Why he went,
  and what he believed then, is not clear."
- **Drafted or volunteered**: both books: drafted, released as an American citizen, then joined anyway. Script follows.
- **The radio**: Waters "parents sent him a radio"; Geroux parents bought it on a visit; Geroux p. 271 "His mother had sent
  him a radio". Script: "his parents got him a radio".
- **Number of attackers**: six to ten (Waters, Geroux, Wikipedia); seven (East Texas History). Script: "six to ten".
- **Prisoners questioned**: 45 (Geroux) / 49 witnesses (Army letter in Waters). Script: "almost fifty".
- **Sentences**: Tex Appeal says seven were "sentenced to death"; Waters (court records) and Wikipedia: life, reduced.
  Script follows Waters.
- **The guards' torture stories**: told in interviews; Waters: the medical report and autopsy photos show it didn't happen.
  Script says so.
- **When Meisel confessed**: Waters "In 1945"; Geroux "December 1945". Script: "near the end of 1945".
- **When he was captured**: Waters "captured in April 1943"; Geroux "captured with the Afrika Korps at Tunis" (the Axis
  surrender in Tunisia, May 1943). Script: "In the spring of 1943 … Krauss was captured with it."
