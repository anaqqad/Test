## Main sources
1. **Task Group 22.3, "Capture of German Submarine U-505"**, report by Capt. D. V. Gallery, June 1944, with enclosures
   (uboatarchive.net/U-505A/U-505.htm, transcriptions of the National Archives copies) **[read directly]**. Times on 4 June:
   1110 Chatelain reports a possible sound contact; 1116 hedgehogs, fighters see the sub; 1121 depth charges; 1122½ sub
   surfaces 800 yards from Chatelain; 1127 cease fire, "official time of surrender", position 21-30 N, 19-20 W; Pillsbury's
   boarding party; tow lines; turn back on 3 June put them in position; pre-cruise orders to plan captures and organize
   boarding parties; the Plan of the Day for 4 June, printed the evening before, listed the crew to man a captured sub;
   pilots told to stop firing, obeyed "in the most disgusted tone of voice"; Jenks to Bermuda with the coding machines.
2. **Enclosure A8, statement of Oblt. Harald Lange, commanding officer of U-505** **[read directly]**. Periscope: three
   destroyers, a possible carrier; dove; two heavy shocks, water in, lights and machinery failed, rudders jammed; surfaced;
   hit in knees and legs, splinters in face and right eyelid, blown to the main deck, lifejacket "no good"; ordered "to
   leave the boat and to sink her"; hauled into a pipe boat; "three cheers for our sinking boat"; the carrier's captain
   told him the boat had been captured.
3. **Gallery, memo "Publish to all hands", Top Secret, 14 June 1944**, scan at uboatarchive.net
   (`docs/Gallery-memo-1944-06-14.jpg`) **[read directly]**. "The operations which we have conducted since 1100 June 4th
   have been classified as top secret." **"The capture of the U-505 can be one of the major turning points in World War
   number two provided repeat provided we keep our mouths shut about it."** "The enemy must not learn of this capture."
   Friends "will read all about it eventually in the history books". "KEEP YOUR BOWELS OPEN AND YOUR MOUTH SHUT".
4. **Enclosure E, list of prisoners** **[read directly]**: Lange, four other officers (incl. a doctor and Paul Meyer), crew.
5. **Navy Department press release, 16 May 1945** **[read directly]**. Revealed after V-E Day; about 150 miles west of
   Cape Blanco, French West Africa; first boarding and capture of a foreign enemy warship on the high seas since 1815;
   59 crew, 58 survived and imprisoned in the U.S.; "They found only one dead man."
6. **Kali Martin, "Louisiana Spotlight: U-505 and Camp Ruston", National WWII Museum, 14 Apr 2020** **[via fetch]**.
   Germany to believe she sank with all hands, to protect the codebooks; names withheld from the Red Cross; not allowed
   to write home; Geneva rights; "could take no chances"; isolated section; families long believed them lost at sea.
7. **Wikipedia, "German submarine U-505"** **[via fetch]**. TG 22.3 (Guadalcanal; Pillsbury, Pope, Flaherty, Chatelain,
   Jenks), sailed Norfolk 15 May 1944; 150 nmi off Río de Oro; one fatality on deck (Gottfried Fischer); Albert David's
   eight-man party; Trosino and the batteries; tug Abnaki; Bermuda 19 June; 58 prisoners, 3 wounded; Camp Ruston,
   isolated, Red Cross denied access; Kriegsmarine declared the crew dead and told their families; returned 1947; King
   considered court-martialling Gallery (Clay Blair); USS Nemo, war bond drive 1945; Father John Gallery, Lenox Lohr, donated
   Sept 1954, Chicagoans raised $250,000; 1964 reunion, binoculars back to Lange; moved underground April 2004, reopened
   5 June 2005. Earlier: Zschech (Sept 1942), Hudson bomb 11 Nov 1942, Lorient, Zschech's suicide 24 Oct 1943, Paul Meyer,
   Lange from 8 Nov 1943.
8. **Wikipedia, "Peter Zschech"** **[via fetch]**. Six times in a row turned back within days, sabotage by French dockyard
   workers suspected; a joke; suicide in the control room in front of the crew.
9. **Wikipedia, "Camp Ruston"** and **Kelby Ouchley, "Camp Ruston", 64 Parishes (2013, upd. 2024)** **[via fetch]**.
   770 acres, ~7 miles west of Ruston, built 1942, WAAC training, first POWs Afrika Korps Aug 1943, peak 4,315 (Oct 1943);
   cotton, timber, roads; scrip; orchestra, theater, library; late July 1944, 56 surviving officers and crew arrived (sic),
   restricted northeast section; last prisoners left 3 Feb 1946; two buildings on the National Register (1991/92); in 1998
   Vincent Spione's National Archives records showed the Chicago museum, which had said the crew was held in Bermuda.
10. **Museum of Science and Industry, "Prisoners of war" (exhibit text), via search** **[via search snippet]**. By August 1944
   the German Navy told relatives the men "must be considered dead"; letters confiscated; last captives home in 1947.
11. **Claire Barrett, Navy Times, 18 June 2024 (on Lachman, *Codename Nemo*)** **[via fetch]**. Over 3,000 sailors in the hunt;
   David first down the hatch; Enigma and codebooks; settings for 15 July 1944; families told the men were missing and
   learned after 1945 they were alive; Red Cross not told, a violation of the Geneva Conventions.
12. **uboat.net, "The U-505 episode"** **[via fetch]**. Crews assembled at Bermuda and told to keep silent; souvenirs
   turned in; Gunner Burr disarmed 13 of 14 charges, the 14th found weeks later.
13. **Wikipedia, "Albert L. David"** **[via fetch]**. Only Navy Medal of Honor of the Atlantic; died 17 Sept 1945; Truman
   gave it to his widow, 5 Oct 1945.

## Disagreements and how the script handles them
- **The dead man:** Gottfried Fischer (Wikipedia) or Hans Fisher (uboat.net). Script: one German sailor, no first name.
- **Crew count at Ruston:** 58 prisoners (TG report, press release, Wikipedia) against "56" in the Camp Ruston article. Script: fifty-eight taken.
- **Tow distance:** about 1,700 nautical miles (Wikipedia) against "about 2,500 miles" (1945 press release). Script: no number.
- **Bermuda date:** 19 June (Wikipedia) or "9 June" (uboat.net). Script: "on the nineteenth of June".
- **First prisoners at Ruston:** June (National WWII Museum) or August 1943. Not used.
- **Return:** camp emptied Feb 1946; crew home in 1947 (Wikipedia, MSI). Script gives both, says the record doesn't show where they were in between.
- **Families:** "considered dead" (MSI) / "declared dead" (Wikipedia) / "missing" (Navy Times). Script follows the museum: told they must be considered dead.
