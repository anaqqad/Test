# Sources: "Navy File Shows What George H.W. Bush Really Did in WW2"

Compiled 22 Nov 2026. Primary documents (government records) are marked **[PRIMARY]**. U.S. government records and presidential remarks are public domain and may be quoted in full. Everything else gets short quotes only.

"Unverified" means I could not confirm the item in a second source. Ranks are given as written in the document cited.

- NAID 305238. Catalog: https://catalog.archives.gov/id/305238. DocsTeach: https://docsteach.org/document/action-report-us-navy-torpedo-squadron-51-vt51-for-actions-occurring-september-12-1944/

- I read the facts below directly from the scans. The marking is "SECRET", with a "DECLASSIFIED" stamp.
  - **Sheet 1 (p.27):**
    - Unit VT-51, based on U.S.S. San Jacinto. Date of action "Sept. 2, 1944".
    - Take-off "0715", zone -9. Position Lat 25°55' N, Long 141°51' E.
    - Mission: "Radio Station at 85.8-50.3 on Chichi Jima / Strike Baker". Time of return "1010".
    - Own aircraft: 4 TBM-1C of VT-51, 4 sorties, 4 attacking. Each plane carried "4-500# G.P." bombs, fuzed ".01 Tail; .1 Nose".
    - Other aircraft: **F6F-5, VF-20, 12; SB2C, VB-20, 8**, both from U.S.S. Enterprise.
  - **Sheet 2 (p.28):**
    - Loss: TBM-1C, VT-51. Cause "Heavy and Med. A/A". Where hit: "Engine". Result: "Crashed in Ocean--Lost".
    - Casualties: "Lt.(jg) W. G. White, USNR" and "J. L. Delaney, ARM 2/c USNR", both "Occupant of TBM-1C lost", status "Missing in action".
    - NOTE, verbatim: "The pilot (Lt.(jg) G.H.W. Bush) of the TBM-1C which was lost, parachuted to water and was picked up by rescue submarine, which reported him unhurt."
    - Fuel and range data: 71 miles out, 75 miles back, 2.9 hours average in the air.
  - **Sheet 3 (p.29):**
    - Targets: "4 Bldgs. (Radio Station) and 1 Tower, at 85.8-50.3 also radio station at 85.6-50.6". Time over target "0825 / 0830" (-9). Visibility "Good".
    - Bombs released at 4,500 ft (target 1) and 4,000 ft (target 2).
    - Results, item 1: "Eight direct hits on target by Lt. Comdr. Melvin, USNR and Lt.(jg) D. W. West, USNR destroyed tower and badly damaged or destroyed buildings."
    - Results, item 2: "Radio station damaged (See XII for narrative) by bombs dropped by Lt.(jg) G.H.W. Bush, USNR and Ens. M. G. Moore, USNR. Extent of damage unobserved."
    - Photographs taken: "No".
  - **Sheet 4 (p.30), narrative (Section XII), verbatim (public domain):**
    - "This division of VT, Composed of Lt. Comdr. D. J. Melvin, USNR, Lt.(jg) D. H. West, USNR, Lt.(jg) G.H.W. Bush, USNR, and Ens. M. G. Moore, USNR was led by Lt. Comdr. Melvin, Commanding Officer and employed normal glide bombing tactics in an attack from South to North though encountering very intense, heavy and medium A/A fire of accurate variety, from areas surrounding the target."
    - "Lt.(jg) Bush, was piloting the third plane over the target. Bush's plane was hit in the engine shortly after final push over at 8,000 ft. In spite of this hit which caused his engine to smoke and catch on fire, Lt.(jg) Bush continued in his dive, releasing his bombs on the radio station at 85.6-50.6 to score damaging hits."
    - "After releasing his bombs, Lt.(jg) Bush turned sharply to the east to clear the island of Chichi Jima, smoke and flames enveloping his engine and spreading aft as he did so, and his plane losing altitude. He advised the C.O. by radio that it was necessary to bail out. At a point approximately 9 miles bearing 045°T from Minami Jima, Bush and one other person were seen to bail out from about 3,000 ft. Bush's chute opened and he landed safely in water, inflated his raft and paddled farther away from Chichi Jima. The chute of the other person (either Lt.(jg) White or J. L. Delaney, ARM 2/c) who bailed out did not open. (Bush has not yet been returned to squadron by rescue sub, so this information is incomplete) The rescue sub promptly affected rescue of Lt.(jg) Bush who was unhurt, planes of VF-20 remaining over Bush's raft to protect him."
    - "While Lt.(jg) White and J. L. Delaney are reported missing in action, it is believed that both were killed as a result of action above described."
    - "It should be noted that VT-51 has established a standard doctrine, whenever wind and other conditions permit, to make bombing runs on targets near water so as to retire over the water."
  - **Sheet 5 (p.31):**
    - Report prepared by: signature hard to read, appears to be "M. E. Kilpatrick, Lt., USNR". Unverified.
    - Approved by "D. J. Melvin, Lt. Comdr., USNR" (signed).
  - The report spells West's initials two ways: "D. W. West" on sheet 3 and "D. H. West" on sheet 4.

## B. Official and scholarly secondary sources

### 8. NARA / Nazi War Crimes and Japanese Imperial Government Records Interagency Working Group, *Researching Japanese War Crimes Records: Introductory Essays* (2006)
- Ch. 4, "Japanese War Crimes Records at the National Archives: Research Starting Points", compiled by NARA staff, pp. 101–104 and endnotes 79–89, pp. 109–110.
- https://www.archives.gov/files/iwg/japanese-war-crimes/introductory-essays.pdf
- Facts:
  - "Between August 1944 and March 1945, on Chichi-Jima, the largest of the Bonin Islands, Japanese forces executed eight downed U.S. airmen. In four of these instances, the prisoners were cannibalized after their executions."
  - The investigation began **21 December 1945** under **Col. P.M. Rixey**. In eight months his board interviewed more than 120 witnesses, producing nearly a thousand pages of testimony.
  - Maj. **Horie Yoshitaka** first claimed the four cannibalized airmen died in U.S. air raids, then admitted the statement was false. He said an order from Tachibana a few days after the war ended required that all records about American flyers be destroyed.
  - 1st Lt. **Enomoto Bunji** (308th Battalion) confirmed the cover story was staged by Maj. **Matoba Sueo**.
  - Matoba, at his second interview (6 and 8 March 1946), said 8–10 POWs were executed and admitted taking part in cannibalism on three occasions. Quote: "Yes, I was a madman due to the war and that is the only reason I can give for being a cannibal."
  - **Early August 1946:** Lt. Gen. **Tachibana Yoshio** and **13 others** were tried on Guam. There were three charges (murder, violation of the laws of war, neglect of duty) and **38 specifications**, including "prevention of honorable burial". Cannibalism had to be charged under that heading "since military and international law at the time lacked provisions to punish cannibalism."
  - **13 of 14 were found guilty.** Tachibana and two officers were sentenced to death; ten received prison terms from five years to life.
  - Medical officer **Teraki Tadashi** fled and left a suicide note (27 March 1946). He was arrested 7 Aug 1948, tried from 4 March 1949, and sentenced 17 March 1949 to four years for mutilating bodies. He was paroled 25 Jan 1951.
  - Other paroled Chichi Jima convicts named: Hayashi Minoru, Isogai Gunji, Kido Matsutaro, Masutani Shinichi, Matsushita Kanehisa, Mori Yasumasa, Sasaki Mitsuyoshi.

### 9. Welch, Jeanie M., "Without a Hangman, Without a Rope: Navy War Crimes Trials After World War II"
- *International Journal of Naval History* 1:1 (April 2002). https://ijnh.seahistory.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/01/pdf_welch.pdf
- Facts:
  - The Navy held trials on Guam and Kwajalein, 1945–49. War Crimes Director was **Admiral John D. Murphy**.
  - Commissions had 5–7 U.S. officers. Defendants had defense counsel, both Allied and Japanese.
  - The Army's official hangman, a master sergeant, trained Navy hangmen. At first the Navy "lacked the proper type of rope."
  - On Chichi Jima: "Tachibana… and 11 other Japanese military personnel were tried for the beheadings of two American airmen in August, 1944 [sic]… One of the executed airmen, a U.S. Navy radioman third class, was dissected and his 'flesh and viscera' eaten."
  - Vice Adm. Mori and Maj. Matoba were also tried "for murder in the deaths of five U.S. airmen, in February, 1945. Major Matoba confessed to cannibalism. However, military and international law had no provisions for punishment for cannibalism per se. They were accused of murder and 'prevention of honorable burial.'"
  - Totals (citing Piccigallo): 47 trials, 123 defendants, 30 death sentences, 10 executions.
  - Rear Adm. Thomas L. Gatch (JAG) on the trials' purpose: "Bringing to justice international gangsters and their underlings…".

### 10. Shuster, Donald R., "U.S. Navy War Crimes Trials on Guam", Guampedia
- https://www.guampedia.com/u-s-navy-war-crimes-trials-on-guam/
- Facts:
  - Trials were held in a quonset hut on Nimitz Hill. The Guam commission heard 51 cases involving 144 persons.
  - "Lt. General Tachibana and Lt. Col. Ito" were found guilty of torture, murder and cannibalism on Chichi Jima and hanged in 1947.
  - The executed were buried in unmarked graves on U.S. military land on Guam.
  - Cites George Wibur, "Five Japanese Military Criminals Executed on Guam for Atrocities," *Navy News*, 26 Sept 1947.

### 11. "Unthinkable Crime", *Time*, 16 Sept 1946
- https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,888305,00.html
- Facts:
  - Contemporary report of the Guam trial. Matoba's confession was read into the record "last week" (early Sept 1946).
  - Matoba was nicknamed the "Tiger of Chichi Jima". Three other officers and ten lower-ranking men were also on trial.
  - Matoba cited "war madness".
  - Investigators first learned of the crimes through **Frederick Arthur Savory**, an islander.
  - **This shows the cannibalism was reported publicly in 1946.** Relevant to the "cover-up" claim; see Disagreements.

### 12. Patch, Nathaniel S., "Mission: Lifeguard. American Submarines in the Pacific Recovered Downed Pilots", *Prologue* (NARA), Fall 2014
- https://www.archives.gov/files/publications/prologue/2014/fall/lifeguard.pdf
- Patch is a NARA reference archivist.
- Facts:

## Disagreements

1. **Enlistment date.**
   - Bush himself (Kilo 8 Pier, 7 Dec 1991, Public Papers) and the San Jacinto Association page say 12 June 1942, his 18th birthday.
   - An NHHC chronology (search snippet) says "13 June 1942 | Enlisted in U.S. Naval Reserve".
   - *Stronger:* 12 June for the swearing-in, per Bush's own public remarks. It is possible the formal USNR paperwork is dated the 13th. Say "on his 18th birthday".
2. **"Youngest naval aviator".**
   - Commissioned 9 June 1943, three days before turning 19: "youngest naval aviator at that time" (San Jacinto Assn / NHHC).
   - Wikipedia's wording is "one of the youngest pilots in the Navy".
   - *Use "one of the youngest".* The superlative cannot be documented.
3. **Escorts on 2 Sept.**
   - The AAR (primary) says 12 F6F-5 of VF-20 and 8 SB2C of VB-20.
   - Pacific Wrecks reverses the numbers (12 SB2C, 8 F6F).
   - *AAR wins.*
4. **The plane's identity and name.**
   - Logbook and Pacific Wrecks: BuNo 46214.
   - Finback patrol report: "plane T-3 of VT-51".
   - Pacific Wrecks: tail "2x" and "no nose art or nickname".
   - Pearl Harbor museum: a "Barbara III" exists in photos, but it is unknown whether that was the plane lost on 2 Sept. Tail shown as X2 or X3 in different sources.
   - *Safe wording:* Bush named his Avengers after Barbara, and one photo shows "Barbara III". Whether the plane lost on 2 Sept carried the name is not documented.
5. **What happened in the cockpit (the core dispute).** Lay this out in order of when each account was written:
   - **AAR (8 Sept 1944, based on the other three pilots):** engine hit, "smoke and catch on fire", "smoke and flames enveloping his engine and spreading aft". "Bush and one other person were seen to bail out", and that person's chute "did not open". Information "incomplete" because Bush was still on the sub.
   - **Finback patrol report, 2 Sept 1944, Bush's own statement hours after rescue:** "he failed to see his crew's parachutes and believed they had jumped when plane was still over CHICHI JIMA, or they had gone down with plane."
   - **Same report, Ens. Beckman (VF-20 fighter pilot, picked up that afternoon):** "it was known that only one man had parachuted from BUSH's plane." It is ambiguous whether the one man was Bush himself, which would contradict the AAR's "one other person", or one crewman.
   - **Bush's logbook (his hand, 1944):** "CRASH LANDING IN SEA NEAR BONIN ISL. – ENEMY ACTION". This is the standard term for the loss, even though he bailed out.
   - **Bush letter, about 3 Sept 1944:** he couldn't see Ted in the turret and assumed he had gone below; "no sign of Del or Ted anywhere around".
   - **DFC citation (Dec 1944):** "smoke and flames from the fire in his plane".
   - **Bush to Doug Wead (early 1980s, per WhoWhatWhy):** saw one chute.
   - **Mierzejewski (1988):** only Bush bailed out; no fire, only a puff of smoke; Bush could have ditched.
   - **Bush (2003 CNN):** "The plane was burning"; "I know I did the right thing".
   - *Assessment:*
     - The two 1944 Navy documents agree the engine was hit and smoking or burning. The AAR reflects three other pilots; Mierzejewski is one gunner's memory 44 years later. The documents are the stronger evidence on the fire.
     - On parachutes the 1944 records themselves disagree: AAR "one other person… chute did not open", Bush "failed to see", Beckman "only one man".
     - No record establishes which crewman, if either, left the plane. Neither body was recovered.
     - Present this as "the records differ" and let the documents speak.
6. **Time in the raft.**
   - "About four hours" (NHHC/San Jacinto Assn, Pacific Wrecks, CNN).
   - Primary times: AAR time over target 0825–0830 (zone -9 = GMT+9) and Finback's 0933 "received word" and 1156 pickup (zone King = GMT+10). Together these give roughly **2 hours 20–30 minutes**. Patch (NARA) says "slightly more than two hours".
   - Bush in 1992 said he was shot down "at 0732". That cannot be reconciled with the AAR without knowing what time zone he meant.
   - *Primary documents favour about 2.5 hours. The zone conversion is my calculation; have it checked.*
7. **Arrival at Midway.**
   - Patch: 29 Sept 1944, "29 days aboard".
   - Patrol report: crossed the date line, then "30 September 1944 … Moored … at MIDWAY". The Bush Library photo of the rescued airmen is dated 30 Sept 1944.
   - *Patrol report wins: 30 Sept 1944, local date.* Bush was aboard about four weeks ("about a month").
8. **Carrier landings.**
   - Bush's logbook: "TOTAL 126 Carrier landings". Pacific Wrecks: 126. Wikipedia: 128.
   - *Logbook wins: 126.*
9. **Marriage date.**
   - 6 Jan 1945 (Wikipedia, Rye NY) vs 5 Jan 1945 (Pacific Wrecks).
   - *Not checked against the Bush Library; Wikipedia's 6 Jan is the commonly cited date.*
10. **Number, names and dates of the executed airmen.**
    - **Count:** eight is consistent across NARA IWG, Wikipedia and Pacific Wrecks.
    - **Eighth man:**
      - Pacific Wrecks lists seven names plus an "unnamed radioman" captured 4 July 1944.
      - Wikipedia and the Navy Memorial name **Warren A. Hindenlang** (VB-109 PB4Y, night raid 4/5 Aug 1944).
      - The VPB-109 Wikipedia page names the lost aircraft's pilot as Lt. Elmer H. Kasperon, 5 Aug 1944.
      - *Hindenlang's capture and execution date is unverified.*
    - **Woellhof's death:** 5, 6 or 7 Aug 1944 depending on the page.
    - **Frazier's death:** 18 Feb (Pacific Wrecks location page) vs 24 Feb 1945 (Pacific Wrecks aircraft page). Beaten to death by Capt. Noburu Nakajima.
    - **Vaughn's death:** 15 March 1945 (Pacific Wrecks, Navy Memorial) vs 9 March 1945 09:00 (Japanese Wikipedia, citing Matoba's order).
    - **Hall's death:** 9 March 1945 (Pacific Wrecks). Possible confusion between Hall and Vaughn.
    - **Hall's middle name:** "Ewing" (Pacific Wrecks, grave records) vs "Ellsworth" (popular sources). *"Ewing" is stronger.*
    - **Who was cannibalized:** four (IWG, Wikipedia) vs five (Japanese Wikipedia). Pacific Wrecks names Mershon and Hall. Welch says one radioman 3/c in the Tachibana case.
    - **Welch's "August, 1944"** for the two beheadings in the Tachibana case conflicts with the February–March 1945 dates; probably an error or a reference to Woellhof and Hindenlang.
11. **The trials.**
    - **Number of defendants:** 14 (IWG/NARA, early Aug 1946) vs 12 (Welch) vs Matoba plus 3 officers plus 10 men (Time, 1946) vs 27–29 (Japanese Wikipedia, likely all Chichi Jima cases together) vs "30 prosecuted" (Wikipedia).
    - **Death sentences:** Tachibana plus two officers (IWG, first trial).
    - **Hanged:**
      - Tachibana, Matoba, Yoshii and one more (Wikipedia).
      - Tachibana, Matoba, Ito, Nakajima (Pacific Wrecks).
      - Tachibana and Ito (Guampedia).
      - *Navy News*, 26 Sept 1947: "Five Japanese… executed".
    - **Tachibana's death:** 24 Sept 1947 at Asan-Maina, Guam (Wikipedia). Matches the *Navy News* date of two days later.
    - **Mori:** life imprisonment on Guam; later death sentence by a Dutch court (Japanese and English Wikipedia; execution date unverified).
    - *Strongest:* NARA IWG for the Aug 1946 trial structure and the "prevention of honorable burial" specification.
    - **The final list of who was hanged on 24 Sept 1947 needs the *Navy News* article or the RG 125 Final Report.** Until then, say "Tachibana, Matoba and others were hanged on Guam in September 1947". Matoba's hanging is consistent across sources; Yoshii, Ito and Nakajima vary.
12. **"Cover-up" / sealed records.**
    - Popular sources and Flyboys reviews say the files were classified to spare families and that families learned only in 2003. One search snippet claims the transcripts were declassified in 1977 (unverified).
    - Against this: *Time* reported Matoba's cannibalism confession publicly in Sept 1946. The NARA IWG volume describes the records as available in RG 125 and RG 331.
    - *Safe wording:* the trial got little press, the details were not shared with the families, and most Americans, including Bush, did not know until Bradley's research around 2002–03. **Avoid saying "sealed until 2003".**
13. **Who filmed the rescue.**
    - The 8 mm film was shot from Finback's conning tower (Pacific Wrecks, Bush Library still).
    - **Ensign William Edwards** was a Finback officer: he is in the 30 Sept 1944 photo and signed the 1984 reunion page "Bill … Edwards".
    - *That he was the cameraman is unverified.*
14. **Mierzejewski's position.** "100 feet ahead" of Bush (UPI). Whether he was in Melvin's or West's plane is unverified.
15. **Iwatake "witnessed" Bush's shoot-down** (Wikipedia). Plausible, as he was a radio operator on Chichi Jima, but this is a single source. Unverified.
