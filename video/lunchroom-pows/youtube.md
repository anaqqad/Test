## Description
```
At eleven thirty one morning during the Second World War, at a railroad station on the line from Louisiana to Arizona, nine Black American soldiers in uniform were told they could only eat in the kitchen. Then about two dozen German prisoners of war walked in with two guards, sat down at the tables and were served. Cpl. Rupert Trimmingham of Fort Huachuca stood outside and watched, and wrote to Yank, the Army Weekly: "Then why are they treated better than we are?"

This is the documented story of the German POWs who were served where Black American soldiers were not: Trimmingham's letter and the hundreds of soldiers who answered it, Lloyd Brown and the lunch counter in Salina, Kansas, Lena Horne at Camp Robinson, Arkansas, the nurses at Florence, Arizona, and the road to Executive Order 9981, told from the letters, the newspapers of the time and the words of the people who were there.
```
