# The 1945 camp films: German prisoners of war in America shown the concentration camps (WWII Frontlines)

After V-E Day the U.S. Army made German prisoners in its American camps watch films of the liberated concentration camps.
Reactions ranged from the "unmoved" audience at Halloran General Hospital (New York Times, June 27, 1945) to the thousand
men at Camp Butner, N.C., who burned their German uniforms (Gansberg 1977; Krammer 1979), the Clarinda letter and the
Fort DuPont rumor that the bodies were Germans killed by the Russians. The story is carried by Gerhard Hennes, held at
Crossville, Tennessee, with his father, and ends with the Army's exit poll, in which only 30 % accepted the reports as true
(Robin 1995). Script in `script.md`, sources in `research/sources.md`. No footage of the camps themselves is shown.
