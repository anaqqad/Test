## Description
On June 26, 1945, about 150 German prisoners of war were brought into the movie theater of Halloran General Hospital on Staten Island and shown a film of the concentration camps the Allied armies had found in Germany. A few covered their eyes. The next day's headline said most were unmoved. That same summer, at Camp Butner, North Carolina, about a thousand prisoners burned their own German uniforms.

This is the documented story of the camp films the U.S. Army made German prisoners in America watch in 1945: Gerhard Hennes and his father, the only father and son in the officers' camp at Crossville, Tennessee; the letter every prisoner at Camp Clarinda, Iowa, signed; the projectionist at Fort DuPont who said the men thought the bodies were Germans killed by the Russians; the camp that voted 95 percent for democracy; and the Army's own exit poll, in which only 30 percent of departing prisoners said they believed the reports. Told from the histories of the camps, a prisoner's memoir and the newspapers of 1945. No footage of the camps themselves is shown.
