## Description
On New Year's Day, 1946, a German general slipped out of a prisoner-of-war camp in Mississippi. He wasn't trying to get home. He was carrying letters: one to a United States senator, one to the director of the Office of Censorship in Washington, complaining about American propaganda in the camps and the loss of tobacco.

His name was Hermann-Bernhard Ramcke, and he was one of dozens of German generals held at Camp Clinton, near Jackson. This is the documented story of how Hitler's generals ended up in Mississippi: the surrender in Tunisia, the secret microphones at Trent Park, the general who was ordered to leave Paris "a field of ruins", the defender of Brest, and the 200-acre model of the Mississippi River their soldiers dug with wheelbarrows and shovels.
