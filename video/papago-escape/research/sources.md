# Sources: "German POWs Dug a Tunnel to Float to Mexico. The River Was Dry."

No source, no line. Every fact in `script.md` maps to one of the entries below. Where sources disagree, the note says
which one the script follows and why.

## Main sources
1. **Ronald H. Bailey, "The Not-So-Great Escape: German POWs in the U.S. during WWII," *World War II* magazine, Nov. 2007**
   (HistoryNet, 5 Nov. 2007): https://www.historynet.com/the-not-so-great-escape-german-pows-in-the-us-during-wwii
   Built largely on John Hammond Moore, *The Faustball Tunnel* (Random House, 1978), which used then newly declassified
   Army records. Used for:
   - Compound 1A held the U-boat officers and crews. Provost marshal Capt. Cecil Parshall knew part of it was hidden from the
     guard towers: "I knew they would discover that blind spot."
   - Lt. Wolfgang Clarus: three ways past the fence, "go through it, fly over it, or dig under it."
   - Digging from about Sept. 1944, directed by four U-boat captains who planned over bridge; entrance by a bathhouse in the
     blind spot, hidden by a loosened board and a coal box.
   - Three-man teams, 90-minute night shifts; dirt flushed down toilets, hidden in attics, carried in pockets, spread on the
     new faustball court. Capt. Jürgen Quaet-Faslem proposed the court ("Shouldn't we have a sports area in this compound?").
   - Tunnel under 3 ft across, as deep as 14 ft to pass under a drainage ditch and a road; up to 3 ft a night.
   - A late-November inspector, standing above the entrance, called the soil "hard as a rock".
   - 178 ft on 20 Dec.; exit disguised with wooden boxes of dirt and grass.
   - Food: toasted bread crushed into crumbs, packed in waxed paper from cereal boxes, mixed with milk or water. Forged papers
     from photographs; stamps made from leather and rubber.
   - The "three mad boatmen": Capt. Wilhelm Günther, Lts. Wolfgang Clarus and Friedrich Utzolino. Folding flatboat of
     scavenged lumber; canvas and tar obtained for "roof repairs"; no part longer than 18 inches, to fit the tunnel.
   - A noisy party in the neighbouring compound covered the escape. 25 men (12 officers, 13 enlisted) out by 2:30 a.m.,
     crossing waist-deep, icy water in the Crosscut Canal in the rain.
   - Christmas Eve: one group in a stable eating bread crumbs and canned milk while a Mexican family sang carols; another had
     a harmonica player who played "Stille Nacht".
   - Sunday count delayed (German officers demanded an American officer take it); by about 7 p.m. Parshall confirmed the
     escape and called the FBI. $25 reward per escapee. *Phoenix Gazette*: "the greatest manhunt in Arizona history."
   - Herbert Fuchs, 22, walked into the Phoenix sheriff's office; two surrendered in Tempe.
   - No recorded sabotage or assault by escaped POWs; outrage over rationed goods found on prisoners: citizens "cannot get a
     single slice of bacon for weeks on end."
   - 1 Jan.: a Papago tracker found a pair asleep less than 30 miles from the border. 6 Jan.: Indian scouts caught
     Quaet-Faslem and Guggenberger.
   - Boatmen: canvas shrank in the rain, the Gila too muddy to float the boat; they abandoned it and walked. Cowboys near
     Gila Bend saw one washing his underwear. Clarus: "We should have known that the Gila wasn't much of a river."
   - Wattenberg, Walter Kozur and Johann Kremer in a cave; Kremer swapped places with a friend on outside work details;
     an inspection found Kremer in camp; three GIs waiting at an abandoned car caught Kozur.
   - 27 Jan.: Wattenberg walked into Phoenix with 75 cents, ate at a restaurant, slept in a hotel lobby chair; a
     street-cleaning foreman found his accent suspicious and alerted police; back at Papago Park by 9 a.m. The camp
     commander called him "the No. 1 Super-Nazi of this camp."
   - No American officer or guard court-martialed; escapees got bread and water for each day absent; some had feared
     execution. Clarus: the tunnel "kept our spirits up even as Germany was being crushed."
2. **Wikipedia, "Great Papago Escape"** (cites Keith Warren Lloyd, *The Great Desert Escape*, Lyons Press, 2019; Melton
   and Smith, *Arizona Goes to War*, 2003). Used for: camp opened 1943 in eastern Phoenix, Germans only by Jan. 1944, mostly
   Kriegsmarine; five compounds; peak ~3,100 prisoners plus 371 American guards and officers; prisoners not required to
   work; tools lent for gardening and a volleyball field; tunnel finished 20 Dec., running east toward the Crosscut Canal;
   plan Salt River → Gila → Colorado → Gulf of California; very little water in the Salt River; boatmen caught 8 Jan. near
   Gila Bend; Guggenberger and Quaet-Faslem caught within 10 miles of the border; Wattenberg captured 28 Jan. 1945; FBI
   investigated lax security; part of the site is now an Arizona National Guard base with a museum display.
3. **Wikipedia, "Camp Papago Park"**: prisoners called the camp "Schlaraffenland", the land of milk and honey; the Salt
   River was not flowing.
4. **Douglas Towne, "Nazis in Papago," *PHOENIX* magazine, 1 Nov. 2023** (cites Jane Eppinga, *Death at Papago Park POW
   Camp*, 2017; *Phoenix Gazette*, 28 Dec. 1944): Drechsler dead within about seven hours of arriving; the blue lines on
   the boatmen's map were unnavigable; underwear drying on a mesquite tree; a prisoner hurt by a cactus surrendered at a
   farmhouse, where the boy's mother called them "charming and handsome guests"; Wattenberg's cave near Piestewa Peak, his
   meal and the Adams Hotel lobby; 1985 reunion quote: "Strange we have to kill each other before we become friends";
   plaque by the Crosscut Canal marking the tunnel's end.
5. **uboat.net, "Jürgen Wattenberg"**: born 28 Dec. 1900, Lübeck; navigation officer of *Admiral Graf Spee*; U-162, three
   patrols, 14 ships sunk; U-162 sunk 3 Sept. 1942 by three British destroyers north-east of Trinidad (49 survivors,
   2 dead); POW at Fort Hunt, Crossville, then Papago Park from Jan. 1944; at large longest, until 28 Jan. 1945; died
   27 Nov. 1995, Hamburg, aged 94.
6. **Wikipedia, "Werner Drechsler"**: U-118 sailor turned informer for U.S. naval intelligence; sent to Papago Park
   12 March 1944 and killed there that night (found hanging in the shower room); seven prisoners hanged at Fort
   Leavenworth, 25 Aug. 1945.
7. **Wikipedia, "Friedrich Guggenberger"**: U-81 torpedoed HMS *Ark Royal* on 13 Nov. 1941; captured July 1943 (U-513);
   recaptured 6 Jan. 1945 about 10 miles from the border; later Konteradmiral in the Bundesmarine and Deputy Chief of Staff
   at NATO's AFNORTH.
8. **American Heritage, June/July 1978** (review of Moore): "Their guards were delighted; it was the first cooperative
   gesture these particular die-hard Nazis had made."
9. **National Park Service, "Tempe, Arizona"**: camp of 3,000, mainly Kriegsmarine; the escapees later called it "more of a
   prank than a real escape attempt."

## Disagreements
- **Tunnel length:** 178 ft (Moore via Bailey, Wikipedia, the PHOENIX article, American Heritage) vs 176 ft (NPS,
  Wikipedia "Camp Papago Park"). Script: 178 feet.
- **The boat:** "folding flatboat" of lumber, canvas and tar (Bailey/Moore) vs "wood-and-rubber raft" (Wikipedia). Script
  follows Moore: a folding boat of scrap lumber and canvas.
- **Wattenberg's cave:** "near camp" (Bailey), "mountains north of Phoenix" (Wikipedia), "near Piestewa Peak" (PHOENIX).
  Script: "a cave in the mountains north of the city".
- **Kremer found / Kozur caught:** 22 Jan. and the next night (Wikipedia) vs 23 and 24 Jan. (Bailey). Script: "late in
  January", no exact day.
- **How the tunnel was found:** accounts differ (a recaptured prisoner vs a guard finding the exit). Script doesn't say.
