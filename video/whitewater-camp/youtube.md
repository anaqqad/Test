## Description
On a Saturday night in February 1944, in a snowstorm, a seventeen-year-old schoolteacher was alone in a Manitoba farmhouse when two young men in blue denim, with a big red circle on the back of their jackets, knocked on the door. They were German prisoners of war. They said their names were Willi and Reinhard, and they helped her with a jigsaw puzzle. Less than an hour later, a Canadian officer walked in.

The prisoners came from a camp at Whitewater Lake, in Riding Mountain National Park: 440 men of the Afrika Korps, sent to cut firewood in a camp with no fence, no barbed wire and no guard tower, only a ring of trees marked with red blazes. At night they slipped out to the dances in the Ukrainian farm communities south of the park, and came back before roll call. This is the documented story of that camp, told from the historian Bill Waiser's work, the guard officer's report, the teacher's own account, and the archaeology of the site: the night trips, the raid, the apology with a ship in a bottle, the dugout canoes, and what happened to the teacher.
