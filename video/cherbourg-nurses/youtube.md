## Description
On July 2, 1944, closed American ambulances carried German women toward the front line in Normandy. They were prisoners of the U.S. Army, the first German Army nurses the Americans had captured in France, taken in an underground hospital when Cherbourg fell. That evening, under a short cease-fire arranged by radio, they were taken back through the lines at Caumont.

Why would the Army give prisoners back? Because a 1929 Geneva treaty said captured medical staff "shall not be treated as prisoners of war" and "shall be sent back." Before they left, the women were walked through an American evacuation hospital and talked to German patients there. One of the officers who arranged the truce was Captain Quentin Roosevelt, the President's grandson, whose father died in Normandy ten days later. This is the documented story, told from the hospital's account, the Army's photographs and captions, and the treaty itself.
