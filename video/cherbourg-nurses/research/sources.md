## Main sources
1. **WW2 US Medical Research Centre, "45th Evacuation Hospital, Special Anecdote"** (med-dept.com/veterans-testimonies/45th-evacuation-hospital/)
   **[read directly in the browser]**, citing the 45th Evac and *Yank*, British edition, 30 July 1944. On 2 July 1944 nine German nurses
   arrived at the 45th Evacuation Hospital near La Cambe (Calvados); DRK personnel and Kriegsmarine auxiliaries, captured after the fall
   of Cherbourg, where they had worked at the underground hospital; American authorities had decided to return them to German lines;
   they did not know until after arriving that they would be released, and were "overjoyed"; the CO took them through the hospital;
   they saw supplies and equipment and talked to German patients and prisoners; "most curious" about care of German patients in England
   and on the continent; the staff hoped they would remember and treat Americans with the same kindness; cease-fire negotiated; closed
   WC-54 ambulances to Balleroy; about two hours' wait while final arrangements were made with the German officers; short cease-fire
   agreed by radio between Capt. Fred Ghercke (CO, POW Interrogation Team No. 24), Capt. Quentin Roosevelt (G-2, 1st Infantry Division)
   and Maj. F. Heeren (2nd Panzer Division reconnaissance battalion); about 1800 hours taken through the lines at Caumont-l'Éventé;
   AP correspondent Don Whitehead wrote about it; medical personnel could be retained to care for prisoners and returned when no longer
   needed; two exchanges, 2 and 9 July 1944, sixteen nurses in all, on the road between Balleroy and Caumont; a photo of "7 DRK nurses"
   at the 45th Evac, La Cambe, 8 July 1944.
2. **U.S. Army Signal Corps caption, ETO HQ 44 6419, 2 July 1944** (National WWII Museum, ww2online.org) **[via fetch]**. "The first
   German Army nurses to fall into American hands, captured in an underground hospital in a cave near Cherbourg"; taken to the 45th
   Evac. Hosp.; initially passed as censored; released without restriction by the SHAEF censor on 14 May 1945.
3. **Signal Corps photo at Balleroy, 2 July 1944** (Wikimedia Commons, "Prisonnières allemandes durant la Seconde Guerre mondiale",
   via PhotosNormandie) **[via API]**. A WC-54 ambulance in front of a house; German "nurses" being returned, with GIs, a colonel and a
   lieutenant; the Allied press called them "Nazi nurses".
4. **Library of Congress, "Captured nurses returned to Nazi lines", 8 July 1944** (item 2008679149) **[via search result]**. Seven German
   nurses on a bench in Cherbourg, an armed MP behind them. (Image not downloaded: the LoC site sits behind a bot check.)
5. **Convention for the Amelioration of the Condition of the Wounded and Sick of Armies in the Field, Geneva, 27 July 1929**,
   in Bevans, *Treaties and Other International Agreements of the U.S.*, vol. 2, pp. 972-973 (`docs/Geneva-1929-wounded-sick-arts-9-12.pdf`,
   Library of Congress) **[read directly]**. Art. 9: medical personnel "shall be respected and protected under all circumstances. If they fall
   into the hands of the enemy they shall not be treated as prisoners of war." Art. 10: volunteer aid society personnel (Red Cross)
   assimilated. Art. 12: "may not be detained"; "they shall be sent back to the belligerent to whose service they are attached as soon as
   a way is open for their return and military exigencies permit"; meanwhile they care for their own side's wounded.
6. **Wikipedia, "Battle of Cherbourg"** **[via fetch]**. Assault 22-27 June 1944; Fort du Roule 26 June; von Schlieben captured 26 June;
   arsenal surrendered 29 June; some held out to 1 July; port wrecked; German losses in the table include about 30,000 captured.
7. **Wikipedia, "Quentin Roosevelt II"** and **"Theodore Roosevelt Jr."** **[via fetch]**. Quentin II, born 1919, grandson of President
   Theodore Roosevelt, 1st Infantry Division, landed at Omaha in the first wave; died 21 Dec 1948 in a plane crash near Hong Kong. His
   father, Brig. Gen. Theodore Roosevelt Jr., died of a heart attack in Normandy on 12 July 1944; Medal of Honor for Utah Beach.
   **Signal Corps SC 191974** (15 July 1944): Capt. Quentin Roosevelt marches in his father's funeral procession at Sainte-Mère-Église.

## Disagreements and how the script handles them
- **Quentin Roosevelt's job:** artillery officer (Wikipedia) vs "G-2, First Infantry Division" (med-dept, from the 1944 account). Script: "a captain of the First Infantry Division".
- **How many in the first group:** nine arrived at the 45th Evac (med-dept); the Signal Corps photo at Balleroy shows four. Script: nine.
- **Where they were held:** "a cave near Cherbourg" (Signal Corps) / "the underground hospital" (med-dept). Script: an underground hospital near Cherbourg.
- **Nurses or not:** DRK nurses and Kriegsmarine auxiliaries (med-dept); the press called them all nurses. Script says both.
- **The press:** AP's Don Whitehead is named only by med-dept; script says "an Associated Press correspondent wrote about it", attributed.
