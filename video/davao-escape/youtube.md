## Description
On Sunday morning, April 4, 1943, ten American prisoners of war walked out of the gate of the Davao Penal Colony on Mindanao with the day's work details, while a Japanese guard checked them off on a chalkboard. That evening the roll call came up ten Americans and two Filipino convicts short. It was the only successful group escape from a Japanese prison camp in the Pacific war.

This is the documented story of the Davao escape: the Bataan Death March and Camp O'Donnell, the swamp, the village of Lungaog, the submarine Trout, and what happened when the escapees brought their story home. Ed Dyess was put under a gag order, Roosevelt held back every escaped prisoner's story for fear of endangering the relief ship Gripsholm, and Dyess died in a burning P-38 in Burbank five weeks before the country finally read what he had seen. Told from John D. Lukacs's history of the escape, McCoy and Mellnik's own 1944 account and the newspapers of 1943 and 1944.
