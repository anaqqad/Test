# German prisoners of war in a German-speaking town: New Ulm, Minnesota, 1944-1945 (WWII Frontlines)

About 150 young German prisoners were sent in June 1944 to a camp outside New Ulm, Minnesota, a town founded by German
immigrants where much of the population still spoke German, to work in the Sleepy Eye cannery and on farms. The town talked to
them, fed them and sang with them despite the Army's warnings; a farm family was fined $300 for keeping one for a weekend; the
sympathy ended when the camps in Germany were found in 1945. Script in `script.md`, sources in `research/sources.md`, the
Minnesota History article and treaty pages in `research/docs/`.

Built at 1080p: `assemble.py --hd`, cards with `HEIGHT=1080`. Spoken subscribe ask at about 55 % with the `SubscribeButton`
overlay. Thumbnails: `python tools/thumbs_episode.py` (badges in the prisoner photo blurred; original in `build/`).
