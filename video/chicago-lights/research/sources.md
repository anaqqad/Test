# Sources: "German POWs Came From a Blacked-Out Continent. Then One Rode Into Chicago at Night."

## Primary source
**Reinhold Pabel, *Enemies Are Human*** (Philadelphia: John C. Winston, 1955). I checked the passages with
the Internet Archive's full-text search (item `enemiesarehuman0000pabe`); the book itself is lending-only.
Passages used:
- Born in Hamburg "during the second year of World War I" (ch. 2, *Growing Up in Prewar Germany*).
- Russia and Sicily service; met "Lieutenant Lindsey from Dallas, Texas" at an American aid station;
  "Lindsey, my old pal from the Volturno" (ch. 7 and later).
- "November 30, 1943. Well, here I am in a POW camp" (North Africa).
- "We are bound for Virginia, and due at Norfolk on January 2"; "we went ashore at Norfolk, Va., in the morning
  hours of January [2, 1944]" (ch. 8, *Prisonerland, U.S.A.*).
- "En route through Virginia and Kentucky we pressed our noses against the windowpanes to take in the sights.
  The first impression we had was the abundance of automobiles everywhere"; "the numerous frame houses,
  especially in Kentucky"; "when the colored porter came through with coffee and sandwiches and politely
  offered them to us as though we were human beings, most of us forgot ... those anti-American feelings".
- "In Camp Grant, Illinois, we found our first permanent [camp]"; gave the daily news report; published
  translations in late 1944 and early 1945.
- Telegram: "The folks are bombed out again. This is the second time".
- Transferred to Camp Ellis; "After May 7, 1945 all noncoms in Ellis were told to 'volunteer' for work".
- Ch. 9, *Operation Vapor*: "During the first week of September 1945 I was transferred to Washington, Illinois",
  a branch camp near the cannery "during the corn season"; "only one watch tower had been erected"; "nineteen
  miles to Peoria"; asked for "the night shift ... instead of the noon shift"; AP clipping quoted in the book:
  "War Prisoner Flees Camp Near Peoria. Camp Ellis, Ill. September 11 (AP). ... from an army branch camp at
  Washington, Ill. 19 miles east of Peoria, Col. C. P. Evers, Camp Ellis Commander, announced today" and
  "disappeared during the change of shifts".
- Bus depot at the Public Square in Peoria; "I never bought a ticket in America before"; "It's two o'clock in the
  morning when I buy a ticket; leaves me $3.75 total cash"; "These eggs are delicious. My first meal in liberty.
  I still have a full hour to kill"; "With every mile this coach eats up, I will get one mile closer to ...".
- p. 185: "These must be some of Chicago's suburbs. A million lights pop up everywhere"; "Brightly colored
  lights and pompous window displays. So this is my new home. Bewildered, I get off the bus at the Randolph
  Street ..."; "the beauty of Chicago's skyscrapers at night. The giant Merchandise Mart with its thousand lights
  reflected in the Chicago River".
- Alias "Phil Brick"; dishwasher, Martha's Restaurant on North Clark Street; Chicago Book Mart on the North
  Side; wife Avis.
- Ch. 1, *The Jig Is Up*: "The morning of March 9, 1953 was just like any other morning in the Chicago Book
  Mart"; an FBI man asked, "Are you Reinhold Pabel?"
- "Propaganda as a weapon, any kind of propaganda, is harmful".

## Secondary sources
- Chris Crawford, "Reinhold Pabel", erasmatazz.com: theology studies, drafted 1940, wounded winter 1941,
  irrigation-dam wound, Lindsey testified for him, about six months in Germany before legal re-entry.
  https://erasmatazz.com/library/people-through-history/reinhold-pabel.html
- Black Gate, review of *Basic Agreement* by Avis Pabel: arrested 1953 at the bookstore; entered legally as a
  POW, so he could only be charged with escaping; six months in Germany, then return. https://blackgate.com/?p=504753
- WBEZ Chicago, "Hosting the enemy: our WWII POW camps": Pabel at Camp Grant, Rockford; FBI article in camp.
  https://www.wbez.org/stories/hosting-the-enemy-our-wwii-pow-camps/cebcf176-4be5-4b07-9c8a-896c94e1e154
- History Collection, "10 little-known facts about Axis prisoners of war": Hoover/FBI article found in the camp
  library; alias Phil Brick. https://historycollection.com/10-little-known-facts-abou-axis-prisoners-of-war-in-world-war-ii/8/
- EBSCO Research Starters, "Dim-out of 1945": mandatory national dim-out January–May 1945, neon signs
  extinguished, street lighting reduced, lifted on V-E Day; coastal blackouts from early 1942.
  https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/military-history-and-science/dim-out-1945
- German blackout (*Verdunkelung*) in force from 1 September 1939: Deutsches Historisches Museum, LeMO poster
  "Verdunkeln!" (1940). https://www.dhm.de/lemo/bestand/objekt/pl004404
- Hamburg raids, July 1943 (Operation Gomorrah, firestorm, tens of thousands killed): standard reference
  (e.g. Keith Lowe, *Inferno*, 2007).

## Title note
The working title was "German POWs Were Told American Cities Were Blacked Out. Then the Train Passed Through
Chicago at Night." I found no source that German POWs were *told* this, and Pabel reached Chicago by **bus**, not
train. The title was changed to stay truthful.
