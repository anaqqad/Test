# Alexander Jefferson, Tuskegee Airman and POW (1944-45): research sources

## Why this topic
Competitor research from each channel's Popular tab, 8-9 Nov 2026 (owner's rule: ideas come from competitors' most-viewed videos):
- WWII Box, "A Tuskegee Pilot Was Shot Down Over France. Then He Was Locked In With White American Officers." 301K in 4 weeks, its
  second biggest video. Its Red Tails cluster also pulls: 175K, 144K, 87K. WWII Desk, "A German Soldier Saluted a Black Pilot. His Own
  Country Didn't." 117K in 3 weeks. Memoirs of WWII, "Memories of a Tuskegee Pilot", 570K.
- Not covered by our past episodes. Owner chose it on 8 Nov 2026 for 9 Nov.
- Our documented angle: Jefferson's own account of the interrogator's book, the gangplank in New York, and what the law and an Air
  Force historian say about the record. Hook: the book with his high school grades and his father's taxes.

Compiled 2026-11-09. The memoir (*Red Tail Captured, Red Tail Free*, Fordham UP, 2005, rev. 2017) is not open, so nothing is taken
from it directly. The Library of Congress transcripts sit behind a bot check, which I did not go around, so they are not used either.

## Main sources
1. **NPR News & Notes, "A Tuskegee Airman's Harrowing WWII Tale", 10 Nov 2006** (npr.org/transcripts/6467779), Jefferson in his own
   words. **[read directly]** Used for: model airplanes, cutting school to walk to an airfield, first ride in a Waco; weight test
   (116 minimum, 115 and a few ounces, water and bananas, 116 and 2 oz); flying over cumulus clouds, "greatest experience"; arrival at the
   strip, watching a P-47 stall and crash; escort work, flak cloud like a hockey puck, pebbles on a tin roof; bomber half on fire, eight men,
   sick in the oxygen mask at 24,000 ft; the strafing run, 400 mph, cliff lit up, hole in the canopy, shell through the floor in front of the
   stick, fire; no training in getting out, pulled the D-ring at once, in the trees, gunners 200-300 yards off; **the interrogator's book**
   (332nd Fighter Group, Negroes, red tails; pictures of all earlier classes; marks at Clark and at Chadsey High School; taxes his dad paid on
   the house; knew more about him than he knew himself); Stalag Luft III, "no segregation, no discrimination"; walked 80 km, twenty below
   zero; Stalag 7A; liberation by Patton's Third Army; next day jeep to Dachau, "the ovens were still warm", the odor, table of gold teeth,
   "Man's inhumanity to man"; gangplank in New York, sign "whites to the right, colored to the left", white soldier below using a slur;
   "best country in the world... the only obligation, make it better", "It ain't perfect but it's home". NPR's intro: "more respect as a POW
   than as a black man in America".
2. **Gathering of Eagles Foundation, profile of Alexander Jefferson, 2010** (goefoundation.org/eagles/jefferson-alexander/) **[via fetch]**.
   Clark, June 1942, chemistry and biology; Howard; Tuskegee April 1943; wings January 1944; first P-51 combat mission after three hours of
   transition; 18 escort missions; 12 Aug 1944, three days before the invasion of southern France; wingmen did not see him bail out, parents
   told killed in action; a month later a Red Cross letter said he was a prisoner; about nine months a POW; about fifty miles sub-zero march,
   then railcar to VII-A; contraband radio, BBC; B-17 crew: "Had you Red Tails been with us, we wouldn't have been shot down."; liberated 29 April 1945;
   Purple Heart 2004.
3. **Phil Scott, "Pilots", AOPA Pilot, March 2005** (aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2005/march/pilot/pilots-(3)) **[via fetch]**. 301st
   Fighter Squadron; after 18 escort missions his first ground attack, a radar site near Toulon; two of the four aircraft shot down; "I was
   number Four."; shell through the cockpit and out the canopy; rolled and bailed out; landed among German soldiers; train to Stalag Luft 3; arrived
   September 1944, "I was the fourth black", about 5,000 American officers; same Red Cross clothing and rations, "There was no racial thing";
   about 80 km march, then cattle cars to near Munich; C-47 to Le Havre, ship to England and on to New York; separate sides at the dock,
   "Back to segregation."; instructor at Tuskegee until the base closed; Lockbourne; taught elementary science in Detroit until 1980.
4. **Public Law 109-213, 11 Apr 2006, Congressional Gold Medal to the Tuskegee Airmen** (`docs/PLAW-109publ213.pdf`) **[read directly]**.
   Finding 1 (Roosevelt overruled his top generals, 1941, one day after the NAACP suit for Yancy Williams); 2 (separate airfield); 4 ("Studies
   commissioned by the Army War College between 1924 and 1939 concluded that Blacks were unfit for leadership roles and incapable of
   aviation."); 5 (992 pilots); 9 (Black Redtail Angels, reputation for not losing bombers); 11 ("66 of these pilots were killed in combat, while
   another 32 were either forced down or shot down and captured to become prisoners of war.").
5. **Wikipedia, "Tuskegee Airmen"** **[via fetch]**. 179 escort missions; 27 bombers lost against an average of 46 for other P-51 groups in
   the Fifteenth; Haulman of the Air Force Historical Research Agency; the "never lost a bomber" claim traced to the Chicago Defender,
   24 March 1945; 32 POWs; EO 9981 (1948); Gold Medal 29 March 2007.
6. **Wikipedia, "Alexander Jefferson"** **[via fetch]**, citing HistoryMakers, Challenger Center, NASA Quest, LoC. Born 15 Nov 1921 Detroit; Chadsey High School
   1938; Army Reserve 23 Sept 1942; analytical chemist three months, Howard; called up April 1943; Ramitelli; 19th mission; Queen Mary to
   New York mid-1945; instructor until 1946; reserves to 1969 (Lt. Col.); teaching certificate Wayne State 1947, Detroit elementary science;
   assistant principal 1969; retired 1979 after 30+ years; Gold Medal ceremony 2007; died 22 June 2022, age 100.
7. **National WWII Museum, "Alexander Jefferson"** (nationalww2museum.org/about-us/notes-museum/alexander-jefferson) **[via fetch]**. 301st; 18 missions over France, Germany and
   Romania; camps; Third Army; Michigan Aviation Hall of Fame 1995.
8. **Wikipedia, "Stalag Luft III"** **[via fetch]**. Near Sagan, now Żagań, Poland, about 100 miles SE of Berlin; ~1,928 kcal German ration plus Red Cross parcels; left just before
   midnight 27 Jan 1945, Soviets about 16 miles away; about 6 inches of snow; about 11,000 marched; 34 miles to Bad Muskau, 30-hour rest, 16 miles to Spremberg; train to Moosburg.
9. **Wikipedia, "Stalag VII-A"** **[via fetch]**. Moosburg, Bavaria; opened 1939 for up to 10,000 Polish prisoners; 76,248 in the main camp at liberation; 29 April 1945, 14th Armored Division; 240 guards surrendered.
10. **City of Detroit, "Mayor unveils statue, dedicates plaza..."** (detroitmi.gov) **[via fetch]**. 24 June 2024, bronze statue by Austen Brantley, Jefferson Field, Rouge Park; field used by Detroit Aero Modelers; he flew model airplanes there later in life.
11. **The Log Book Project, "Alexander Jefferson"** (thelogbookproject.com/alexander-jefferson/) **[via fetch]**. In officer's uniform at the dock; treated better as a POW than at home.

Background only, not read out: FDR Library copy of the 1925 Army War College memo, "Employment of Negro Man Power in War" (`docs/FDR-Library-1925-Army-War-College-memo.pdf`).

## Disagreements and how the script handles them
- **Mission count:** NPR/Wikipedia 19th mission; GOE "18th"; AOPA "after 18 escort missions" his first ground attack. Script: eighteen escort missions, the nineteenth was the strafing run.
- **Place:** Toulon (NPR-era sources, GOE, AOPA, Wikipedia) against Douamont (National WWII Museum, which is near Verdun). Script: near Toulon.
- **Date:** 12 Aug 1944 nearly everywhere; one local wiki says 18 Aug. Script: 12 Aug.
- **Stalag Luft III's distance:** Jefferson "80 miles east of Berlin"; Wikipedia about 100 miles southeast. Script: about a hundred miles southeast of Berlin.
- **The march:** Jefferson 80 km and twenty below zero; GOE fifty miles; Wikipedia 34 + 16 miles, freezing. These agree on distance; script gives the temperature as his memory.
- **Ship home:** Wikipedia (citing LoC) Queen Mary; NPR and AOPA Le Havre, then England, then New York. Compatible. The film of the Queen Mary is tagged as that ship in July 1945, not as his crossing.
- **The dock:** NPR (his words) sign plus a white soldier; Wikipedia a white sergeant; AOPA soldiers. Script: "a white soldier". The slur is not repeated.
- **Retirement:** 1979 (Wikipedia) or 1980 (AOPA). Script: more than thirty years, no year.
- **"Never lost a bomber":** NPR's intro and the law's "reputation" against Haulman's count. Script says 27.
- **The interrogator's book:** only Jefferson's account exists here. Script says so.
- **Camp rank detail in GOE** ("highest-ranking POW") is garbled, so it is not used.
