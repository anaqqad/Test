## Description
In the late summer of 1944, a German interrogator sat down with an American fighter pilot who had just been shot down over southern France. Before the pilot said a word, the German named his unit: the 332nd Fighter Group, the Red Tails. Then he opened a book with the pilot's college grades, his high school grades in Detroit, and the tax his father paid on the family house.

The pilot was Lt. Alexander Jefferson, one of 32 Tuskegee Airmen captured in the Second World War. He spent nine months in Stalag Luft III and Stalag VII-A, where, in his words, there was no segregation and no discrimination. He saw Dachau the day after his liberation. Then he sailed home to New York, walked down the gangplank in an officer's uniform, and met a sign: whites to the right, colored to the left. This is the documented story, told from his own interviews, the law that gave the Tuskegee Airmen the Congressional Gold Medal, and the record around them.
