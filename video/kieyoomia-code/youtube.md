## Description
A winter day in a Japanese prison camp. A guard marches an American prisoner, stripped naked, onto the parade ground and leaves him in the snow. He can go back inside, he is told, when he tells them the code. The prisoner was Joe Kieyoomia, a Navajo silversmith from New Mexico's 200th Coast Artillery, captured on Bataan. The code was being spoken across the Pacific in his own language. And he could not understand a word of it.

This is the true story of the Navajo code: Philip Johnston's idea, the first twenty-nine Navajo Marines who built the code at Camp Elliott in 1942, the "iron fish" and the "tortoise," Guadalcanal, Saipan and the 800 messages without an error at Iwo Jima, and the one Navajo prisoner Japan tried to break it with. Where Kieyoomia's accounts and the records disagree, including where he was when the bomb fell on Nagasaki, we tell you so.
