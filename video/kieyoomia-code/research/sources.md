[1] **"Neither Navajo Captive Nor Code Was Broken."** Associated Press, *Deseret News*, 28 Sep 1995. **[P-interview]**
URL: https://www.deseret.com/1995/9/28/19195524/neither-navajo-captive-nor-code-was-broken
(archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20201112024254/https://www.deseret.com/1995/9/28/19195524/neither-navajo-captive-nor-code-was-broken)
The AP writer is Tim Korte (see [2], same text with his byline). This interview is the source of almost every vivid detail in the Kieyoomia story.
- Opening line: Army "Sgt. Joe Kieyoomia" and the code talkers' words were "both … like granite against Japanese efforts to break them."
- "Captured after the fall of the Philippines in 1942, Kieyoomia lived 43 months in Japanese prison camps."
- "A member of New Mexico's famed 200th Coast Artillery unit, he survived the notorious Bataan Death March."
- As a POW he "lived on meager meals of rice laced with weevils."
- He "watched as weakened Americans were ordered to dig their graves, then were shot by Japanese guards."
- Quote: "At the end of one week, they told me I was going to be next … But a few days later, I was sent to the Japanese mainland."
- Age given in 1995: "says Kieyoomia, 72" (conflicts with every birth year; see Disagreements).
- "his captors at a Nagasaki prison initially tortured him because - judging from his surname - they thought he was Japanese-American."
- Quote: "I told them I was Navajo." / "They didn't believe me … The only thing they understood about Americans was black and white. I guess they didn't know about Indians."
- Interview setting: "outside his remote home on the Navajo reservation in northwestern New Mexico"; described with "crystal-blue eyes … and a pair of bifocals."
- "After months of beatings … the Japanese accepted his claim to Navajo ancestry. But he says the torture that followed was worse."
- Quote: "One day, two Japanese women visited me … They wrote Navajo words in English and asked what they meant. So, I told them: 'This means bird, this means turtle, this means water.'"
- He thought the Japanese were baffled after hearing the code talkers in Navajo, "which was unwritten at the time."
- "When the Japanese questioned Kieyoomia, his translations were useless." Quote: "I didn't know about the code."
- "One winter day, a guard marched Kieyoomia - stripped naked - from his tiny cell onto an icy parade ground." Told he "couldn't return inside until he revealed the Navajo code."
- Quote: "It was 27 degrees outside. They made me stand one hour in snow this deep" (hands spread 6 inches). "The guard said if I moved, he would shoot me."
- His feet had frozen to the ground; the guard shoved him, "causing the skin on the bottom of his feet to tear." Quote: "I left my soles on that parade ground."
- "Beatings continued almost daily"; at one point he "could no longer use his right leg."
- "When he tried starvation, guards beat him until he ate." Quote: "I wanted to die … Many times, I thought I was close."
- Survived the Nagasaki bomb, "saying he was protected by the concrete walls of his cell. He was abandoned for three days after the bombing but says a Japanese officer finally freed him."
- After return "he regained the use of his wounded leg, and his feet healed."
- "Years later, Kieyoomia says, he learned of the Code Talkers and their accomplishments."
- Quote: "I salute the Code Talkers … And even if I knew about their code, I wouldn't tell the Japanese." He showed "his 12 service medals."

### PART B: CONTEXT — 200th COAST ARTILLERY, BATAAN

[20] **Wikipedia, "200th Infantry Regiment (United States)" (200th Coast Artillery section), citing Clay, *US Army Order of Battle 1919-1941* (2010).** **[S]**
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/200th_Infantry_Regiment_(United_States)
- Redesignated 200th Coast Artillery (AA) 1 July 1940 (from the 111th Cavalry); inducted into federal service 6 Jan 1941 at home stations in New Mexico.
- "doubled in size with draftees to 1,800 men while at Fort Bliss" (Kieyoomia was one of these draftees, per [4]).
- Equipped with 12 3-inch guns, .50-cal machine guns, 60-inch searchlights.
- Chosen for the Philippines partly because its men "virtually all spoke fluent Spanish"; arrived September 1941; defended Clark Field from Fort Stotsenburg.
- ~03:00, 8 Dec 1941: full alert after radio picked up Pearl Harbor news.
- 515th CA formed in Manila from one battalion of the 200th (8/19 Dec 1941).
- "The 200th CA surrendered on 9 April 1942 to the Japanese 14th Army."

[21] **Wikipedia, "Bataan Death March."** **[S/W]**
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan_Death_March
- Forcible transfer of "around 72,000 to 78,000" POWs: about 66,000 Filipino and about 12,000 American.
- From Mariveles and Bagac to Camp O'Donnell via San Fernando (rail to Capas); total ~105 km marched.
- Deaths before O'Donnell: "5,000 to 18,000 Filipino deaths and 500 to 650 American deaths."
- Maj. Gen. Edward P. King surrendered on 9 April 1942; quote attributed to King: "You men remember this. You did not surrender … you had no alternative but to obey my order."
- Homma executed 1946.

### PART C: THE NAVAJO CODE TALKERS

[22] **Adam Jevec, "Semper Fidelis, Code Talkers," *Prologue* (NARA), Winter 2001, vol. 33 no. 4.** **[S, NARA, cites RG 127 documents]**
URL: https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2001/winter/navajo-code-talkers.html
- Johnston got the idea "early in 1942, when he read a newspaper story about the army's use of several Native Americans during training maneuvers with an armored division in Louisiana."
- Went to the naval office in Los Angeles, then Eleventh Naval District (San Diego), then Maj. James E. Jones at Camp Elliott; assembled four Navajos in LA; met Jones at Camp Elliott 27 Feb 1942, "with the demonstration to occur the next day" (28 Feb 1942).
- Johnston's written proposal: Navajo remained "'unwritten' because an alphabet or other symbols of purely native origin" did not exist (RG 127, Entry 18A, box 600, folder 13).
- Vogel's six test messages; e.g. "Enemy expected to make tank and dive bomber attack at dawn" came back as "Enemy tank dive bomber expected to attack this morning."
- 6 March 1942: Vogel to the Commandant recommending 200 Navajos; Vogel called Navajo "completely unintelligible" to other tribes; "only twenty-eight Americans" had more than superficial knowledge; claimed Navajo was the only tribe not yet studied by Germans posing as students/art dealers/anthropologists.
- Col. Frank Halford (Director of Recruiting, 26 Mar 1942) objected to "combat directing officers depending on an order being translated."
- "On May 5, 1942, the first twenty-nine Navajos arrived at the recruit depot in San Diego."
- First code: "211 words"; examples "da-he-tih-hi" hummingbird = fighter plane, "gini" chicken hawk = dive bomber, A = "wol-la-chee" ant, E = "dzeh" elk.
- 20 July 1942: 200 more recommended. Johnston enlisted 2 Oct 1942 (vs 22 Sep in [26]), trained his first class in November.
- Alphabet grew "from twenty-six to forty-four terms"; vocabulary "211 … expanded to 411."
- New proposal: 303 more Navajos at 50 a month; quota later cut to 25 a month.

## Disagreements

1. **Kieyoomia's birth year / age.** Wikipedia [17], NM Blue Book [7], Find a Grave [19]: born Nov (21) 1919, died aged 77. Army enlistment card [4]: born 1917. Obituary [6]: "84" at death in Feb 1997 (→ born c.1912-13). AP 1995 [1][2]: "72" (→ c.1923). Best: the 1941 Army record [4] (closest to the event) gives 1917; the exact day 21 Nov is only in tertiary sources. Script: say "born around 1917-1919" or avoid a year; never say his age at capture to the month.
2. **How he joined.** Enlistment record [4]: drafted ("Selectee") 28 Mar 1941 at Santa Fe. McClain [8], Holiday [9], Gilbert [12]: "enlisted/joined the Army in March 1941". Blue Book [7]: "When World War II started, he joined the New Mexico National Guard". Best: [4] (primary): drafted March 1941, joined the 200th as one of its draftees at Fort Bliss [20].
3. **Rank.** "Sgt." in AP [1], Wikipedia, Find a Grave; "CPL" in the NARA POW file [3] and ADBC file [5]; "Pvt" in the unit roster in *Beyond Courage* [16] (likely his pre-war rank). Best: Corporal while a POW per NARA; he may have been promoted later. Script: "Corporal Joe Kieyoomia" for the war years.
4. **Birthplace / home.** "Navajo from Arizona" (Holiday [9]); "born on the reservation" (Blue Book [7]); Army nativity code 81 ≈ Arizona (my inference [4]); 1941 residence Santa Fe County, NM [4]; 1995 home near Sanostee, NM [2]; died at Shiprock [6]; three sisters at Blue Gap, AZ [6]. Best: born on the Navajo reservation, probably in Arizona; lived in the Sanostee/Shiprock area of NM after the war. Nothing places him at Shiprock before the war.
5. **Arrival in the Philippines.** 200th arrived September 1941 [20]; Gilbert [12] says "November 1941". Best: September (unit history).
6. **Where and how he was questioned.** AP 1995 [1]: "two Japanese women" brought Navajo words written in English letters. McClain [8], Holiday/Doris Duke [9], Rogers & Bartlit [10], Gilbert [12]: made to listen to (taped) radio broadcasts; McClain also: questioned in "the commander's quarters", wrote down the Navajo words he heard. McCoy 1981 [14] (unnamed POW): recordings plus wires twisted around his head. These are compatible (several sessions) but the wire detail is single-source and unnamed.
7. **Injuries.** AP [1]: frozen feet, could not use his right leg, near-daily beatings. McClain [8] and Durrett [11]: club broke his ribs and wrist; British doctor "Whitfield" bound his ribs (single source). Not contradictory; the doctor's name is [1x].
8. **How long in the snow.** AP [1]: "one hour", 27 °F, snow about 6 inches. Bataan memorial page [6] and Wikipedia [17]: "hours". Best: AP (his own words): one hour.
9. **Nagasaki on 9 August 1945 — the biggest conflict.**
   - AP 1995 [1]: in a Nagasaki prison cell, saved by its concrete walls, abandoned three days, freed by a Japanese officer. Blue Book [7] follows this.
   - McClain 1994 [8] (an earlier interview) and Gilbert 2008 [12]: transferred to "Matishima/Matsishima … 60 miles south of Nagasaki … the day before America dropped the atomic bomb."
   - NARA POW data file [3]: camp at liberation coded "Tokyo POW Camp (Shinjuku) Tokyo Bay Area" (could be a generic code, but it is not Nagasaki).
   - Best: unresolved. The script must attribute: "In 1995 he told the AP he was in a concrete cell in Nagasaki when the bomb fell. In an earlier account, he said he had been moved out of the city the day before. U.S. Army records list him as liberated from a Tokyo-area camp." Do NOT call him a confirmed hibakusha in the narrator's voice.
10. **Length of captivity.** 43 months (AP [1], obituary [6], WATM); "3½ years" (Wikipedia, *Open Wounds*); "April 1942 to 4 September 1945" (McClain [8]); "1,240 days" (French book [16]). 9 Apr 1942 → Sep 1945 ≈ 41 months; 43 months would run to about Nov 1945 (i.e., counting until he reached home). Best: "more than three years" / "about 41 months in Japanese hands".
11. **Did he know of the code talkers during the war?** AP [1]: learned of them "years later". Fact sheet [15] and McCoy [14]: an (unnamed) Bataan Navajo POW told "a code talker"/"his code-talker cousin" after the war: "I never figured out what you guys who got me into all that trouble were saying." These sources never name him; identification with Kieyoomia is likely but not stated. Script: "A Navajo prisoner from Bataan — almost certainly Kieyoomia — later told a code talker…"
12. **Death date / place.** Obituary [6]: died Monday 17 Feb 1997, Northern Navajo Medical Center, Shiprock; buried Memory Gardens (Farmington). Wikipedia "San Juan County" (consistent). Agreed.
13. **Number of Navajo code talkers.** "375 to 420" (DoD fact sheet [15], Prologue [22]); "about 400" (CIA [23], Bush [27]); "more than 400" (AP 2026 [30]); "more than 350" (PL 106-554 [28]); silver medals to "approximately 300" (Wikipedia [25]); "200 and 400" (McCoy 1981 [14]). Best: "about 400" (range 375-420).
14. **Original 29 vs 30.** CIA [23]: 30 approved, "one dropped out", 29 arrived. Wikipedia/Prologue: 29 sworn/arrived. Wikipedia Johnston [26]: 3 more added, 32 built the code. Best: "29" for the platoon; "29, later joined by three more" for the code builders.
15. **Swearing-in / arrival date.** 4 May 1942 Fort Wingate (Wikipedia [25][26]); 5 May 1942 arrival in San Diego (Prologue [22]). Both fit. Gilbert's timeline calls Fort Wingate "Arizona" (it is New Mexico).
16. **Code size.** 211 initial terms → 411 (CIA [23], Prologue [22]); "about 450" military terms (fact sheet [15]); June 1945 dictionary ~550 vocabulary entries by my count [24]. Best: "211 words at first, over 400 by the end."
17. **Speed claim.** 3-line message in 20 seconds vs 30 minutes for machines (fact sheet [15], Wikipedia [25]); 2.5 minutes vs "hours" (CIA [23], a later skeptical lieutenant's test). Different tests; quote each with its source.
18. **Iwo Jima messages.** "over 800 … all without error" by six talkers in the "first two days" (fact sheet [15]) = "800 error-free messages in a 48-hour period" (PL 106-554 [28]). CIA [23] drops the time limit. Consistent.
19. **Bodyguards ordered to kill code talkers.** Stated as fact by Congress (PL 106-554 [28]); told as hearsay by Bill Toledo (CBS [31], Wikipedia [25]); denied by Bonham ("No direct orders were ever given") and the Marine Corps per CBS [31]; NSA (Sioux code talker article, search summary only) calls it a myth spread by *Windtalkers*. No written order found. Script: bodyguards = documented; kill order = rumor, never documented, never carried out.
20. **Johnston's enlistment date and rank.** 22 Sep 1942 staff sergeant (Wikipedia [26]); 2 Oct 1942 (Prologue [22]); "Master Sergeant" (McCoy [14]). Best: "staff sergeant, autumn 1942".
21. **2001 Gold Medal ceremony.** Five living originals, four present (Prologue [22], Wikipedia [25]); Bush named five incl. Joe Palmer "represented by his son Kermit" [27]; CIA says "four surviving" [23]; Bush said he honored "21 Native Americans" [27] (likely those present or represented; Wikipedia says gold medals went to families of the 24 deceased). Best: "Five of the 29 were still alive. Four came to the Rotunda."
22. **Arisue quote.** Only found as paraphrase (fact sheet [15], Durrett [11], Prologue intro [22]); no direct quote with a primary source located. Script: paraphrase with attribution ("Japan's chief of army intelligence later said…"), no quote marks.
23. **Who first proposed / built the code.** Johnston is credited with the idea; Wikipedia [26] notes USMC documents credit BIA staff with insisting on a coded vocabulary and that the Navajo recruits built it. Script: idea = Johnston; code = the 29 (+3).
24. **The 200th's losses.** "Half of the 1,800" died (Blue Book timeline [7]); "only 900 returned alive … one-third of those died within one year" (Blue Book [7]). Consistent; state as "about half never came home".
