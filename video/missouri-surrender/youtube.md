## Description
```
At 8:56 on the morning of September 2, 1945, eleven Japanese men climbed aboard the USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay. One of them, the diplomat Toshikazu Kase, saw the little Rising Sun flags painted on the ship, one for every Japanese plane and submarine her gunners claimed to have destroyed, and was sure he had been brought there to be humiliated. Twenty minutes later General MacArthur called two freed prisoners of war to stand behind him while he signed: Lt. Gen. Jonathan Wainwright, who surrendered Corregidor, and Lt. Gen. Arthur Percival, who surrendered Singapore. On the way back, Kase began a report for the Emperor, and in it he asked a question about his own country.

This is the documented story of the eighteen days that ended World War II: the Atsugi rebellion, the white Betty bombers flown to Manila, the B-29 supply drops over the prison camps, the Navy's rescue of the Omori and Shinagawa prisoners ("You have no authority, period!"), MacArthur's landing at Atsugi, the road to Yokohama lined with soldiers who turned their backs, Wainwright flown out of Manchuria, and the 23 minutes on the Missouri, with MacArthur's own recorded voice.
```
