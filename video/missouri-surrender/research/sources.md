# Sources: the Missouri surrender, 15 Aug - 2 Sep 1945

## Why this topic
The reference channel WWII Box (@WWIIBox) is still running its "Japan Expected MacArthur to X. Then He Y." series
(23 Nov 2026: Let Them Starve 189K, Hang Hirohito 80K, Women's Vote 66K, Jail Its Soldiers 61K in 7 days) and has not
done the surrender itself. Other channels' Missouri videos were trending the same week: "Japan's Delegation Expected
Humiliation on the Deck of the Missouri" (237K in 3 weeks), "Japanese Officers Refused to Bow" (165K in 6 days),
"What MacArthur Did When Japanese Officers Refused to Bow" (420K). Owner, 23 Nov 2026: "let's make another MacArthur or
something that can go viral". Our documented version: the Japanese diplomat Kase's own account, the Navy's rescue of
the Tokyo Bay prisoners, and the two freed generals MacArthur called forward.

Full notes with page numbers and verbatim quotes: `research/facts_raw.md` (sources [1]-[23]); outline:
`research/story_outline.md`; media: `research/media_candidates.md`.

## Main sources
1. NHHC, MacArthur's surrender speech, typed text (Turner papers) [1]; his radio address, 2 Sep 1945 [2].
2. NHHC, deck plan with Nimitz's notes and his letter of 2 Sep 1945 [3]; H-Gram 053 (Sam Cox, 2020) [4]; "Japan
   Capitulates" photo pages with minute-by-minute times [5].
3. Toshikazu Kase, *Journey to the Missouri* (Yale, 1950), ch. 1 and pp. 262-65 [6]. Kase's account is one delegate's,
   written in 1950 under the occupation; the script says so (act 19).
4. *Reports of General MacArthur*, Vol. I Supplement (GHQ SCAP, 1966; public domain) [7]: Yomiuri list of fears (p. 24),
   Atsugi and Tench (pp. 26-27), MacArthur's words at Atsugi (p. 29), the road (p. 30), hamburgers and grapes (p. 31),
   B-29 drops (pp. 94-98), Simpson's radio, the roof messages, Nimitz's message and the Japanese protest (p. 100),
   32,624 prisoners (p. 101). Pages 24, 31 and 100 are shown on screen with the quoted lines highlighted.
5. *Admiral Halsey's Story* (1947), pp. 277-84 [8]: the escaped British prisoners, "Go ahead", the Omori gate, 794 by
   midnight, Umezu signing standing, the 450 planes.
6. Robert L. Eichelberger, *Our Jungle Road to Tokyo* (1950), pp. 261-64 [9].
7. Wainwright's own story, King Features serial (Hope Star, 15-17 Nov 1945) [10]; AP dispatches, 31 Aug 1945 [11].
8. National Institute for Defense Studies (Japan MoD), "'A Divine Nation Does Not Surrender!' The Atsugi Rebellion"
   (2026) [12]; Wikipedia, Yasuna Kozono [13].
9. NBC broadcast of the ceremony (archive.org; used only to check what MacArthur said, not played) [22]: settles the
   wording "Will General Wainwright and General Percival step forward and accompany me while I sign?" and names the
   freed prisoners on deck.

## Real voices (played)
- MacArthur on the Missouri, U.S. Navy Combat Bulletin No. 7 (NARA 80-MN-9034G): 567.6-584.4 s ("We are gathered
  here...") and 616.6-635.4 s ("I now invite the representatives...").
- MacArthur on the Missouri, United News 208-UN-171B (NARA 39079): 179.7-198.3 s ("The issues...") and 471.9-488.8 s
  ("Let us pray... These proceedings are closed.").
- The "malice or hatred" and "freedom, tolerance and justice" lines are not on any government film reel; the narrator
  reads them (the typed NHHC text) and they appear on quote cards.

## Disagreements (handled in the script)
- Shigemitsu's leg: right (NHHC, Wikipedia) or left (Kase, Halsey): the script says "a leg".
- Perry's flag: NHHC (Susquehanna) followed; the script says only "the flag Commodore Perry had flown ... in 1853".
- Flyover: the Navy's figure, 450 carrier planes, then "the B-29s" without a number.
- Prisoners freed the first night: Halsey's 794 used, attributed to Halsey; Simpson's "approximately 500" quoted from
  his radio.
- MacArthur's words at Atsugi: the 1945 GHQ wording [7] used.
- The sidearms story: told as "is said to", with "it is not in the records of 1945". Churchill's remark: not used.
- Umezu's suicide threat: Kase's hedge ("it was said") used.
- Who stalled at the table: Shigemitsu "seemed not to know where to sign" (H-Gram, NBC), not "stalling" (Halsey).
- The Emperor's reaction: second-hand twice; the script says "We know this only from Kase, who heard it from
  Shigemitsu."
- Kase's report: never published verbatim; the quote card uses his 1950 book's wording.
