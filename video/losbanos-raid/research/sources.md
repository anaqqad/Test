## Editor's notes (19 Nov 2026)
- Numbers in narration: 2,147 (camp roll, citation, Swing's 1700 report); the 1945 AP headline's 2,146 is shown as a headline only.
- Japanese dead: "reports in 1945 said ... to the last man, about 240; later historians 70-80" (Disagreements 2).
- U.S. dead: the TD captain + 2 more at Lecheria; guerrillas Castillo and Soler (Disagreements 3).
- Reprisals: told as a range with attribution (acting mayor 600-700; others to 1,500+); killings before the raid (Tadlak, Maahas) are stated;
  the question "was it revenge?" is answered with both sides and Ben Edwards' dissent (Disagreements 14-15). Calamba not attached to the raid.
- Henderson-only scenes are attributed in the script: rice sacks ("internees told Henderson"), lantern escape ("Ben Edwards described"),
  "quarter to seven" ("Muller later told Henderson"), Dorothy Still ("she later wrote").
- Maajas is spelled Maahas (the barangay's name today). Iwanaka: "does not appear again in any record we could find".
- Not used: Knute Rockne letter (cut for length), Sascha Jansen (Santo Tomas), Ang children photos (only the caption card).

## Sources

### PART A: ARMY RECORDS AND OFFICIAL HISTORIES

[1] **Maj. Edward M. Flanagan Jr., *The Angels: A History of the 11th Airborne Division, 1943-1946* (Washington: Infantry Journal Press, 1948), ch. 10 "The Los Baños Raid", pp. 93-98.** Public domain (archive.org, CC PD mark). **[P/S — division history written by an 11th Airborne officer three years after the raid]**
URL: https://archive.org/details/TheAngels11thAirborneDiv (full text: https://archive.org/download/TheAngels11thAirborneDiv/TheAngels11thAirborneDiv_djvu.txt)
- Mission date: "ON 3 February, the Division Commander had been ordered to attack, as soon as possible, the Japanese internment camp at Los Banos, and to free if possible the 2,147 civilians and prisoners of war held there."
- Not until 19 February "could think about Los Banos." Camp "some fifty miles behind the Japanese lines" (other sources: 20-25 miles; see Disagreements).
- Results as given in 1948: "We rescued 2,147 internees. Two hundred and forty-seven Japanese were killed. We lost only two men killed and four wounded. Of all the internees, only one was wounded, and he very lightly."
- Five phases: I secret planning; II Recon Platoon + guerrillas infiltrate by banca; III B Company parachute assault; IV amphibious attack by 1st Bn 511th; V diversionary ground attack. Estimate: "between 8,000 and 15,000 Japanese troops were within four hours' march of the camp."
- Pete Miles: "Five days before the operation, the guerrilla scouts brought back an escaped internee. Mr. Peter Miles, an engineer who had broken from the camp, joined a guerrilla spy, and returned to the Division command post at Paranaque. He corrected the map of the camp…" He marked "the quarters of all internees, the guard houses, the position of the sentries, and the several known blockhouses and pillboxes", and "closely estimated the number who would require evacuation by litter." "The assistance which this brave man rendered cannot be too highly praised."
- Plan approved "without change" by XIV Corps commander Maj. Gen. Oscar Griswold, target date 23 February.
- "Nine C-47 airplanes of the 65th Troop-Carrier Squadron" and "fifty-nine noisy and ungainly amphtracs" (other sources: 54).
- "There was no moon on the night of 22-23 February 1945"; amtrac course "seven and four-tenths miles long, with a right-angled turn to the right in the middle of it."
- Midnight scare: Black Widow night planes reported "Hundreds of trucks, with lights ablaze" entering the camp. "the General decided to ignore the reports, and not pass the message on to the participating troops." At 0100 Swing drove to Mamatid beach.
- Recon Platoon "about thirty-two men and an officer, reinforced by about eighty Filipino guerrillas"; crossed the night of the 21st, which "was stormy"; on the night of the 22nd "about seven hours of laborious plodding across the flooded rice paddies."
- "At thirty seconds before 0700 on the morning of the 23d, two white columns of phosphorus smoke rose from the drop zone behind the camp, and two identical white columns rose from the beach" (Rottman [13] and Wikipedia [16] say green smoke).
- Killer parties got "as close, in some cases, as three feet from the assigned Japanese sentry". A party "set fire to the barracks in which were stored the arms and ammunition of the prison guards."
- Ringler was briefed "Three days before"; DZ "surrounded on three sides by trees, and on the other by railroad track. Across the field ran a high-tension wire." Parachutes "flown in from Leyte the next day". The company "slept under the wings of their planes" at Nichols Field.
- "Precisely at 0700, watchers on the ground saw Lieutenant Ringler's parachute snap open over the camp… In less than a minute, all men were on the ground. None was injured."
- B Company reached the camp "in less than fifteen minutes", destroyed "the three pillboxes"; "All the Jap garrison lay dead" (contradicted by Konishi's own escape [10]).
- Amtracs: first wave reached the beach "At 0700"; "two amphtracs were sunk"; one internee "received a grazing flesh wound".
- Diversion: "At 0700 precisely, the first artillery round … landed on Lecheria Hill." "The captain in command of the tank destroyer company fell, his forehead pierced by a Japanese bullet — our first fatal casualty of the day." Later: "Two men had been killed in this fight, and two had been wounded."
- 1700: Swing reported to Griswold "that 2,147 individuals had been transported to new Bilibid Prison with one slightly wounded casualty; that our casualties had been two killed in action and four wounded".
- MacArthur's radio to the division: "An operation such as that performed today will gladden the hearts of soldiers throughout the world."
- Anecdote (unattributed): an old lady, glum: "Night after night I've dreamed of this day, and in all my dreams of rescue, I was rescued by Marines. You're not Marines." Treat as camp folklore (compare [11]).
- Note: this 1948 chapter credits the guerrillas only as reinforcements of the Recon Platoon and never names a Filipino.

[9] **United States of America v. Sadaaki Konishi, U.S. Military Commission, Manila, 23 Nov 1946 - 15 Jan 1947 (case no. given as "51J-104279"), as quoted from the NARA trial record in Henderson [11], Epilogue.** **[P via S — I have not seen the record itself]**
- Commission: Col. John L. Rice (president), Col. William Hambry, Maj. Mamerto Montemayor (Philippine Army), Maj. Raymond Ballweg ("a veteran of Bataan"), Maj. Hugh Fkees [sic, OCR]. Chief prosecutor Frank J. O'Neill. Konishi had "advanced pulmonary tuberculosis with cavitation" and wore a surgical mask in court.
- O'Neill: Konishi carried out an order by Gen. Fujishige to "ruthlessly wipe out hundreds of Filipino citizens . . . anybody who falls in their path, regardless of whether they are men, women, children or babies."
- Witness Fernando Bernardo (Los Baños resident), 28 Feb 1945: ordered with six other men to bury bodies; found a boy of two or three still alive: "He was sitting up and one of his hands was tied to his mother's body. I heard him call, 'Mama,' and tell her he was hungry." Konishi told them to turn their backs; "I noticed the wound of a bayonet."
- Witness Ang Kim Ling (Chinese boy, barrio Anos, 6 Mar 1945): "They bayoneted us." He received four wounds; his six-year-old sister sixteen. "I heard my sister whining, and I crawled out from under the dead bodies and dug her out." Recognised bodies: "My grandfather and grandmother. My father and mother. My uncles and aunts." Twenty-six killed that night (a Signal Corps caption [40] says nineteen others of the Ang family).
- Acting mayor Aurelio Almasan (made mayor 12 Feb 1945 after the real mayor fled): in a deposition he said massacres "started on February 21, 1945—two days before the raid", when 300 soldiers came down from Mount Makiling; estimated "six hundred to seven hundred residents were killed in and around Los Banos and the surrounding barrios between February 21 and March 6."
- Capt. Chusiro Kudo (Kudo Unit, Saito Battalion, 17th Infantry, 8th Division): "The order came from [General] Fujishige that all people who had connections with guerrillas must be killed." Arrived at the college "around 4 A.M. on February 27"; told his men everyone on the college grounds was to be killed "because we thought that area was a guerrilla area." On the church: "I never heard them."
- U.S. Army War Crimes Report No. 214 (quoted in the record): "More than sixty residents … took refuge in the Roman Catholic church on the college grounds… The remainder refused to leave the church and were immolated"; Maajas, 22 Feb 1945: 45 killed from five families; Tadlak, 21 Feb 1945: 65 killed from ten families; "An estimated 1,500 citizens of Calamba … were massacred between February 12-15, 1945" (i.e. BEFORE the raid).
- Food evidence: about 400 g of food daily by Sept 1944, "150 grams or less by February 1945"; internees "lost on average a total of thirty to sixty pounds in three months".
- Paul Hennesen saw Konishi hand his white-handled pistol to a soldier who "went up to George and blew his brains out" (George Louis, shot re-entering camp about 6:45 a.m., 28 Jan 1945).
- Verdict 8:30 a.m., 15 Jan 1947: guilty of Spec. 1 (ordering the killing of "James Gardner, a child … tied to the body of his dead mother", 28 Feb 1945); Spec. 2 (permitting the killing of "one named and sixty unnamed" Chinese and Filipino civilians, 6 Mar 1945); Spec. 3 (attempted killing of Ang Kim Ling and Ang Elisa); Spec. 4 NOT guilty (burning homes); Spec. 5 guilty of "aiding and abetting a policy of general starvation … between August 1, 1944, and February 23, 1945, thereby causing the death of four named Americans and numerous unnamed"; Spec. 6 guilty (George Louis). Sentence: "The Commission sentences you to death by hanging."

[10] **Sworn statement of Sadaaki Konishi to Capt. Leslie J. Gobeyn, Army War Crimes Detachment, 28 Nov 1945 (trial exhibit), as quoted in Henderson [11], Epilogue.** **[P via S]**
- On rations: "In my opinion, that was not enough. If it had continued, they might starve. However, the allotment was the order from headquarters, and I just complied with that order."
- Q: did the order "cause starvation"? Konishi: "Yes, actually it caused deaths."
- On 23 Feb: "I was still in bed at that time, and then I heard that paratroops had landed." He hid in a ravine "until about 12 o'clock that night", with "About seven of us with our [camp] commandant Iwanaka."
- 27 Feb: sent to Los Baños "to investigate the routes taken by the American tanks"; "Saito did not think that 2,000 people could be transported on these amphibian trucks".
- "I heard about an order from [Fujishige] to eliminate all guerrillas." Q: "Did you hear Fujishige make a speech saying that each man must kill seventy people?" A: "Yes, he gathered various battalion commanders, company commanders and other officers when he made that statement."
- Denied the George Louis killing ("I swear to God I wasn't there"), blamed "Lt. Kaseno, the captain of the guards", and denied the 6 March massacre.

### PART B: THE BOOKS

[11] **Bruce Henderson, *Rescue at Los Baños: The Most Daring Prison Camp Raid of World War II* (New York: William Morrow, 2015).** Full text read via archive.org. **[S — best-researched narrative; uses NARA trial files, Konishi's POW and Sugamo records, author interviews with Muller and Santos, Danner and Sams memoirs]**
URL: https://archive.org/details/rescue-at-los-banos-the-most-daring-prison-camp-raid-of-world-war-2
- Camp origin: 800 able-bodied men from Santo Tomas transferred "on May 14" (1943) to build the new camp. More than 300 more trucked down on 7 Apr 1944 (incl. Margaret Sherk/Sams).
- Iwanaka (from July 1944): "spent most of his days in a kimono and sandals, puttering around in the garden outside his office, potting geraniums, painting watercolors … and writing haiku." Konishi "was in effect its self-appointed dictator because Iwanaka was never known to countermand a single order of Konishi's."
- Konishi was "twenty-eight years old, five-foot-four, and 135 pounds", from Fukuoka Prefecture, a prewar farmer.
- Rice sacks [1x]: Konishi let starving internees carry the 100-lb rice sacks from the warehouse for over an hour, then "ordered them to carry all the rice back to the warehouse."
- Dr. Dana Nance: October 1944 intake fell to "the 'appalling figure' of 881 calories—a 65 percent decline from September"; 380 new beriberi cases; guards estimated at 3,000-4,000 calories a day. Nance to the commandant, 1 Nov 1944: "THE CAMP URGENTLY NEEDS MORE FOOD."
- Thanksgiving 1944: Konishi blocked Red Cross holiday packages: "Before I'm done … you'll be eating dirt." [from internee accounts]
- Sign in camp: "KEEP OFF THE GRASS. WE EAT IT."
- Nance in late Nov: "If you fail to remedy this situation, it can only mean that you are deliberately adopting a starvation policy." Konishi: "Nothing can be done."
- "With the death rate now averaging seven people per week … The number of gravediggers had to be doubled to six."
- Pat Hell found "about thirty feet outside the northern boundary", hit three times, "still clutched a scrawny dead chicken". Ten days later George Louis was shot crawling back under the fence.
- Escape: "At 9 P.M., on February 18, the three men … walked out the door with a lantern. Pretending to be ill, Miles, limping and groaning, was in the middle". Crawled under the wire behind the hospital; "A manned sentry post was located not more than forty feet away." Ben kicked "an empty tin can. The noise was deafening." Taken to the PQOG camp at Tranca (Romeo Espino).
- Miles ("Prentice 'Pete' Miles … a rough-and-tumble Texan in his early thirties", once sent to Sumatra by the St. Louis Zoo to collect animals) and Edwards "had paced off distances in the camp".
- KEY SCENE: Muller asked about routine; Miles mentioned calisthenics. "'Every morning?' Muller asked. 'Yes, and always at the same time.' 'What time?' 'They start exercising at quarter to seven.'" Exercises "started at 6:45 A.M. and ended at 7:15 A.M."; guns "stacked and locked in a connecting room"; guards wore "only sandals and traditional fundoshi undergarments"; chant "Tenno Heika Banzai!" Muller moved H-hour "from 8 A.M. to 7 A.M."
- Muller estimated "a hundred and fifty to two hundred Japanese soldiers were at the camp."
- Nurse Dorothy Still, minutes before 7 a.m. [M — from her memoir *What a Way to Spend a War*]: "We could do calisthenics, too … if we were as well fed as they are." A patient then "wondered out loud about the columns of white smoke rising nearby."
- Jerry Sams, "down to ninety pounds", climbed a roof and saw the exercise field "now contained scores of half-naked dead bodies."
- Margie Whitaker asked if the Marines had landed: "Sorry, sister, Army paratroopers."
- Casualties: Soule force "three KIAs—in addition to the tank commander, two 188th enlisted men, J. C. Doiron and Vernal R. McMurtrey"; "No other U.S. soldiers were killed in the raid on the prison camp. Two guerrillas from the Hunters ROTC, Atanacio Castillo and Anselmo Soler, were killed".
- Swing to his father-in-law Gen. Peyton March: "We pulled off a raid yesterday that would have made Knute Rockne envious in his heyday. As you know, it is seldom that everything clicks, but this time there wasn't a single miscue."
- MacArthur press release: "Nothing could be more satisfying to a soldier's heart than this rescue. I am deeply grateful. God was certainly with us today."
- Iwo: "The lack of newspaper headlines about the prison camp raid was not a case of wartime censorship. For on February 23, 1945, the same day as the raid, a combat photographer named Joe Rosenthal snapped an image…"
- Gustavo "Tabo" Ingles (Hunters ROTC inspector general): arrested 23 June 1943; "water cure", beatings, thumbs; at Fort Santiago "electric shocks" and "bamboo slivers inserted beneath his fingernails"; sentenced to 13 years; broken out of New Bilibid with 50 others by Hunters ROTC on 24 June 1944.
- George Gray (internee committee) to Ingles: "Under international law, we are a civilian internment facility". Asked: "Do you have any way to transport more than two thousand people, some sick and feeble, out of enemy territory to U.S. lines?"
- Epilogue (Konishi): captured 25 Sept 1945; born 19 Jan 1916; chaplain John P. Wallace's 1983 letter claimed he baptized and executed Konishi on 17 June 1947 — "the gospel" repeated by Flanagan ("There can be no question … that Sadaaki Konishi went to his death by hanging on 17 June 1947"). Henderson: "Sadaaki Konishi did not die that day." MacArthur stayed the execution on 14 Aug 1947 (Supreme Court appeal threat); Konishi entered Sugamo 20 Sept 1948 (TB, moved to the 361st Station Hospital); 12 Apr 1949 stay vacated; hanged by 1st Lt. Ellis Coker: "The trap was sprung at thirty-three minutes after midnight, April 30, 1949. Sadaaki Konishi was pronounced dead fourteen minutes later."
- Dramatis personae: Ben Edwards back in the Philippines within a year, farmed near Los Baños, hunted with ex-guerrilla friends, died 2010 aged 91; Pete Miles lived in Manila, died of a heart attack 1962; Freddy Zervoulakos moved to the U.S., 10 years U.S. Navy, died 1998 aged 74; Muller later deputy CG 101st Airborne in Vietnam, retired brigadier general 1971 ("I only wish the general had said that to me back in 1945"); Dr. Dana Nance awarded the Medal of Freedom 1946, died 1987; Skau killed in a plane crash approaching Okinawa after ordering Santos and Squires off the plane; Squires married internee Margie Whitaker (wedding photo 1947); Dorothy Still Danner fainted on Leyte before the nurses' photo, Bronze Star, died 2001; Jay Vanderpool 1984 oral history: "The guerrillas did an excellent job; everyone did. There was enough glory for everyone involved that day."; baby Lois McCoy (born three days before) became a nurse; amtrac gunner Dwight Clark thought hot shell cases had scarred her and called her in 1998: "I heard you cry. Do you have a scar on your face?"; the 672nd got a Presidential Unit Citation in 2011.
- Roster: typed by internee Carol Terry "on a 1920s manual typewriter", retrieved by George Gray, "recognized by the U.S. government as the official camp census. A total of 2,147".

## Disagreements

1. **How many were freed.** 2,147: Flanagan 1948 [1], DUC citation [3], CMH [4], Henderson book [11] (official census typed by Carol Terry), Wikipedia [16], NHI marker [22], Bell [25], Breuer [34]. 2,146: AP 24 Feb 1945 [5][6] (with a nationality breakdown that sums exactly to 2,146), Henderson's own 2015 article [12], Lucas [35]. 2,142: Holm [21] ("Six people died and one baby … was born afterward"). 2,132: Gaerlan [24]. 2,130: planners' figure in Rottman [13]. 2,122: Flanagan 1986 [14]. 2,200: 188th GIR narrative [40] and Dengler [33]. "about 2,500": 1962 Hunters plaque [22]. "two thousand four hundred": Morgan's memory [28]. **Best:** say "about 2,100" or "2,147 on the camp roll"; 2,146/2,147 are the same count give or take one person (the roll was typed days before the raid; births and deaths after it explain small differences).
2. **Japanese killed.** 247 (Flanagan 1948 [1]); "the commander, his staff and 243 guards" (AP 1945 [5][6]); "nearly 250" annihilated (CMH [4]); "70-80" (Rottman [13], Wikipedia [16]); "as many as 80" (Henderson [12]). The 1945 claim of a garrison killed "to the last man" is false: Konishi, Iwanaka and about five others hid in a ravine and escaped ([10], [13], [18]). **Best:** Rottman's 70-80 inside the camp, plus unknown losses to the diversion force; say "dozens of guards were killed; the commandant and his deputy got away."
3. **U.S. and Filipino casualties.** 2 killed, 4 wounded (Flanagan 1948 [1], Swing's 1700 report); "two killed … two wounded" in the diversion (same book); 3 killed, 2 wounded (Henderson [12], Wikipedia [16]); 3 KIA = TD company captain + Doiron + McMurtrey (Henderson [11]); Rottman [13]: two Company B (188th) men + TD captain killed, plus an HQ soldier who "died the next day"; Flanagan 1986 [14]: "without the loss of life or limb"; Lucas [35]: 2 killed, 3 wounded. All agree: no U.S. soldier killed inside the camp itself; the dead fell in the Soule diversion at Lecheria Hills. Filipinos: 2 Hunters killed (Atanacio Castillo, Anselmo Soler), 4 wounded ([11][13][16]). Internees: one lightly wounded ([1], [40]); "two" (AP [5]); "three slightly" (Lucas [35]). **Best:** "three American soldiers and two Filipino guerrillas died; none of them inside the camp, except the two guerrillas."
4. **Times on 23 Feb.** Amtracs: boarded/left 0400 (Wikipedia [16]) vs moved out 0515 (Rottman [13] timeline, Miskimon [18], Holm [21]). Smoke and jump: "thirty seconds before 0700" white phosphorus smoke and the jump "Precisely at 0700" (Flanagan 1948 [1]); green smoke spotted at 0658 (Rottman [13]); green light at 6:58 (Miskimon [18]); recon fire opened about 0657:30 (Holm [21]). End: last amtracs off at 1500 (Wikipedia/Rottman), "completely finished by 1330" (188th narrative [40]), all at Muntinlupa "by 1700" (AAR [40], Flanagan [1]). **Best:** smoke 6:58, jump 7:00, firing started a minute or two early; last people off the beach mid-afternoon.
5. **Smoke colour.** White phosphorus (Flanagan 1948 [1]) vs green (Rottman [13], Wikipedia [16]). Unresolved; avoid naming the colour.
6. **Number of amtracs.** 54 LVT-4s (Rottman, Wikipedia, Miskimon, Holm); 52 (188th narrative [40]); 59 (Flanagan 1948 [1]); "Sixty" (Henderson [12]); 67 moved on 21-22 Feb (U.S. interrogator's figure in [2]). **Best:** 54.
7. **Jump altitude and numbers.** 500 ft (Rottman/Wikipedia), 400 ft (Salecker [19], Holm [21]), 400-500 (Miskimon). Jumpers: "About 140" (Salecker), 132 parachutes (Holm), 170 paratroopers (Henderson [12]). Squadron: 65th TCS (Flanagan [1], Rottman) vs "75th" (Holm). **Best:** about 130-140 men from nine C-47s at roughly 400-500 feet.
8. **How far behind the lines.** "some fifty miles" (Flanagan 1948 [1]); 25 miles (DUC [3], AAR [40]); 20 miles (Wikipedia [16]). **Best:** 25 miles (the official citation).
9. **When the raid was ordered.** 3 Feb (Flanagan 1948 [1], Wikipedia [16]); MacArthur ordered it 12 Feb (Henderson [12]); Eichelberger told Swing about the camp 12 Feb (Holm [21]); 188th got orders 20 Feb (Dengler [33]). Compatible: assigned early, planned in earnest from mid-February.
10. **Pete Miles' escape.** Night of 18 Feb with Edwards and Zervoulakos (Henderson [11], Rottman [13]); Miskimon [18] adds "Jack Connors" [1x]; *Free Philippines* [7] says six escaped internees joined the guerrillas. Arrival at HQ: "Monday, the 19th" (Flanagan 1986 [14]); "before noon" (Rottman [13]); "Five days before the operation" (Flanagan 1948 [1] = 18 Feb). Henderson [11] alone has the lantern/fake-illness/tin-can details (from Edwards' 1988 oral history) — mark as Edwards' account. Zervoulakos' age: 19 (Wikipedia) vs 20 (Henderson; died 1998 aged 74). **Best:** escaped night of 18 Feb, reached the division on 19 Feb.
11. **Who planned it / whose raid was it.** U.S. accounts: Muller (G-2) and Quandt (G-3) planned it, Swing approved, Griswold signed off ([1], [11]); Flanagan 1948 never names a Filipino. Filipino markers: the joint guerrilla force "organized by Lt. Col. Gustavo Ingles" and the "initial raid was led by Lt.Col. Honorio Guerrero and the ROTC Hunters", Company B "assisting" (NHI 2005 [22]); 1962 plaque says Hunters freed the camp with help from "some American airborne soldiers" [22]. Middle view: Lt. Stilz said it "could not have succeeded without" guerrilla intelligence and action [33]; Vanderpool: "enough glory for everyone" [11]. **Best:** U.S. plan and transport, guerrilla intelligence, guides and much of the perimeter killing; tell both versions explicitly.
12. **Iwanaka and Konishi's ranks and roles.** Konishi: Warrant Officer (Rottman [13], Henderson [11], trial [9]) vs "Lieutenant" (Arthur [15], Wikipedia [17]); Rottman notes he "is sometimes described as a lieutenant." Iwanaka "senile"/"dementia"/geraniums and haiku — internee impressions only ([11][13][18]). **Best:** "Warrant Officer Sadaaki Konishi, the camp's supply officer and real power".
13. **Konishi's capture, trial and execution — the biggest correction.** Capture: spotted "In July 1945" by an ex-internee golfer in a POW work detail (Rottman [13]) vs "captured by U.S. forces in Luzon on September 25, 1945" (Henderson [11], from his POW record). Trial: "tried in January 1947" (Rottman) vs began 23 Nov 1946, verdict 15 Jan 1947 (Henderson). Death: "hanged in June" 1947 (Rottman), "hanged in 1947" (Miskimon [18], Wikipedia raid article [16]), "17 June 1947" (chaplain Wallace's 1983 letter, Flanagan), vs hanged at Sugamo, Tokyo, 00:33 on 30 April 1949 (Henderson, from Army records; Wikipedia Konishi article [17]); Arthur [15] already reported "Konishi was not hanged on June 17, 1947". **Best:** Henderson (documents: MacArthur's stay of 14 Aug 1947, Sugamo entry 20 Sep 1948, execution order 27 Apr 1949). The 1947 story is a documented myth — use it as a payoff.
14. **The reprisal death toll.** About 1,500 (Wikipedia [16], Miskimon [18], Gaerlan NWW2M [24]); "Up to 1,500" (Rottman [13]); "over 1,500" (Roberts [20]); "an estimated 1,400" tied to stilts (Salecker [19]); "over 2,000" (Gaerlan [23]); 600-700 in Los Baños and barrios 21 Feb-6 Mar (acting mayor Almasan's deposition, via [9]); named incidents: 65 at Tadlak (21 Feb), 45 at Maajas (22 Feb), 60+ burned in the college church (27 Feb), 26 at Anos (6 Mar) ([9]); "Seventy people hiding in a church" (Rottman). **Best:** "hundreds — local and Army estimates run from about 600 to 1,500". Do not attach the separate Calamba figure (1,500, Feb 12-15) to the raid.
15. **Was it retaliation for the raid?** Yes, "nothing more than revenge" (Rottman [13]); "in retaliation" (Wikipedia [16]); "That very same night" (Gaerlan [24]) vs "Starting two nights after" (Rottman) vs "A few days after" (Wikipedia). Against a simple cause-and-effect: killings at Tadlak (21 Feb) and Maajas (22 Feb) and the Calamba massacre (12-15 Feb) came BEFORE the raid ([9], Report No. 214); Almasan dated the start to 21 Feb [9]; Fujishige's anti-guerrilla orders predate it (Nov 1944 per Gaerlan [23]; captured order in [2]); Fujishige said the raid changed nothing in his dispositions [2]; Ben Edwards: "I do not believe the atrocities in and around the college were the direct result of the liberation" [20]. The 27 Feb attack on the college grounds and the 28 Feb / 6 Mar killings tied to Konishi clearly followed the raid [9][10]. **Best (script):** "The killing around Los Baños had already begun. After the raid, it got worse — and it reached the camp itself."
16. **Who did the killing.** Saito Battalion / Kudo Unit, 17th Infantry ([9], [20], [23]); 17th Infantry + Kempeitai + Filipino Ganaps (Rottman [13]); Japanese with Makapili (Wikipedia [16]). Compatible.
17. **Fujishige's rank, command and fate.** Colonel commanding the 17th Infantry and the Fuji Force (CMH [4], Rottman [13], charge sheet [29], Gaerlan [23]); "Lt. Gen." (Flanagan 1948 [2], Holm [21] who also wrongly makes him 8th Division commander — that was Lt. Gen. Shizuo Yokoyama per Wikipedia [16]/Gaerlan); "Major General" (generals.dk [31], promoted 10 June 1945; Henderson photo caption). Fate: death penalty (Flanagan [2]), hanged (Batangas blog [30]), executed 17 July 1946 (generals.dk [31], weak). **Best:** "Colonel Masatoshi Fujishige"; say he was sentenced to death; give no execution date unless a stronger source turns up.
18. **The trench / planned massacre.** Trench or holes seen (Henderson [11][12], Rottman [13] — "may have been air raid slit trenches or mass graves"); Espino's "scheduled for massacre" radio message is documented ([13][14][15]); Arthur [15]: "It was never positively established that the massacre feared by Espino was in fact planned". **Best:** feared, reported, never proven.
19. **Rations.** About 900 cal/day from late 1944 (Rottman [13]); 881 cal in Oct 1944 (Nance via Henderson [11]); "under 450 calories" by Jan 1945 (Holm [21]); 400 g/day Sept 1944 → 150 g or less by Feb 1945 (trial [9]); ~500 g of palay per person per three days (prosecutor's question to Konishi [10]). Deaths: "one or two" a day (Rottman, Miskimon) vs "seven people per week" (Henderson). **Best:** "under 900 calories by autumn 1944, far less by February; people were dying every week."
20. **When the camp opened.** Dec 1942 (Salecker [19], Holm [21]) vs first 800 men transferred May 1943 (Henderson [11]: 14 May; Rottman [13]; Wikipedia [16]; NHI marker "1943" [22]). **Best:** May 1943.
21. **The 1945 overshadowing by Iwo Jima.** Rottman [13], Henderson [11][12] and Flanagan 1986 [14] say the flag raising crowded the raid out of the papers. But the raid was front-page news on 24 Feb 1945 in the AP report, often in the same headline as Iwo ([5][6]); Rosenthal's photo was only published on Sunday 25 Feb [37]. **Best:** "It made the front pages for a day — then the photograph from Iwo Jima took over, and Los Baños faded from memory."
22. **Recognition of the units.** DUC by War Department GO 71, 22 Aug 1945 [3] vs "In January 1948" (Rottman [13]); Henderson calls it the Presidential Unit Citation (the later name). The 672nd's citation came in 2011 [11].
23. **Victim names.** "Pat Hell" (Henderson [11]) / "Howard 'Pat' Hell" (prosecutor in [10]) / "Jan Howard Hell" (Signal Corps caption [40]); killed 20 Jan 1945 (prosecutor [10]; bill item 122 [39]). Ang family: 26 killed that night (trial [9]) vs "nineteen others" of the family (caption [40]) — both can be true (not all victims were Angs).
24. **"Annihilated to the last man" vs escapes.** 1945 reports ([5], [40]) and Flanagan 1948 [1] say the garrison was wiped out; Konishi's statement [10], Rottman [13] and Miskimon [18] show Iwanaka, Konishi and a few others escaped and others fled to the hills.
25. **Sascha Jansen.** She was a Santo Tomas internee, not Los Baños (search results; her own accounts). Do not use her for this episode.

## Story beats

Target ~4,300 words / 28-30 min. Open loops: **(A)** How did the Americans know the guards would be unarmed at that exact minute? **(B)** Konishi ran into a ravine — what happened to him? **(C)** The marker at the camp today tells a different story of who freed it. **(D)** The town that fed the prisoners — what happened to it after the Americans left? (+ a small loop: why did nobody remember it — Iwo Jima.)

1. **Cold open — 6:45 a.m., 23 Feb 1945.** On a field inside the camp, off-duty guards line up in loincloths for their daily calisthenics; their rifles are locked in the arms room [11][13]. In the camp hospital, a starving Navy nurse watches them: "We could do calisthenics, too, if we were as well fed as they are." [11 — her memoir]. A patient notices columns of smoke. 6:58: green light. Nine C-47s at a few hundred feet; parachutes [13][18]. Freeze. "Someone had told the Americans exactly when these men would be undressed. He had crawled out under the wire five nights earlier." (Loop A.) (~250 words)
2. **Title + the number.** 2,147 names on a typed roll; the Americans, Britons, Dutch, Poles, nuns, missionaries, a 3-day-old baby; 25 miles behind Japanese lines; an estimated 8,000-10,000 Japanese troops within a few hours' march [3][5][11][13][16]. Tease Loops B-D in one line each: the man who ran the camp got away; the Filipinos tell it differently; the town paid. [1][5][13][22]
3. **How civilians ended up in a prison camp.** Manila 1942, Santo Tomas; May 1943: 800 men sent to build a camp on an agricultural college by a lake and a volcano [11][16][28]. Ben Edwards, Pan Am mechanic [11].
4. **Hell's Half-Acre and Vatican City.** Who was inside: families, children born in camp, 11 Navy nurses, ~300 Catholic religious [16][21]. The first two commandants were "lenient" [13].
5. **July 1944: Konishi.** Iwanaka's geraniums and haiku; Konishi the real power; the rice sacks carried back [11][13] (mark as internee accounts). Calories: 881 in October [11]; "THE CAMP URGENTLY NEEDS MORE FOOD" [11].
6. **"You'll be eating dirt."** Thanksgiving packages blocked; "KEEP OFF THE GRASS. WE EAT IT."; dogs and cats; the gravediggers doubled; "Nothing can be done." [11][13][28] Sally Morgan: people died "with food under their beds" [28].
7. **Freedom Week.** 7 Jan 1945 the guards walk away; flags go up; a week later they come back angrier: curfew, shoot on sight [13]. Pat Hell found with a dead chicken in his hand [11]; George Louis executed at the gate on 28 Jan [9][40]. The trench [11][13].
8. **The other side of the wire: the guerrillas.** Gustavo Ingles — tortured at Fort Santiago, broken out of prison by his own Hunters ROTC [11]. Hunters, PQOG, Marking's, Huks, the Chinese 48th Squadron already watching the camp [16][22][26]. Freddy Zervoulakos slips out to meet Ingles, 12 Feb [13].
9. **The committee says no.** George Gray: "we are a civilian internment facility"; the question nobody could answer — how do you move 2,000 starving people? [11][13]
10. **Three men and a lantern (pays Loop A, part 1).** 18 Feb, 9 p.m.: Miles fakes illness, the hospital, the gap under the wire, the tin can [11 — Edwards' account]; banca across the lake [13].
11. **"Quarter to seven" (pays Loop A).** 19 Feb, Parañaque: Muller's debrief; H-hour moved to 7:00 [11][14]. The plan: recon by banca, a parachute company, 54 amtracs, a diversion down Highway 1 [1][13]. Swing [36].
12. **"Scheduled for massacre."** 21 Feb radio message [13][14]; the stormy banca crossing; the schoolhouse at Nanhaya, Edwards drawing the camp on the blackboard [1][16]; the night march through flooded paddies [1]. Midnight: the Black Widow report of trucks — Swing tells no one [1]. (Note honestly: never proven a massacre was planned [15].)
13. **7:00 (replay of the cold open, now complete).** Sentry fires early; Hunters with bolos; smoke; the jump; amtracs on the beach; Lecheria Hills [1][13][21]. Inside: Sally under the bed, "paratrooper boots" [28]; "Sorry, sister, Army paratroopers" [11]; barracks burned to get people moving [16][28]; baby Lois [11][21].
14. **Getting 2,000 people out.** Two lifts across the lake; the beach under mortar fire; Burgess' decision; Soule's men die at Lecheria Hills; Castillo and Soler, the two guerrillas killed [1][11][13]. 17:00: 2,147 at Muntinlupa [1]. MacArthur: "God was certainly with us today." [5]
15. **The same morning on Iwo Jima (small loop paid).** Front pages 24 Feb: "Yanks Free 2,146 … and Break Iwo Stalemate" [6]; the photo published 25 Feb [37]; Los Baños fades [13]. 
16. **The town (pays Loop D).** Killings had already begun on 21-22 Feb (Tadlak, Maajas) [9]; after the raid, the Saito Battalion; 27 Feb the college church [9]; families tied to house stilts [13]; the child tied to his dead mother [9]; Ang Kim Ling [9]. Toll: hundreds, estimates to 1,500 [9][13][16]. Ben Edwards' dissent [20]; Rottman on blame [13]. Fujishige's order [2].
17. **Konishi's escape and trial (Loop B part 1).** Hid in the ravine with Iwanaka; back in Los Baños 27 Feb [10]; captured Sept 1945; trial Nov 1946, the mask, the witnesses; "Yes, actually it caused deaths" [10]; sentence 15 Jan 1947 [9]. Iwanaka was never caught in any source found [13][18].
18. **The hanging everyone got wrong (pays Loop B).** 1983 chaplain's letter, "17 June 1947" repeated for decades — actually 30 April 1949, Sugamo, 00:33 [11][15]. Fujishige sentenced to death [2].
19. **Two plaques (pays Loop C).** The U.S. citation (25 miles, B Company, the Recon Platoon) [3] vs the Filipino markers: "The initial raid was led by … the ROTC Hunters" (2005) and the 1962 plaque crediting "some American airborne soldiers" [22]. Stilz and Vanderpool: both true [11][33].
20. **Afterward.** Skau killed over Okinawa in Aug 1945 [32]; Miles d. 1962 in Manila; Edwards farmed near Los Baños and hunted with old guerrilla friends; Zervoulakos in the U.S. Navy; Lois McCoy became a nurse; the call in 1998 [11]; Swing ran U.S. immigration [36]; 2005 marker, scholarships for Filipino students at the UP Rural High School, dinners every 23 Feb [16][21]. Close on the field where the guards exercised — today part of UP Los Baños [13][22].
