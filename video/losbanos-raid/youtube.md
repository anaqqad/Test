## Description
At a quarter to seven in the morning on 23 February 1945, the off-duty guards of a Japanese internment camp in the Philippines lined up for their daily exercises, in loincloths, with their rifles locked away. Fifteen minutes later, American paratroopers dropped beside the camp, Filipino guerrillas came through the wire, and 54 amphibious tractors crossed a lake to carry 2,147 starving men, women and children to freedom. Someone had told the Americans the exact minute. He had crawled out under the wire five nights earlier.

This is the true story of the raid at Los Baños, told from Army reports, a war crimes trial and the internees' own accounts: the camp's supply officer Sadaaki Konishi and the starvation ration, the escape of Pete Miles, Ben Edwards and Freddy Zervoulakos, the Hunters ROTC and the other guerrillas, the 11th Airborne's plan, the morning of the raid, the front pages it shared with Iwo Jima, the killings of Filipino civilians around Los Baños, and the hanging date the books got wrong for decades. Where the sources disagree, we tell you so.
