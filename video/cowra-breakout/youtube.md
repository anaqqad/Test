## Description
```
At about two in the morning of August 5, 1944, a bugle sounded in the Japanese compound of No. 12 Prisoner of War Camp at Cowra, New South Wales, and hundreds of Japanese prisoners went for the wire with baseball bats, mess knives, clubs and blankets. The man said to have blown it was Hajime Toyoshima: the Zero pilot shot down over Darwin in 1942, the first Japanese prisoner taken on Australian soil, who had lived at Cowra for nineteen months under a false name.

This is the documented story of the Cowra breakout, the largest prison break of the Second World War: the Field Service Code that told soldiers never to be taken alive, the Tiwi man who captured Toyoshima, the order to move the privates to Hay, the vote on scraps of paper, Privates Ben Hardy and Ralph Jones at their Vickers gun, the night on Broadway, the manhunt, May Weir's tea and scones, the court of inquiry, the George Cross six years late, and what became of the survivors, told from the official history, the inquiry's findings and the recorded words of guards and prisoners.
```
