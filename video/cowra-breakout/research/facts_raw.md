## PRIMARY / OFFICIAL SOURCES

### [1] Gavin Long, *The Final Campaigns* (Australia in the War of 1939-1945, Series 1 Army, Vol. VII), AWM, Canberra, 1963, Appendix 5 "The Prison Break at Cowra, August 1944", pp. 623-624
- AWM PDF (as cited by Wikipedia): https://www.awm.gov.au/images/collection/pdf/RCDIG1070308--1-.pdf (the AWM redirect was broken when checked; volume page https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/RCDIG1070206/)
- Text read from an identical 2-page scan of the appendix: https://revfrankhughesjr.org/images/Prison_break_at_Cowra_AUG_1944.PDF (page headers "APPENDIX 5" and "624 PRISON BREAK AT COWRA" match the official volume).

p. 623:
- "Despite the fact that Japanese troops had been schooled to die rather than surrender there were, by August 1944, 2,223 Japanese prisoners of war in Australia, including 544 merchant seamen. There were also 14,720 Italian prisoners, mostly from the Middle East, and 1,585 Germans, mostly naval or merchant seamen."
- Contrast with Australians in Japanese hands: "most of the 21,000 Australian prisoners in Japanese hands were being under-fed and over-worked and that many were dying of disease and malnutrition, the Japanese prisoners in Australian camps were well fed, were living in comfortable quarters, and were thriving."
- "At this time 1,104 Japanese prisoners were in No. 12 Prisoner of War Compound near Cowra, the centre of an agricultural district in the middle west of New South Wales."
- Layout: "This establishment was divided into four camps: "A" for Italians, "B" for Japanese, "C" for Koreans, and "D" for Indonesians. The whole compound formed an octagon about 800 yards across, and the four camps were separated by two intersecting roads and were fenced with thick barbed-wire entanglements about 8 feet high."
- Guards: "The 22nd Garrison Battalion guarded the prisoners, its commander, Lieut-Colonel Brown, holding also the appointment of commander of the "Cowra P.W. and Internment Group"." (fn 1: Col M. A. Brown, b. Candelo NSW 10 Jul 1889; CO 22 Grn Bn 1942-44.)
- "Six 30-foot observation towers overlooked the compound and at night lights from these towers swept the camps and fixed lights lit up the wire and the roads."
- "As the Japanese improved in health and strength as a result of their good rations, and of constant wrestling and baseball, their spirits rose."
- Quote from a garrison officer (fn 2 = E. V. Timms, "The Blood Bath at Cowra", in *As You Were*, 1946; Timms "a novelist ... was a major in the 22nd Garrison Battalion"): "They did not understand the Articles of the Geneva Convention ... and our strict adherence to its terms merely amused them and further convinced them of our moral and spiritual weakness. They read into our humane treatment of them a desire to placate them, and this they felt sure sprang from our secret fear of them."
- "Information was received at the Group headquarters early in August that the Japanese were discussing a mass outbreak. Additional precautions were taken, and also it was decided to move all the Japanese privates from Cowra to Hay (N.S.W.), where there was another big P.W. Group."

p. 624:
- "The Geneva Convention provides that prisoners must be given 24 hours' notice of such a move, and on 4th August notice was given to the Japanese camp leader that all Japanese at Cowra except officers and N.C.O's were to go to Hay on the 7th."
- "That night the guards were alert and tense. About 2 a.m. a Japanese ran to the camp gates and shouted what seemed to be a warning to the sentries. Then a Japanese bugle sounded. A sentry fired a warning shot."
- "More sentries fired as three mobs of prisoners, shouting "Banzai", began breaking through the wire, one mob on the northern side, one on the western and one on the southern. They flung themselves across the wire with the help of blankets. They were armed with knives, baseball bats, clubs studded with nails and hooks, wire stilettos and garotting cords."
- "The men not on guard, most of whom were sleeping fully clothed with rifles and 50 rounds beside them, raced out to reinforce the guard."
- Hardy and Jones: "The strongest group of Japanese--about 400, broke through the wire on the north-west. Here Privates Hardy and Jones punched their way through the prisoners, manned a Vickers gun and fired it until they were knifed and clubbed to death. The Japanese swung the gun round to fire on the Australians' huts but it jammed, and its Japanese crew was killed."
- "No Japanese succeeded in crossing the road that bisected the compound from east to west. Here the fire was so deadly that soon more than 200 Japanese were sheltering in a deep drain from which they emerged at dawn to surrender. Meanwhile Japanese who had remained in the camp had set fire to every building in it."
- "Some hundreds of prisoners had now broken away into the open country, where camp guards, troops from a training camp two or three miles away and two police constables were soon rounding them up. The Japanese offered no resistance in this phase. Some of them hanged themselves from trees before they were found by the searching troops."
- Police: "Constable A. P. McGovern of Mandurama and Constable C. H. R. Cooper of Woodstock ... Cooper arrested eight of them single-handed and three in company with McGovern."
- Casualties: "Including those who killed themselves, 234 Japanese died and 108 were wounded. Thirty-one killed themselves and 12 were burnt to death in huts set on fire by Japanese. Sixteen of the wounded showed signs of attempted suicide. The 22nd Garrison Battalion lost 3 killed and 3 wounded."
- fn 3: "Pte B. G. Hardy, GC, N103951; 22 Grn Bn. Motor mechanic; of Willoughby, NSW; b. Marrickville, NSW, 28 Aug 1898. Killed in action 5 Aug 1944."
- fn 4: "Pte R. Jones, GC, N244527; 22 Grn Bn. Labourer; of Crookwell, NSW; b. Gorleston-on-Sea, England, 26 Sep 1900. Killed in action 5 Aug 1944."
- Note: Long does NOT name the bugler, does not mention Toyoshima, the vote, or Lt Doncaster.

### [2] Commonwealth Parliamentary Debates, House of Representatives, 8 September 1944 (vol. 179), p. 650: John Curtin, statement "Mutiny of Japanese Prisoners of War"
- https://historichansard.net/hofreps/1944/19440908_reps_17_179/ (subdebate 32; page marker "page 650" precedes the statement)
- Curtin did not name Cowra: "in the darkness about 2 o'clock on the morning of 5th August, 1944, over 900 Japanese prisoners of war in a camp in Australia made an unprovoked mass attack upon their guards."
- "The Japanese had armed themselves with mess knives, baseball clubs and other improvised weapons. They first set their sleeping huts alight and then rushed the fences of the camp. The Japanese had prepared themselves with extra clothing, gloves and padding for surmounting or passing through the wire fences."
- "Large numbers who escaped through the outer fence of the camp attacked and killed an Australian machine-gun crew and attempted to storm the garrison quarters. These attacks were met by fire from the Australian guards, who showed excellent discipline and restraint throughout the incident."
- "The prisoners of war who escaped after the attacks had failed were subsequently recaptured - all but a small number by nightfall on the day of escape. During the search for escapees an Australian officer was brutally murdered by a party of Japanese."
- "Eighteen of the twenty sleeping huts and two administration huts in the camp were burned to the ground. In burned huts incinerated bodies of Japanese were found. Many other Japanese committed suicide or were killed by their comrades inside and outside the camp."
- "Total casualties sustained by the Japanese prisoners of war were - 1 officer killed. 230 other ranks killed or died of wounds or died by suicide, 1 officer wounded. 107 other ranks wounded."
- Military Court of Inquiry summary (as read by Curtin): conditions "fully in accordance with the provisions of the International Convention, that accommodation and rations for the Japanese were provided on the same scale as for Australian troops"; "no complaints as to treatment had been made by or on behalf of the Japanese prior to the mutiny, that the mutiny was carried out according to a premeditated and concerted plan formulated by the Japanese, and put into effect following a meeting secretly held in the camp at midnight"; "the attack by the Japanese was characterized by a suicidal disregard of life, that firing ceased as soon as control of the camp was assured".
- Breakdown of the dead: "of the 231 dead Japanese it is found that twenty died by hanging and strangulation inflicted by the Japanese on themselves or on one another, nine by suicide from stabbing, two by suicide under a train, five from a combination of self-inflicted wounds and gunshot wounds, and twelve from causes unknown but their bodies were found in huts burned by the Japanese. Sixteen of the wounded showed evidence of attempted suicide."
- "the responsibility for the incident rests entirely on the prisoners of war themselves, and that it was their intention to engage in suicidal combat with their guards."
- Reporting to Japan: "In accordance with recognized practice the Australian Government has furnished a report on the mutiny to the representative of the protecting power for transmission to the Japanese Government."
- Note: the Court of Inquiry's president/members are not named here; not found in any source checked (see Open questions).

### [5] AWM oral history S01027: Sgt Ashley Phillip Joseph Cooper (N226295), Cowra POW Group, interviewed by Daniel Connell, Brighton NSW, 12 Dec 1990 (Keith Murdoch Sound Archive)
- Page: https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/S01027 ; transcript PDF https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/awm-media/collection/S01027/document/1043742.PDF (30 pp.; PDF page = printed "Page N of 30")
- p. 13: "The Italians knew they had no way of getting back to their families until the war was over, and they were quite content to just sit back and - as the expression is - ride it out."
- p. 15 (weapons): guards issued .303 rifles; "shown how to operate a Bren gun, how to operate a Sten gun, and how to operate an Owen gun. But we had no practical experience on a rifle range whatsoever." "There was two four-wheeled trailers put on the perimeter of the Japanese compound, which contained a Vickers gun each. One of those Vickers guns was the machine-gun post where Hardy and Jones ... they were added extra". Watchtowers: "I think, they used to have a Hotchkiss gun in them."
- p. 19 (guards' complacency; also quoted on [22]): "Well, we'd never ever thought they'd even try it, it was such a ridiculous thought on our part, that they would even try it."
- p. 20 (also quoted on [22]): "You see, of that time, the Japanese soldier, his god was the Emperor."
- p. 21: woke to "this terrific noise going on, machine-guns going flat out, pistols going off, Very lights going up in the air. I thought, oh, the bastards have broke, and with that I dived into my trousers and jacket, over my pyjamas". "I could see the huts alight in the Japanese compound, it was like a big bonfire." Floodlights "short-circuited". "They utilised their own dead to walk over." A quarter guard (WWI veteran with asthma) phoned "'The bastards have broken, make way for me, open the gates'... they dropped the first Jap about six feet behind him."
- p. 22: "from two o'clock till about half-past five, until dawn, went just like the snap of a fingers." A comrade said "it was the fiercest and bloodiest fighting he'd ever seen". On the gun: "I think it was Hardy that took the bolt out of the gun and made the gun into such a position which it couldn't be fired."
- p. 23: in front of the gun post "the dead bodies were piled one on top of the other as they fell, in a semi-circle around the machine-gun, to a height of around about three to four feet high ... just like a pile of firewood with dead bodies." Hardy and Jones were "clubbed, or cut their throats and clubbed them to death".
- p. 26: "two more, that laid down on a railway line between Blayney and Cowra and they committed suicide ... around about seven o'clock in the morning." Doncaster "went over with a group of young eighteen-year-old call-ups, who'd only had a uniform on for a few days. All they had were bayonets". A soldier "with an Owen gun" wanted to kill ~20 captured Japanese; Cooper stopped him citing reprisals against Australian POWs. Cooper claims Tokyo Rose broadcast the breakout on the 8 a.m. news (uncorroborated; see Open questions).
- p. 28: "we were locked up for six weeks after the outbreak, there was no leave whatsoever".

### [6] AWM oral history S00932: Margaret and Bruce Weir, interviewed by Daniel Connell, Cowra NSW, 26 May 1990
- Page: https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C285042 ; transcript PDF https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/awm-media/collection/S00932/document/1044075.PDF (16 pp.)
- PDF p. 3: farm mechanic "Stan Bradtke - coming down the path with three Japanese prisoners dressed in their maroon garb. My mother was making scones".

- PDF p. 6: on the 40th anniversary (1984) one of the three returned: "He ran around, as my brother said, like a little dog off a chain." "I told him that we still had the jug that my mother served tea to him and the other two prisoners ... it was the very same jug."

### [7] AWM oral history S03331: Marekuni Takahara, Masayoshi Yamada and Teruo Murakami, interviewed by Terry Colhoun (interpreted by Dr Keiko Tamura), recorded 7 Aug 2004 (60th anniversary)
- Page: https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1074219 ; transcript PDF https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/awm-media/collection/S03331/document/9157900.PDF (13 pp.; transcript spells "Muakami")
- PDF p. 4, Takahara (captured after drifting at sea; handed to Australians on Bathurst Island): "I realised that we became prisoners of war and I thought I was in the most shameful state as a citizen of Japan and I wanted to look for opportunity to kill myself." "It would be better to be shot by Japanese Zeros than by anybody else."
- PDF p. 5, Yamada: "we were making gloves for baseball. We were given two pairs of leather boots and we took the leather apart".
- PDF p. 6, Yamada: "I went to Broadway." "I couldn't get out of the camp. As you know Broadway is quite wide and there were lots of shooting going on ... The only thing we could do was lie in the ditch." "I had a baseball bat with me. It was not to attack anything. My friend also had a baseball bat and we talked before the breakout that if one of us was shot and very close to die, the other would hit the head of the other and let the other die as quickly as possible." "When the vote was taken I said yes to the breakout". Punishment afterward: "just water and bread and that's what happened for 2 or 3 days. Then gradually the jam was added and butter was added."
- PDF p. 7, Murakami (arrived Cowra about May 1944, 5th Group): "I said I voted yes but to be honest, I didn't think it was a good idea I wanted to vote no." "I joined with the mind that I would not see the end of it, that I would soon die in the breakout." "I went towards Broadway, and went into the Broadway and then I laid on the ground in the side ditch until day broke."
- PDF p. 8, Takahara: "I already made up my false name in Darwin and I didn't tell the Australians that I was military personnel, I said I was steward of a fishing boat". "Many of us had false names and we just didn't talk about the past". "I always say I am a graduate from Cowra University."
- PDF p. 9, Takahara: "I was told the signal of the breakout would be, will come out at 2, and I belonged at the first group." His task: "reach the Officers Camp across the Broadway". "by the time I went into the Broadway there were already about 30 of them, or 30 dead bodies lying on the Broadway". A bullet grazed him and "hit somebody who was behind me ... hit his heart and he died instantly."
- PDF p. 10, Yamada: "the most shameful thing to be captured alive, that's been kind of drummed into us". "I think that is the feeling I had towards life, something I wanted to cling to life. But once the decision was made to breakout I felt, okay, I come along with you."
- PDF p. 10-11, Takahara: "People say that Japanese POW broke out because of this separation between the soldiers and Non-Commissioned Officers."
- PDF p. 11, Yamada (president of the Cowra Association in Japan): "everybody thought we would die, but something happened and we are still alive". Takahara: first commemoration was the 40th anniversary; he persuaded 12 Buddhist priests to attend (pp. 11-12).
- PDF p. 12, Murakami used his own name in camp.

### [8] Australian War Memorial, Encyclopedia, "Cowra breakout" (last updated 7 Dec 2020)
- https://www.awm.gov.au/encyclopedia/cowra
- "By August 1944 there were 2,223 Japanese prisoners of war in Australia, including 544 merchant seamen. Of these 1,104 were housed in Camp B of No. 12 Prisoner of War Compound near Cowra ... They were guarded by the 22nd Garrison Battalion."
- "On Friday 4 August, in response to information that the Japanese were discussing a mass outbreak, notice was given that all Japanese prisoners below the rank of Lance Corporal would be transferred to the Hay Prisoner of War Camp. About 2 am on Saturday 5 August 1944 a prisoner ran shouting to the camp gates. Soon afterwards an unauthorised bugle was heard and prisoners, armed with knives and improvised clubs, rushed from their huts and began breaking through the wire fences. Sentries opened fire but several hundred prisoners escaped into open country, while others who remained set fire to the camp buildings."
- "On the night of the breakout three Australian soldiers were killed and another three were wounded. Privates B.G. Hardy and R. Jones, who were overwhelmed while manning a machine gun post, were posthumously awarded the George Cross. In the following nine days 334 prisoners were retaken. In all, 234 Japanese were killed and 108 wounded."
- Bugle image AWM C115060. Cites Long pp. 623-24 and NAA FS 198.

### [10] Claire Hunter, "The bugle and the breakout", AWM blog, 30 Jul 2019 (updated 29 Jul 2024), quoting AWM curator Dr Kerry Neale
- https://www.awm.gov.au/articles/blog/the-bugle-and-the-breakout
- "Just before 2 am on Saturday, 5 August 1944, Japanese fighter pilot Hajime Toyoshima sounded the bugle that signalled the beginning of the Cowra breakout."
- Bugle "made by Boosey and Hawkes Ltd of London in the 1930s, but the actual origins of the bugle, and how it came to be in Toyoshima's possession, are a bit of a mystery." Camp commander Major Edward Timms recovered it, hung it in his Sydney sitting room; widow donated it after his death in 1978.
- Camp "Located three kilometres north-east of the town ... originally built in 1941 for Italian prisoners of war". "The first Japanese prisoners arrived at Cowra in January 1943".
- "Like many Japanese prisoners, Toyoshima had given a false name to avoid the shame of being captured from impacting on his family."
- Capture: Zero "damaged in the first air raid over Darwin on 19 February 1942 and he made a crash landing on Melville Island. It was there that he was approached and apprehended by a Tiwi man, Matthias Ulungura, from the Snake Bay settlement. Ulungura disarmed him before taking him to Bathurst Island". "There is an anecdotal story that Ulungura used a stick pointing into his prisoner's back to mimic a gun". Sgt Leslie Powell, 23 Field Company RAE, "was actually unarmed as well, so he used Toyoshima's own service pistol to escort him into captivity." At Cowra "he was known as Tadao Minami."
- Trigger: plans "relocating all Japanese prisoners below the rank of Lance Corporal to a camp at Hay". "In Japanese military culture at the time, NCOs and privates had a relationship often likened to older and younger brothers."
- Neale: "They knew they wouldn't actually succeed and that they would probably die during the night, but it was the attempt that was important. They were desperate to lose the stigma of being a prisoner and were willing to take whatever measures were necessary."
- Neale: "while some crawled under the wire most actually threw themselves on the wire for others to climb over."
- Neale: "Privates Ben Hardy and Ralph Jones dragged their coats on over their pyjamas and pushed their way through the rioting prisoners to a machine-gun ... They continued to fire their Vickers gun until the very end."
- "Hardy's final actions were to render the gun unusable ... He was clubbed to death, and Jones was fatally stabbed by one of the prisoners."
- "Private Charles Henry Shepherd was stabbed in the chest and killed by a prisoner as he emerged from the guardroom; and Lieutenant Harry Doncaster, 19th Australian Infantry Training Battalion, was killed trying to catch a group of escapees." VDC member Thomas Hancock "also died as a result of an accident during the search".
- "Among the dead was Toyoshima, the man who had blown the bugle ... He committed suicide in a ditch just outside the perimeter wire."
- "The Japanese dead were buried in a plot next to the Australian War Cemetery in Cowra - the only Japanese military cemetery in the world outside Japan." Survivors "were split up and moved to camps at Hay and at Murchison in Victoria".
- "Army Sergeant Major Akira Kanazawa was one of the ringleaders of the breakout. He had stressed that, while Australian Army soldiers were to be attacked, no prisoner was to harm or interfere with civilians in any way." "no civilians were harmed during the breakout."
- "16-year-old Bruce Weir and his father ... approached by three Japanese escapees ... Bruce's mother May gave the men tea and scones until the authorities arrived". "In a certain sense, reconciliation began with the actions of May Weir."
- "On the 20th anniversary of the breakout, in 1964, the Ambassador of Japan and the head of the Australian War Graves Commission opened the military cemetery in Cowra".

- https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C93930

## Disagreements

1. **How many broke out / took part**
   - Took part: "over 900" (Curtin [2]); "almost a thousand" (AWM AWAS blog [15]); "1,104 ... rushed the barbed-wire fences" (DVA [23]); 1,104 "in the breakout" (SMH [37]); "all but 138" of 1,104 (Carr-Gregg p. 182 via [38]); "Approximately 545 Japanese attempted escape" (AWM [9]); "most of the 1100" (Gordon, ADB [20]).
   - Got outside the wire: "some hundreds" (Long [1]); 378 (Migration NSW [26], AIDR [42]); 359 (Wikipedia [43]; SBS "358 other prisoners" [35]); 334 (SMH [37], Gordon ADB [21]: "334 prisoners who managed to escape"); "300 had escaped" (NLA [27]).
   - 334 is also given as the number *recaptured* (AWM [8], NSW [28]), incl. 25 who died outside (NLA [27], SMH [37], Smithsonian [38]).
   - Safest wording: about 1,100 in B compound, most took part, several hundred got outside, 334 brought back within nine days.
2. **Japanese dead**: 231 (Curtin/Court of Inquiry [2], NAA [4], Migration NSW [26], SBS [35], ADB [21], SMH [37]) vs 234 incl. later deaths (Long [1], AWM [8][9][10], DVA [22][23], NLA [27]). Smithsonian [38]: 231 at first, three more died of wounds = 234. JACAR [32]: 1 officer + 230 others.
3. **Japanese wounded**: 108 (Long, AWM, NAA) vs 1 officer + 107 other ranks (Curtin [2]; SMH "107 wounded"). Same total, counted differently.
4. **Suicides**: Long "Thirty-one killed themselves and 12 were burnt to death" [1]; Curtin: 20 hanged/strangled (by themselves or each other), 9 stabbing, 2 under a train, 5 self-inflicted plus gunshot, 12 unknown in burned huts [2]; NLA "around 20" burned in huts [27]; Smithsonian ~183 gunshot, 31 by violence [38]. Long's 31 = Curtin's 20+9+2.
5. **Australian dead**: four (Hardy, Jones, Shepherd, Doncaster: NAA [4], AWM [10]) vs five including VDC Sgt Hancock, accidentally shot 7 Aug, died nine days later (Places of Pride [18], Smithsonian [38]). Long counts only the 22nd Garrison Bn: "3 killed and 3 wounded" [1].
6. **Australian wounded**: 3 (Long [1], AWM [8], Conversation [39]) vs 4 (SMH [37]; JACAR [32]).
7. **Time of the bugle**: "about 2 a.m." (Long [1], AWM [8], REL [11]); "Just before 2 am" (AWM blog [10]); "1.30 a.m." (Migration NSW [26]; the 2024 commemoration was held at 1.30 am [28]); "1:45am on 4 August" (LANT [25], date wrong). Takahara was told the signal "will come out at 2" [7].
8. **End of firing**: Cooper "from two o'clock till about half-past five" [5]; "By 6:20 am, a ceasefire was called" (NLA [27]).
9. **Who was to be moved to Hay**: "all the Japanese privates" / "all Japanese at Cowra except officers and N.C.O's" (Long [1]); "all Japanese prisoners below the rank of Lance Corporal" (AWM [8][10]); "about 700" (SMH [37], SBS [35]); separation of NCOs from privates (ADB [20], DVA [23]). Move date 7 August (Long [1], Migration NSW [26]). Notice given on 4 Aug: "afternoon" (SMH [37]).
10. **The vote**: on toilet paper (nippon [34]) vs "a blank piece of paper" handed out by group leaders, circle = yes, cross = no, in "five groups of 20 to 30 people" (SBS/Apthorpe [35]; Murakami "I drew a circle"). Result: majority yes; "an estimated 80 percent" (Smithsonian [38], source unclear). Curtin: plan carried out after "a meeting secretly held in the camp at midnight" [2]. Who admitted wanting to vote no: Anzac Portal says Takahara [22], but the AWM transcript shows Murakami [7] p. 7; Yamada also voted yes [7] p. 6.
11. **Number of attack groups**: "three mobs" (north, west, south) (Long [1]); three objectives (NLA [27]); "four groups" (Smithsonian [38]); Takahara "first group" [7]; Cooper "about four basic directions" [5] p. 21.
12. **Compound layout**: Long: A Italians, B Japanese, C Koreans, D Indonesians [1]; Anzac Portal: A and C Italians, B Japanese NCOs and men, D Formosans, Koreans and Japanese officers [22]; Gordon/ADB: Italians two sections, B Japanese privates and NCOs, fourth Japanese officers plus Koreans and Formosans [20]. NSW register: Indonesians interned for the Dutch [29].
13. **Hardy and Jones's gun**: Hardy removed the bolt / disabled it (Cooper [5] p. 22, "I think"; AWM blog [10]; DVA "It is believed" [23]; SMH [37]) vs the Japanese swung it round and "it jammed" (Long [1]), "probably because they neglected to swing the ammunition belt with it" (Gordon ADB [20]). Roles: Hardy firing, Jones feeding (ADB [20]). Deaths: Hardy clubbed, Jones stabbed (AWM [10]); "knifed and clubbed" (Long [1]); "bashed to death" (Gazette [3]). "No. 2 Vickers gun" appears only on Wikipedia [43] (unsourced); Cooper says two Vickers on four-wheeled trailers [5] p. 15.
14. **Who blew the bugle**: Toyoshima per AWM [10][11], DVA [22][23], nippon/Imai [34], LANT [25], Migration NSW [26]. Long says only "a Japanese bugle sounded" [1]. No source checked names anyone else.
15. **Toyoshima's death**: suicide in a ditch just outside the wire (AWM [10][11]); took his life "the moment he got over the fence" (DVA [23]); cause recorded as "Gun shot wound to chest and self-inflicted wound" (cemetery database [24]); "likely ... took his own life after being shot at" (LANT [25]).
16. **Toyoshima's capture**: Ulungura used a stick to mimic a gun (anecdote, AWM blog [10]) vs "the back of his tomahawk" (Places of Pride [19]) vs grabbed his wrist and took his revolver (Ulungura's own words, LANT [25]). Date of capture 19 Feb 1942 (AWM, LANT) vs "1942-02-24" in the cemetery database [24] (probably the date of handover or registration). Spelling: Ulungura / Ullungura / Ulungarra.
17. **Toyoshima's cover story**: "a Sergeant Pilot flying from Ambon to Darwin" (AWM P00022.001 [12]) vs "crewman in a recently shot down Japanese Navy patrol bomber" (AWM REL/04058 [11]). Rank: Sergeant [12][24], Chief Petty Officer [12 related item], "third-class flight officer (final, posthumous rank)" [34].
18. **Toyoshima's age/origin**: DOB 20 Mar 1919, Kanagawa (cemetery database [24], probably Minami's false data); "1920-44" (nippon [34]); "a proud 22 year old from Kagawa Prefecture" (LANT [25]).
19. **When Toyoshima reached Cowra**: 9 Jan 1943 (cemetery database [24]); "January 1943" (Smithsonian [38]).
20. **Warning prisoner**: "shouted what seemed to be a warning" (Long [1]); "weeping and appeared to be in great fear" (Carr-Gregg p. 70 via [38]); "didn't want anything to do with the escape bid" (SMH [37]); later killed by his own side (Rolls family account, ABC [36]; Smithsonian [38]). Warning shot(s): "A sentry fired a warning shot" (Long) vs "two shots" (Rolls [36][37]).
21. **Doncaster's death**: "at dusk" (Places of Pride [17]) vs "killed later that day" (NSW register [29]) vs "in the days after the breakout" (DVA [23]). How: clubbed (Cooper [5]) vs knife attack after he picked up a rock (Places of Pride [17]); Curtin: "brutally murdered by a party of Japanese" [2].
22. **Earlier warning**: Korean prisoner report on 3 June 1944 (Migration NSW [26]); "Two months earlier ... Takeo Matsumoto" (SMH [37]); "6 weeks before" (Anzac Portal [22]); "Information was received ... early in August" (Long [1]).
23. **Recapture**: "all but a small number by nightfall on the day of escape" (Curtin [2]) vs nine days (AWM [8]) vs "several weeks before every Japanese prisoner of war was captured or accounted for" (AWAS blog [15]); "lasted 10 days" (AIDR [42]).
24. **Cemetery and garden dates**: Japanese decision 1960 (National Trust [30]); proposal and land "ceded to Japan" 1963 (NSW register [29]; "ceded" is doubtful, check); opened 1964 (AWM [10], Conversation [39], Anzac Portal [22], Migration NSW [26]). Garden: 1979 (National Trust [30], Conversation [39]); "1978-79" (Migration NSW [26]); Anzac Portal wrongly implies 1964 [22].
25. **"Only Japanese war cemetery"**: "the only Japanese military cemetery in the world outside Japan" (AWM blog [10]) is a strong claim; Wikipedia says only one in Australia. Use "the only Japanese war cemetery in Australia" unless verified.
26. **Jones's enlistment**: 15 Jan 1942 mobilised (ADB [21]) vs enlisted at Goulburn 24 Feb 1942 (AWM [14]). Hardy's age: born 28 Aug 1898, so 45 at death.
27. **Murakami's rank**: "former army private first class" (nippon [34]) vs "The former private" (ABC [36]). Age at death 103 (Council [31]), consistent with the ABC's "98, ... this month turns 99" in Aug 2019.

## Open questions

1. Court of Inquiry: president and members not found. Read AWM54 780/10/3B or 3C, or NAA A1608 AX20/1/1 (digitised, barcode 652125?) in a normal browser. JACAR [32] says over 60 witnesses, 7-15 Aug 1944.
2. Was there a documented order that escapees not be shot unless necessary? Not found. What was found: search parties of trainees were unarmed or had only bayonets (Places of Pride [17], Cooper [5] p. 26); the Japanese order not to harm civilians (AWM [10]). A farmer shot two escapees (SMH [37]); not otherwise verified.
3. The "Number 2 Vickers gun" label: only on Wikipedia (unsourced). Check Gordon or McLachlan before using it on screen.
4. The vote on toilet paper: nippon.com [34] (from survivor interviews) vs blank paper (SBS [35]). The exact tally is unknown; 80% is unsourced in [38].
