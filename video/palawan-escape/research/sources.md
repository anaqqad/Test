# Sources: the Palawan massacre and escape (14 December 1944)

## Why this topic
Competitor research on 17 Nov 2026 (YouTube search sorted by views and by upload date). Sealed Orders posted "The True Story of
the Palawan Escape — the 11 Americans Who Swam Away From a Massacre" the day before (34 min). Escape stories are the strongest
format in the niche this month (Breakfiles' escape titles at 160K–660K, "The American POW Who Stole a German Fighter" 279K in two
weeks, fern's "How to Escape Auschwitz" 3.1M). The owner asked for a much more interesting and hooking episode than usual, so the
cold open is the fake air raid alarm itself, told from the survivors' sworn statements, with open loops (150 men, 11 alive, a
five-mile swim, the 500 at Cabanatuan, the 1958 release).

Our version is built on primary documents that the competitor did not show: the U.S. Army War Crimes Branch report of
18 August 1945 and the survivors' sworn statements filed with the Tokyo tribunal (scans from Japan's National Diet Library), plus
the Signal Corps photo album of the camp in March 1945 (National Archives).

## Primary documents (read from the scans; files in `research/docs/<NDL id>/`)
1. **War Crimes Branch report, 18 Aug 1945** (GHQ USAFPAC, Office of the Theater Judge Advocate), IMTFE Pros. Doc. 2869
   "Massacre at Palawan", NDL 12922764, 5 pp. Read in full.
   - p. 1: 1 Aug 1942 arrival; McDole's appendectomy "without anaesthetic"; two prisoners' arms broken over papayas.
   - p. 2: "A rule was imposed providing for the execution of ten prisoners for each one that escaped." Raids by B-24s on
     19 and 28 Oct 1944; trenches "five feet deep, four feet wide, and of sufficient length to accommodate 50 men"; "only one
     entrance but due to American officers' continual suggestions, two entrances were permitted". "After each raid the Japs
     seemed to take the attitude that the prisoners were the cause of the bombings and treatment became worse." "on 14 December
     1944 there were 150 in prison." About 0200 two officers announced early work; called back at noon; two raids during lunch;
     early afternoon the men "were forced into their shelters ... the Japs saying that there were hundreds of American planes
     approaching". "between 50 and 60 Jap soldiers, armed with light machine-guns, rifles, and carrying buckets of gasoline".
   - p. 3: "approximately 40 of 'A' Company. They first threw a lighted torch into the entrance and followed it with a bucket or
     two of gasoline ... which exploded". Men "mowed down by machine guns and rifles". "30 or 40 others successfully tore
     barehanded through barbed wire fences and ran to the water's edge". Swimmers "shot in the water from the shore or from a
     barge moving just off shore". Those "in crevices were hunted down and dynamited". Bogue, Barta, Petry, Pacheco and Martyn
     swam "about 2100 hours". Bogue: "For five days and nights, without food or water except rain" until rescued by "Filipino
     prisoners at Iwahig Penal Colony". Nielsen: coconut husks "for three hours", "feigned death", a cove with 15 Americans
     "eight of whom were subsequently killed from a barge"; "struck in the leg, head, and ribs by bullets, but nevertheless kept
     on swimming", "washed seaward by a strong current, finally reached the other side of the five-mile bay at 3:00 p.m."
     Balchus and Deal; three Japanese "using torches".
   - p. 4: "Balchus hit him on the head with a big piece of coral"; "Deal ran over the cliff and Balchus ran down the beach,
     dived into the water and swam the bay". Filipinos dressed the men "in civilian clothing and took them to Brooke's Point";
     "picked up by the 'Catalina'" on 6 Jan 1945. Barta "became unconscious about half-way across", woke "floating out of the
     mouth of the bay but only about 50 yards from the shore", reached it "about sunrise"; ten days in the jungle. McDole stayed
     with Hamrick "until the latter died of gangrene on 18 December 1944", swam to Iwahig, "had to remain on a fish trap all
     night, from which he was rescued by Iwahig prisoners gathering their fish"; Brooke's Point 7 Jan, PBY 21 Jan 1945. "Martyn
     swam the bay but did not contact the others." After the landing of 28 Feb 1945, "identification tags, certain personal
     items, and fragmentary records" found; 15–23 March 1945 "seventy-nine individual skeletons were buried".
2. **Sworn statement of Sgt. Douglas W. Bogue, USMC**, 17 Feb 1945, IMTFE Pros. Doc. 8259, NDL 12924614 (statement on pp. 21–22
   of the scan). One of "350 men" sent from Cabanatuan Camp No. 3; arrived "the first of August". Heard troops moving "At about
   two a.m."; Japanese "in full field equipment"; "About noon all hands were called in"; "At about two p.m. I saw two P-38's";
   "The Japs were saying that hundreds of planes were coming"; "This was the first time that they ever forced us under cover so
   we thought it might be the real thing"; "sixty or so soldiers armed with light machine guns and rifles, and some were carrying
   buckets of gasoline and torches. They attacked the A company air raid shelter first"; "I told them our only chance was to get
   through the fence"; "I concealed myself in the rocks ... I lay there until nine p.m."; "While swimming the bay we could see
   Japs burning bodies in fires"; heard dynamite; four officers (Bruni, Mango, Turner, Knight) burned in their foxhole near A
   Company.
3. **Sworn statement of Fern Joseph Barta, RM1c, USN**, 1945, IMTFE Pros. Doc. 8260, NDL 12924615 (p. 6 of the scan): one of
   "350 prisoners of war" sent from Cabanatuan; "The Japs had spotted a convoy headed for Mindoro. At about 2 p.m. the Japs called
   an air raid, although I saw no planes either then or later. They said a lot of planes were coming." Escaped through "a drain
   which lead out of the camp underground"; "The outer end of this led out at the top of a forty or fifty foot cliff"; "About forty
   men got into the rocks"; tourniquet on Hamrick's arm; "There were 150 men in the camp at this time"; Martyn, "a strong swimmer",
   started "the five mile swim across the bay" with him. The scan shows the classification "changed from Secret to Restricted".
4. **Statement of Rufus W. Smith**, sworn 20 Sep 1946, IMTFE Pros. Doc. 8350, NDL 12924702 (p. 8 of the scan): guards said "we
   would be killed if the Americans came back"; "he even went so far as to say if they made a landing we would never live to see
   the American Army"; after the first bombing, three trenches with one entrance planned; "drums of gasoline were just over the
   fence a little way from them, which on the 14th day of December, 1944 they used on us."
5. **Sketch of POW Camp No. 10A**, "at Puerto Princesa, Palawan Island, on 14 December 1944, Sketch by Sgt. Bogue USMC", IMTFE
   Pros. Doc. 8487, NDL 12924810 (p. 3).
6. **US protest via the Swiss Legation**, 3 June 1945, "re massacre of 150 American POWs at Puerto Princesa", IMTFE Pros. Doc.
   8454, NDL 12924790 (title used).
7. **Signal Corps photo album 2985 "Philippine Islands – Palawan"**, National Archives 111-SCA (NAID 80684841): captions of the
   camp in March 1945 ("Front view of American prison building ... where 150 Yank war prisoners were burned alive in December by
   the Japs"), the cooking shed, the barracks, the graves after "a military funeral and full honors", MacArthur at the camp
   (8 June 1945) and at Iwahig Penal Colony, Palawan guerrillas, the landing of 28 Feb 1945 (41st Division, 186th RCT), the
   airdrome survey of June 1946. Also NAID 204949548 (USAF 58405AC), one of the shelters by the fence.

## Secondary sources
8. Stephen L. Moore, *As Good as Dead* (Caliber, 2016), pp. 136–137 (19 Oct 1944 B-24 waggling its wings over the camp), p. 150
   (10 Dec convoy sighting; Terada's 13 Dec message "if the POWs are harboring an enemy feeling, dispose of them at the
   appropriate time"), p. 151, pp. 244–257 (Brooke's Point, the 6 Jan and 21 Jan flights).
9. Michael E. Krivdo, "Catalyst for Action: The Palawan Massacre," *Veritas* (USASOC) 14/1 — escape through the dug-out end of
   shelter C; the reports classified; link to the Sixth Army's rescue raids.
10. V. Dennis Wrynn, "American Prisoners of War: Massacre at Palawan," *World War II* (Nov 1997, HistoryNet) — Smith's shark;
    the 1948 Yokohama trial (opened 2 Aug 1948; 16 tried, 6 acquitted; Terada life; Deguchi death commuted to 30 years on
    19 Jul 1950; all released from Sugamo on 31 Dec 1958); 159 men returned to Manila in Sept 1944.
11. Michael S. Bell, three articles, National WWII Museum (July 2022) — Nielsen's debriefing by 7 Jan 1945; Lapham's 26 Jan 1945
    warning about the 500 at Cabanatuan; 123 men buried in one grave at Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery (1952); Kojima and
    Sato never tried.
12. Wikipedia, "Palawan massacre" (Terada as 2nd Air Division commander; 16 of 33 charged put on trial) — leads only.
13. Universal Newsreel, 21 Feb 1945, "Luzon Yanks Freed From Jap Prison": 121 Rangers and 286 Filipino guerrillas, 513 prisoners
    released at Cabanatuan.
14. NHCP marker, Plaza Cuartel, Puerto Princesa: "some 150 American prisoners of war were burned to death".
15. Survivors' roster: Lorna Nielsen Murray, "Palawan Roster" (west-point.org), cross-checked with National Archives records.

## How the script uses the sources
- "Hundreds of American planes", "first time they ever forced us under cover", "the real thing": Bogue (2) and the report (1).
- The ten-for-one rule, the trenches, the torch and gasoline, the 30 or 40 men at the water's edge, Nielsen's swim, Balchus and
  the coral, Barta waking at the mouth of the bay, McDole on the fish trap, the 79 burials: the report (1).
- Gasoline drums over the fence, "never live to see the American Army": Smith (4).
- The convoy, Terada's message: Moore (8); Barta (3) names Mindoro.
- The shark: Smith's own later account (10), told as "he later said".
- Cabanatuan: Lapham's warning (11) and the newsreel (13); the script says "Historians link" and that the records do not spell
  out how much Palawan weighed, because no source quotes Krueger or Mucci naming Palawan.
- The trial and 1958 release: Wrynn (10), Bell (11).

## Disagreements
- **Dead**: 139 (most sources; 150 minus 11), 138 (1948 charge), "some 150" (marker). The script says "out of one hundred and
  fifty men, eleven lived".
- **Arrival**: 1 Aug 1942 (report, Bogue, Barta) vs 12 Aug (Wikipedia). Script: August 1942. Numbers: 346 vs "350" (Bogue,
  Barta). Script: "about three hundred and fifty".
- **Bay width**: "five-mile bay" (report, Barta) vs "about four miles" (Moore). Script: five miles.
- **Soldiers in the attack**: "50 and 60" (report), "sixty or so" (Bogue). Script: "about sixty".
- **Planes**: Bogue saw two P-38s; Barta saw none. The script gives both.
- **Cabanatuan freed**: 513 (newsreel, Moore), 516, 522. Script: 513.
- **Trial**: 16 tried (Wrynn, Krivdo, Bell) vs 14 (Camp). Script: 16.
- **The 6 Jan aircraft**: Navy (Bell) vs Army (Moore). Script: "a Catalina flying boat".
