## 1. The camp
- "On 1 August 1942, Barta, Bogue, and McDole arrived at Puerto Princesa" (1, p. 1). The same date is in 5 and in 2 (p. 151).
- 346 POWs came from Cabanatuan and Bilibid to build an airfield (5, 6). The camp was in old Philippine Constabulary barracks (5, 6, 9).
- Over the whole period "nearly six hundred men" passed through the camp (2, p. 151). Moore's talk says "more than six hundred" (3).
- The work: felling jungle by hand, crushing coral and pouring concrete (6, 7). The cleared area was about 2,400 x 225 yards and the strip 1,530 x 75 yards (5). They also built revetments for about 150 planes (9).
- Conditions: wormy rice and camote-vine soup; men who could not work had rations cut by 30% (5). Medicines were removed from the Red Cross parcels of January 1944 (5, 6). McDole's appendix was taken out without anaesthetic (1, p. 1; 5). Two men had their arms broken by the cook Nishitani over papayas (1, p. 1). The camp imposed a rule of "execution of ten prisoners for each one that escaped" (1, p. 2).
- Escapes: 9 or 11 Aug 1942 (six men), 29 Aug 1942 (Little, Watkins), Feb 1943 (Pryor was later beheaded), 28 Jun 1943 (White, Wilson; recaptured and shot) (1, pp. 1-2).
- In September 1944, 159 POWs were sent back to Manila, leaving 150 (5, 6). Doc. 2869: "on 14 December 1944 there were 150 in prison" (1, p. 2).
- Japanese personnel:
  - Capt. Nagayoshi Kojima, "the Weasel", 131st Airfield Battalion, "camp commandant" (5; 2, p. 151).
  - 1st Lt. Yoshikazu Sato, "the Buzzard" (5, 4). Doc. 2869 calls him "Captain Sato, commander of the Jap garrison" (FLAG on rank).
  - Lt. Sho Yoshiwara and Lt. Ryoji Ozawa (supply) (5).
  - MSgt. Taichi Deguchi, Kempeitai (5, 2).
  - MSgt. Toru Ogawa, gun barge (5; 2, p. 151).
- The name "Camp 10-A" is used by 4, 6 and 9 and by the Signal Corps caption in 4. The name's origin was not found.
- Filipino helpers: the "Palawan Special Battalion" had 1,058 enlisted men by late 1944 under Maj. Pablo P. Muyco (6). Capt. Nazario Mayor's company was based at Brooke's Point (2, p. 244; 3). Trinidad "Triny" Mendoza, code name "Red Hankie", was the widow of the executed Capt. Higinio Mendoza (3; 2, p. 136; the chapter title in 2).

## 2. The days before
- 19 Oct 1944: Cdr. Justin A. Miller (VPB-101, from Morotai) flew his B-24 *Miller's High Life* over the camp at about 300 ft and waggled his wings. He then hit the seaplane dock and about 40 planes on the strip, and was shot down about 10 miles out (2, pp. 136-137).
  - Wrynn has "a single B-24... sank two enemy ships" (5). Bell has three ships (6).
- 28 Oct 1944: "more Liberators destroyed 60 enemy aircraft on the ground" (5, 6). Doc. 2869 has "raids by 17 B-24's on 19 and 28 October 1944" (1, p. 2).
- After these raids the POWs were ordered to dig three trenches "five feet deep, four feet wide", each holding 50 men, with "two entrances... one at each end" after American officers' requests (1, p. 2). The roof was coconut logs, fronds and earth (4).
- POWs painted "American Prisoner of War Camp" on the barracks roof (5). MacArthur's warning to Terauchi was dropped as leaflets on 25 Nov 1944 (5, 6).
- The convoy: on 10 Dec a lookout on Surigao saw a US convoy of "more than three hundred ships" (2, p. 150). Terada expected a "Probable landing on Palawan Island" (2, p. 150). The convoy was actually bound for Mindoro (5).
- The order: Terada's 13 Dec dispatch: "if the POWs are harboring an enemy feeling, dispose of them at the appropriate time" (2, p. 150; 9).
  - That evening Kojima briefed Ozawa, Yoshiwara, Chino, Yamamoto, Abe and Ogawa. Ogawa was to patrol the bay with a gun barge (2, p. 151). Each soldier was given 30 rounds (5, 6).
  - FLAG: the "kill-all" order of August 1944 (the Formosa camp journal) is not mentioned in any Palawan source read. Moore's talk mentions only a general 1944 decree (3). Do not use it without a further source.
- 13 Dec: the Japanese cook asked Balchus whether he could swim the bay, "a distance of about four miles" (2, p. 151).

## 3. 14 December 1944
- About 0200: two officers announced early work (1, p. 2). At the strip Smith told McDole: "Something is going on, Dole" (4, quoting 12, p. 111).
- About 1100 to noon: work stopped. Sato, on a box: "Americans, your working days are over!" The men were trucked back to camp (4; 2, p. 154). Doc. 2869 says they "were called back at noon" (1, p. 2).
- Just after noon the church bell rang the first alarm. Two P-38s were seen (2, p. 154; 5). A second alarm came 35 minutes later; only one Japanese seaplane was seen (2, p. 155). Sato then forbade the men to go back to the barracks (2, p. 155).
- Nielsen counted "some sixty armed guards" (2, p. 155).
- About 1400, third alarm: "hundreds of American planes" were said to be coming (1, p. 2; 2, p. 155). Bogue: "This was the first time that they ever forced us under cover" (4, quoting the Bogue statement, NARA RG 125).
- "Between 50 and 60 Jap soldiers, armed with light machine-guns, rifles, and carrying buckets of gasoline" attacked the first shelter, which held "approximately 40 of 'A' Company". They "threw a lighted torch... and followed it with a bucket or two of gasoline" (1, pp. 2-3).
- Navy signalman C. C. Smith was killed with Sato's saber (1, p. 3; 6). The doctor, Lt. Carl Mango, was machine-gunned (5, 6).
- In Shelter C, Marine Rufus Smith shouted: "They're murdering the men in 'A' Company pit!" (4, quoting Escape & Evasion Rpt No. 23). The men in C dug through a pre-loosened end of the trench (4).
- Sketch in 4: Shelter A about 50 men, B 40, C 42. Camp gives A 50, B 35, C 25-30 (7). FLAG.
- Barta: "Three soldiers, one Marine, and I crawled out of the shelter and under the fence" (4, quoting the Barta statement of 15 Feb 1945).
- "30 or 40 others... tore barehanded through barbed wire fences and ran to the water's edge" (1, p. 3). Krivdo says more than two dozen slid down the 50-60 ft cliff (4). Moore's talk says about 30 (3).
- On the beach, men were hunted with rifles, bayonets, grenades and dynamite, and swimmers were shot "from a barge moving just off shore" (1, p. 3).
- That night Kojima's officers, the Kempeitai and the Obayashi Tai "held a celebration" (1, p. 4).
- Dead: 139 is the usual figure (4, 6, 8, 9). The Japanese claimed 149 (6).
- The four officers killed were Lt. Cdr. Henry C. Knight, Capt. Fred Bruni, 1st Lt. Carl L. Mango (MD) and WO Glenn C. Turner (8; 5 spells "Brunie").

4. Tommy T. "Pops" Daniels: Army Air Corps (8)
5. Elmo V. "Mo" Deal: Army, A Btry 59th CA (8)
6. Ernest J. Koblos: Army, C Btry 59th CA (8)
7. Glenn W. "Mac" McDole: USMC, M Co 1/4th Marines (8). He is a Pfc in 5 and 6 and a Cpl in 4.
8. Eugene Nielsen: Army Pfc, B Btry 59th CA (6, 8)
9. Alberto D. "Beto" Pacheco: Army, Sgt., Hq Btry 200th CA (AA), New Mexico (8; 4; 3)
10. Edwin A. Petry: Army Air Corps, S/Sgt., 7th Materiel Sqn, 19th Bomb Gp (8; 4)
11. Rufus W. "Smitty" Smith: USMC, L Co 3/4th Marines, Texas (8). He is a Cpl in 4 and 5 and a Sgt in 4 and 6.

## 4. Escapes
- **Nielsen**: hid under coconut husks for three hours, "feigned death", and found 15 Americans in a cove, "eight of whom were subsequently killed from a barge". He dove in and was "struck in the leg, head, and ribs by bullets", then was "washed seaward by a strong current". He reached "the other side of the five-mile bay at 3:00 p.m." (1, p. 3). Wrynn says about 20 Japanese fired at him (5). A Filipino with a bolo led him to the guerrillas: "I think I am the only one alive from the Palawan prison camp" (6).
- **Balchus and Deal**: Deal was wounded in the shoulder, so they gave up on swimming. Balchus "hit [a guard] on the head with a big piece of coral". Then "Deal ran over the cliff and Balchus... swam the bay" (1, pp. 3-4). Deal was bayoneted many times: "about twenty" (search snippet) to "thirty" (3), so FLAG the count. Mendoza helped him (3). He was evacuated about 20 Feb 1945 (3; FLAG, talk only).
- **Bogue, Barta, Petry, Pacheco, Martyn**: Bogue gathered them and "about 2100 hours they swam the bay" (1, p. 3). Bogue then spent "five days and nights, without food or water except rain" until Filipino convicts at Iwahig Penal Colony found him (1, pp. 3-4). "By the Grace of God I was overlooked" (6). Bogue's fight with Japanese sailors at a Lewis gun comes only from 3 (FLAG).
- **Barta**: "became unconscious about half-way across" and reached shore about sunrise; then "10 days" in the jungle (1, p. 4). He fell from a tree and ruptured his scrotum, and McDole later removed 52 maggots from the wound (2, pp. 246-247). Wrynn says he was hidden in a well at Iwahig (5).
- **Pacheco and Petry**: hid in a cave, were attacked by crabs, and swam when the high tide forced them out (6).
- **Martyn** (Pfc Donald Martyn, USMC) swam the bay but was never seen again (1, p. 4; 5).
- **McDole**: hid in the garbage dump (5, 6; 3 says Nielsen was with him). He saw an American tortured and burned (1, p. 4). He stayed with Hamrick until Hamrick "died of gangrene on 18 December 1944". McDole then swam to Iwahig, spent a night "on a fish trap" and was rescued by Iwahig convicts (1, p. 4). Asked if he was a POW, he said "I was, but no longer" (6).
- **Smith**: "coming up a ladder into Hell"; badly bitten by a shark on the left arm and shoulder (5; also 6 and 3). This rests on Smith's own later account; Doc. 2869 does not mention it, so attribute it to him. The phrase "shark-infested" is Moore's jacket copy, not evidence.
- **Daniels**: shot several times and left behind (1, p. 4). He stayed with the guerrillas "more than forty-seven days" until after the 28 Feb landing (3; FLAG, talk only).
- Bay width: "five-mile bay" (1, p. 3; 5; 6) vs "about four miles" (2, p. 151).

### Way out
- Six men (Smith, Nielsen, Koblos, Petry, Pacheco, Balchus) reached Mayor's camp at Brooke's Point on the afternoon of 22 Dec (2, p. 244). Brooke's Point is about 25 miles south (6).
- On 6 Jan 1945 Capt. Clarence L. Solander's PBY "Playmate 42" left Morotai at 0505, landed after 1250, took off at 1335 and reached Morotai at 2005 (2, pp. 248-249). The 73-year-old Thomas Loudon, Mayor's father-in-law, flew with them (2; 6).
- They were debriefed by G-2. Nielsen's debriefing was on 7 Jan (6; 9, citing Sides p. 12).
- McDole, Bogue and Barta reached Brooke's Point on 7 Jan (2, p. 252; 1, p. 4). On 21 Jan Lt. Kenneth F. Brissette (VPB-54) flew them out, via Mindanao, to Leyte and the hospital ship *Tangier* (2, pp. 252-253; 1, p. 4).
- The reports were classified "Top Secret" and the survivors were told to keep silent (4, 6).

## 5. Aftermath
- 28 Feb 1945: Eighth Army landing at Puerto Princesa, Operation Victor III (4). The landing force was the 186th RCT of the 41st Infantry Division (6; 9). Losses: US 12 killed and 56 wounded; Japanese about 900 killed (4, citing Lofgren CMH). After the landing, investigators found "identification tags, certain personal items, and fragmentary records" (1, p. 4).
- 15-23 Mar 1945: the 601st QM Co (GR) buried "seventy-nine individual skeletons". Twenty-six had been piled four and five high (1, pp. 4-5). Planks near the openings were charred, consistent with "a gasoline fire" (1, p. 5). Bell gives 123 recovered (6). Investigators also found men who had died trying to dig their way out (1, p. 5); do not show this.
- 3 June 1945: the US protest over the "massacre of 150 American POWs" went to Tokyo via the Swiss Legation (IMTFE Pros. Doc. 8454, Commons title).
- 1952: the remains of 123 men were reburied in a mass grave in Section 85 of Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery, St. Louis (4, 6). The burial date of 14 Feb 1952 comes from Find a Grave-type pages only (FLAG).
- Trial at Yokohama, opened 2 Aug 1948 (5). Sixteen were tried and six acquitted (4, 5, 6). The charge was the murder of 138 Americans (11).
  - Terada: life (5, 6).
  - Deguchi: death, commuted to 30 years by MacArthur on 19 Jul 1950 (5).
  - Ogawa: 2 years (5).
  - Sawa: 5 years; he admitted killing at least three (5).
  - Kojima, Yoshiwara and Sato were never tried; all three are presumed dead (6, 9).
  - All prisoners were freed from Sugamo on 31 Dec 1958 (5). Moore: "the end results could not have been more lenient" (4, quoting 2, pp. 276-278).

## 6. Link to Cabanatuan
- Moore claims "The first six men out of the Palawan camp had helped save more than thirty-six hundred other POW lives" (2, p. 251). He also says the six "triggered" the Cabanatuan rescue (2, p. 256).
- Bell: on 26 Jan Maj. Robert Lapham "warned that over 500 POWs at Cabanatuan were in danger of being executed, similar to what had happened at Palawan" (6). It is unclear whether "similar to Palawan" was Lapham's own wording or Bell's.
- Krivdo calls the massacre a catalyst (4). Wikipedia says Sixth Army decided on rescues because of the survivors' accounts, citing 4 (9).
- No source I read quotes Krueger or Mucci naming Palawan. In the raid article, Krueger ordered the raid after White's report of Lapham's information, citing Breuer 1994 (9).
- Verdict: the link is plausible and widely asserted, but it is an inference by historians, not a documented order. Script wording: "historians link"; avoid "because of".
- Sides' *Ghost Soldiers* (2001) opens with Palawan, pp. 7-17 (9). It was not read.

## 7. Disagreements
- Dead: 139 (most sources), 138 (1948 charge, 11), 149 (Japanese claim, 6), 159 (7, likely an error), "some 150" burned (10), and 141 names on the roster (8: 133 firm plus 8 doubtful).
- Arrival: 1 Aug 1942 (1, 2, 5) vs 12 Aug 1942 (9, 7). Starting number: 346 (5, 6) vs about 300 (9).
- Sato: Captain (1) vs 1st Lt. (2, 5, 6).
- Shelter sizes: see section 3.
- Ranks: Nielsen is called a "Marine CPL" in 4, but he was Army (6, 8). See also the ranks in the survivor list.
- PBY of 6 Jan: Navy (6) vs "18th Army Air Force" (2, p. 248; possibly an OCR error). Moore dates the McDole telegram "January 19, 1944" (2, p. 252); it should be 1945.
- Cabanatuan raid freed 513 (2) vs 516 (4) vs 522, including 492 Americans (6). Total rescued in the raids: 3,600+ (2) vs "nearly seven thousand" (6) vs over 7,000 (4).
- Trial: 14 guards (7) vs 16 (4, 5, 6); 33 charged (9). Sentence range: 2 years to death (5) vs 5 to 30 years (4).
- 1945 press: Wikipedia cites *Canberra Times*, 5 Mar 1945, p. 1, and an AP story by John A. Reichmann of 4 Sep 1945 (9). Neither was read (Trove blocked); this is a gap.

## 8. Today
- Plaza Cuartel, Puerto Princesa, has an NHCP marker (2016, English plaque later). It says "some 150 American prisoners of war were burned to death by Japanese soldiers", that a few survivors swam to Iwahig, and that the 1952 burial was at Jefferson Barracks (10). Beside it is a memorial by former Palawan POW Don Schloat, installed 26 Sep 2009 (blog sources; FLAG).
