## Description
At two in the afternoon on December 14, 1944, an air raid alarm sounded over a prison camp at Puerto Princesa, on the Philippine island of Palawan. The guards said hundreds of American planes were coming and, for the first time ever, forced all 150 American prisoners into their shelters. Then about sixty soldiers walked toward the trenches, some of them carrying buckets of gasoline. Eleven men lived.

This is the documented story of the Palawan massacre and escape, told from the men's own sworn statements and the U.S. Army's 1945 war crimes report: the airfield the prisoners built by hand, the warnings they were given, the drain under the fence, the cliff, the five-mile swim across the bay at night, the convicts and Filipino guerrillas who saved them, the flying boats that took them out, the rescue at Cabanatuan a few weeks later, and the trial that ended with every convicted man free by 1958.
